Q1. In Massachusetts, when following a motorcycle, you should use a following distance of at least:

Massachusetts recommends a four-second following distance behind a motorcycle, more than the three-second rule used for other motor vehicles.

Q2. In Massachusetts, when passing a vulnerable road user such as a pedestrian, bicyclist, or person using a wheelchair, you must leave a passing distance of at least:

Massachusetts law requires drivers to leave a safe passing distance of at least four feet between their vehicle and a vulnerable road user.

Q3. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q4. In Massachusetts, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q5. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q6. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q7. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q8. In Massachusetts, it is illegal to follow closer than what distance behind an emergency vehicle that is responding to an alarm with its lights and siren on:

Massachusetts law makes it illegal to follow closer than 300 feet behind a responding emergency vehicle.

Q9. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q10. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.
