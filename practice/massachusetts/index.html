Q1. In Massachusetts, it is illegal to follow closer than what distance behind an emergency vehicle that is responding to an alarm with its lights and siren on:

Massachusetts law makes it illegal to follow closer than 300 feet behind a responding emergency vehicle.

Q2. A left turn on red from a one-way street onto another one-way street is:

Most states permit this turn after a complete stop and yielding, but it is banned in some cities, so obey any posted sign.

Q3. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

States teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q4. In Massachusetts, when following a motorcycle, you should use a following distance of at least:

Massachusetts recommends a four-second following distance behind a motorcycle, more than the three-second rule used for other motor vehicles.

Q5. You are involved in a crash. You are required to:

Stopping, identifying yourself and rendering aid are legal duties everywhere. Leaving the scene turns a minor crash into a criminal charge.

Q6. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q7. In Massachusetts, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q8. In Massachusetts, when passing a vulnerable road user such as a pedestrian, bicyclist, or person using a wheelchair, you must leave a passing distance of at least:

Massachusetts law requires drivers to leave a safe passing distance of at least four feet between their vehicle and a vulnerable road user.

Q9. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q10. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.
