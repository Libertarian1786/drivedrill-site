Q1. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q2. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q3. In Rhode Island, a supervising driver for a Limited Learner Permit holder must be:

Rhode Island requires a supervising driver to have held a license for at least five years and to be at least 21 years old.

Q4. In Rhode Island, you may not park within how many feet of a crosswalk at an intersection?

Rhode Island's parking rules set a 20-foot no-parking zone next to a crosswalk at an intersection.

Q5. A wide white line across your lane at an intersection is:

Stop at the line, not past it. If your view is blocked once stopped, edge forward slowly only after stopping at the line.

Q6. Another driver is passing you. You should:

Speeding up while being passed strands the other driver in the oncoming lane. Hold your line and let them complete the pass.

Q7. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole manoeuvre. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q8. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q9. In Rhode Island, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practise across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a licence in a weekend.

Q10. In Rhode Island, once issued, a driver's license is generally valid for up to:

Rhode Island driver's licenses are generally valid for a maximum of five years before renewal is required.
