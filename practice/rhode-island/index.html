Q1. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q2. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q3. In Rhode Island, it is illegal to drive on a tire with tread worn to less than:

Rhode Island's vehicle equipment rules set 2/32 of an inch as the minimum legal tire tread depth.

Q4. In Rhode Island, you may not park within how many feet of a crosswalk at an intersection?

Rhode Island's parking rules set a 20-foot no-parking zone next to a crosswalk at an intersection.

Q5. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q6. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q7. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q8. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q9. In Rhode Island, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q10. In Rhode Island, a driver under the age of 18 who wants to use a cell phone while driving:

Rhode Island law bars drivers under 18 from using a personal wireless communication device at all while driving, including hands-free or speakerphone use, except in an emergency.
