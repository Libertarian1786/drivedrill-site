Q1. In Louisiana, to pass the OMV knowledge test for a driver's license, you must correctly answer:

The Louisiana driver's guide states you must correctly answer 80 percent of the knowledge test questions to receive a passing score.

Q2. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q3. In Louisiana, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q4. In Louisiana, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practise across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a licence in a weekend.

Q5. The most common cause of freeway merging crashes is:

Match the speed of traffic before you merge; joining at 40 mph into 65 mph traffic is what causes the problem.

Q6. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q7. The safest place to be around a large truck is:

If you cannot see the driver's face in the mirror, they cannot see you. Trucks have large blind spots on both sides, in front and behind.

Q8. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q9. In Louisiana, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q10. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole manoeuvre. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.
