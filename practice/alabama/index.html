Q1. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q2. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q3. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q4. The pedestrian signal shows a flashing orange hand with a countdown while you wait to turn. It means:

The countdown is for people already crossing. Wait until the crosswalk is clear before completing your turn.

Q5. Under Alabama's child restraint law, an infant must ride in a rear-facing seat until:

Alabama's child restraint law sets a specific age-or-weight threshold for when an infant may move out of a rear-facing seat.

Q6. In Alabama, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q7. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q8. A green arrow pointing left means:

A green arrow is a protected turn. Still check the crosswalk for people before you move.

Q9. In Alabama, if you cannot safely move out of the lane next to a stopped emergency vehicle, you must slow to:

Changing lanes is the first choice. The speed rule is what applies when traffic or the road makes a lane change unsafe.

Q10. In Alabama, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.
