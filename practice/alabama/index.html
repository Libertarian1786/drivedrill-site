Q1. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

The license consequence is administrative and starts before any court date in most states, and the record follows you for years.

Q2. A yellow sign showing a red octagon means:

STOP AHEAD gives you distance to slow down for a stop sign you cannot see yet, often around a curve or over a crest.

Q3. In Alabama, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q4. In Alabama, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q5. A yellow sign showing a curve with the number 35 under it means:

Yellow advisory speeds are recommendations for ideal conditions. In rain, ice or fog a safe speed is lower, and you can still be cited for driving too fast for conditions.

Q6. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q7. Under Alabama's child restraint law, an infant must ride in a rear-facing seat until:

Alabama's child restraint law sets a specific age-or-weight threshold for when an infant may move out of a rear-facing seat.

Q8. Two lanes merge into one because of road work. The most efficient approach is to:

The zipper merge keeps queues shorter and speeds more even. Take turns at the merge point rather than fighting over it.

Q9. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q10. In Alabama, if you cannot safely move out of the lane next to a stopped emergency vehicle, you must slow to:

Changing lanes is the first choice. The speed rule is what applies when traffic or the road makes a lane change unsafe.
