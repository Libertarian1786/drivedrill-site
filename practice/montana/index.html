Q1. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q2. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q3. Overdriving your headlights means:

Low beams show about 350 feet ahead. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q4. In Montana, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q5. In Montana, you may not park within what distance of a railroad crossing?

Montana prohibits parking within 50 feet of a railroad crossing.

Q6. In Montana, when the posted speed limit is under 55 mph, a driver in a funeral procession may not drive more than how far below that posted speed limit?

Montana limits funeral procession drivers to no more than 5 mph below the posted speed limit on roads posted under 55 mph.

Q7. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q8. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q9. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q10. In Montana, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so roughly one drink can reach it. The licence consequence is administrative and starts before any court date.
