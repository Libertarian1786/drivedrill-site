Q1. During the late-night hours when a Montana teen with a first-year restricted license may not drive alone, which trip is still allowed?

Montana's night rule has exceptions for emergencies, farm work, and travel to and from school, church or work. Social trips are not on the list.

Q2. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q3. You leave a brightly lit gas station and pull onto a dark road. What should you do?

Your eyes need time to adjust after bright light. Drive slowly until they do, so you can see the road and anything on it.

Q4. In Montana, the recommended driving speed inside a roundabout is:

Montana's roundabout guidance tells drivers to slow down, since driving speeds in roundabouts are 15 mph or less.

Q5. You stop behind a large truck that is stopped on an uphill grade. Why should you leave extra space?

A large truck can drift back as it starts up from a stop on a hill. Stop well back so you have room if it rolls toward you.

Q6. You may park in a space marked with the International Symbol of Access:

Accessible spaces need a valid placard or plate, and the person it belongs to must be involved in the trip. Fines are steep and vehicles are towed.

Q7. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q8. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q9. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q10. In Montana, if you move, you must notify the Driver Services Bureau of your new address within:

Montana requires drivers to notify the Driver Services Bureau of a change of address within 10 days.

Q11. At night, a vehicle with only one headlight is coming toward you. What should you do?

A single light could be a car with a burned-out headlight, so you cannot tell how wide it is. Drive as far to the right as you safely can until it passes.

Q12. Under current Montana law, minimum liability insurance must cover damage to other people's property in one accident up to at least:

Montana law (MCA 61-6-103) requires at least $20,000 of coverage for damage to other people's property in one accident. The April 2024 manual still prints the older $10,000 figure.

Q13. In Montana, you may not park within how many feet of a fire hydrant?

Montana prohibits parking within 15 feet of a fire hydrant.

Q14. Compared with ice at a much colder temperature, ice at about the freezing point is:

Ice near the freezing point is more slippery than much colder ice, because a thin layer of water sits on top of it. Take extra care when the temperature hovers around freezing.

Q15. In Montana, who is responsible for making sure every person in the vehicle is buckled up or in a child safety seat?

Montana law places the responsibility on the driver to make sure each occupant is properly buckled or in a child safety seat.

Q16. In Montana, a first conviction for driving without the required liability insurance adds how many points against your driving record?

Montana assesses 5 points against the driving record for a first conviction of driving without required liability insurance.

Q17. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

Q18. A signal at the end of a freeway on-ramp lets cars onto the freeway one at a time, switching between red and green. What must you do?

A ramp meter controls how quickly cars join the freeway. Stop at the line while it is red and go when it turns green.

Q19. In Montana, what does the adult who signs a minor's consent form for a permit or license agree to?

Signing the consent form makes the parent, guardian or responsible adult financially responsible for any damage that results from the minor's driving.

Q20. Montana's Four Second Sight Distance Rule tells you that you are driving too fast for how far you can see when:

Pick an object at the edge of what you can see and count four seconds. If you reach it before you finish, you are going too fast to stop for something you could see ahead, so slow down.

Q21. Where should a child in a rear-facing car seat never ride?

An inflating front airbag can strike a rear-facing car seat and seriously injure or kill the child. A rear-facing seat belongs in the back seat.

Q22. In Montana, when you are directly behind another vehicle and about to overtake it, you must dim your high beams within:

Montana's headlight rules require dimming high beams to low beams when overtaking another vehicle from behind within 500 feet.

Q23. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q24. What are the striped areas next to disabled parking spaces for?

The striped access aisle beside a disabled parking space gives room for wheelchairs, ramps and lifts. Parking there is not allowed, even with a disability placard.

Q25. You are about to turn at a corner, but you realize you are not in the proper lane for the turn. What should you do?

Do not turn from the wrong lane or cut across other traffic at the last moment. Drive on to the next intersection, or around the block, and make the turn from the proper lane.
