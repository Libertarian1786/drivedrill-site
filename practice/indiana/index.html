Q1. You need to move across several lanes of traffic on a highway. How should you do it?

Change lanes one at a time: signal, check your mirrors and blind spot, move into the next lane, then repeat. Cutting across several lanes at once surprises other drivers.

Q2. In Indiana, in which crash should you not move your vehicle off the road?

Do not move the vehicle if the crash involves hazardous materials or results in injury, death or someone being trapped.

Q3. How does moving floodwater affect SUVs and pickup trucks?

Moving floodwater about two feet deep can carry away most vehicles, including SUVs and pickup trucks. Do not assume a heavy vehicle is safe in it.

Q4. If you double your speed, about how much longer is your braking distance?

Braking distance grows much faster than speed. Doubling your speed makes the braking distance, and the force of a crash, about four times greater.

Q5. At what times are deer and other animals most likely to be on or near the road?

Animals such as deer are most active around dawn and dusk. Slow down and watch the sides of the road at those times, especially where animal crossing signs are posted.

Q6. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q7. An Indiana teen has completed a BMV-licensed driver education program. What is the youngest age for a probationary license?

With BMV-licensed driver education completed, you may get a probationary license at 16, after meeting the other requirements.

Q8. When passing a bicyclist in Indiana, what is the minimum room you should leave?

Pass a bicyclist only when there is a safe amount of room, at least 3 feet, and no danger from oncoming traffic.

Q9. You are on an on-ramp joining a highway, and a yellow ADDED LANE sign is posted. What does it mean for you?

An ADDED LANE sign means two roads come together and a lane is added, so the entering lane keeps going and you are not required to merge. Still watch for traffic changing lanes.

Q10. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. The law often also requires them in rain, fog or snow, or whenever the wipers are on.

Q11. What do you need to drive an autocycle in Indiana?

An autocycle can be driven with a valid Indiana driver's license. A motorcycle permit or endorsement is not required.

Q12. The diagonal stripes on a road-work barricade slope down toward the right. Which way should you go?

Barricade stripes slope down toward the side traffic should pass on. Stripes that slope down to the right mean bear to the right.

Q13. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q14. An Indiana driver fails a chemical test for intoxication. The driving privileges are suspended for:

A motorist who fails a chemical test faces a 180-day suspension of driving privileges.

Q15. On the Indiana driving skills exam, what happens if you cross a solid yellow line?

Passing in a no-passing zone or otherwise crossing a solid yellow line is one of the errors that automatically fails the skills exam.

Q16. A bicyclist is riding at the right edge of your lane while an oncoming truck approaches. What is usually the best way to handle both?

When two hazards meet you at the same time, adjust your speed so you deal with one danger at a time. For example, slow down and let the truck pass before you pass the bicyclist.

Q17. Your Indiana license has been expired for 1 year. What must you do to renew it?

A license expired at least 180 days but not more than 5 years requires an administrative penalty, a knowledge exam and a vision screening.

Q18. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q19. A deer suddenly steps into the road ahead of you. What is usually the safest choice?

Swerving to miss an animal can send you into oncoming traffic or off the road, which is often worse than hitting it. Brake firmly, stay in control and keep your lane.

Q20. You have a minor crash in an Indiana travel lane. No one is hurt and no hazardous materials are involved. What must you do with your vehicle?

If a crash happens on the traveled part of a highway, the driver must move the vehicle off the road to a spot as close to the crash as possible.

Q21. Unless posted otherwise, the speed limit in most Indiana urban residential areas is:

In most urban residential areas, vehicles may not exceed 30 mph or the posted limit.

Q22. On a road with no sidewalk, where should people walk?

Where there is no sidewalk, pedestrians should walk on the left side or shoulder, facing traffic, so they can see what is coming. Drivers should expect people walking there, especially at night.

Q23. In Indiana, a conviction for driving 16 to 25 mph over the speed limit adds how many points?

Indiana assigns 2 points for 1 to 15 mph over, 4 points for 16 to 25 mph over and 6 points for 26 mph or more over the limit.

Q24. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q25. You turn left from a two-way street onto a street with two lanes going your way. Which lane should you turn into?

Finish a left turn in the left lane closest to the middle of the street, going your way. After the turn, you may signal and change lanes when it is safe.
