Q1. A motorcycle is entitled to:

Never share a lane with a motorcycle. Riders move within the lane to avoid road hazards and to stay visible.

Q2. In Indiana, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q3. An unsecured load in the back of a pickup truck is:

You are responsible for anything that falls off your vehicle. Tie loads down and cover loose material.

Q4. In Indiana, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q5. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q6. In Indiana, once issued, a learner's permit remains valid for:

Indiana learner's permits are valid for two years from the date they are issued.

Q7. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q8. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q9. Using cruise control on a wet road is discouraged because:

If a tire starts to hydroplane, cruise control may hold or add throttle. Drive with your own foot when the road is wet.

Q10. In Indiana, a driver's license issued to someone younger than 75 years old is valid for:

Indiana driver's licenses are valid for six years for holders younger than 75; the renewal period shortens for older drivers.
