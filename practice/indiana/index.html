Q1. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q2. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q3. You may park in a space marked with the International Symbol of Access:

Accessible spaces need a valid placard or plate, and the person it belongs to must be involved in the trip. Fines are steep and vehicles are towed.

Q4. In Indiana, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q5. In Indiana, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q6. In Indiana, during the first 180 days of a probationary license, which passengers may still ride along without a supervising adult in the front seat?

The general rule bars passengers during the first 180 days of a probationary license, but Indiana carves out an exception letting the driver carry a child, stepchild, sibling, step- or half-sibling, or spouse without another accompanying adult.

Q7. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q8. In Indiana's manual, because passenger air bags can injure small occupants, it recommends seating children younger than 12 years of age:

Because air bags can injure children and small adults, the manual cites the National Safety Council recommendation to seat children younger than 12 in the back seat.

Q9. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q10. In Indiana, the state minimum liability insurance standard is commonly referred to as:

Indiana's minimum liability coverage is $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, plus $25,000 for property damage, commonly called 25/50/25 liability insurance.

Q11. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q12. A funeral procession with its lights on is passing through an intersection. You should:

Do not cut into or interrupt a procession, even if you have a green light, unless directed by an officer.

Q13. Your vehicle registration must be:

Expired registration is an easy stop for an officer and, in many states, grounds for towing.

Q14. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q15. An unsecured load in the back of a pickup truck is:

You are responsible for anything that falls off your vehicle. Tie loads down and cover loose material.

Q16. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q17. In Indiana's manual, wearing a seat belt is said to increase a front-seat occupant's chance of surviving a crash by about:

The manual states that seat belts increase the chance of survival by 45 percent and cut the risk of serious injury by 50 percent.

Q18. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q19. Outside of an interstate, a rural divided state highway in Indiana caps vehicles at:

Indiana caps speed on a rural state divided highway at 60 mph or the posted limit, lower than the 70 mph allowed on rural interstates.

Q20. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q21. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q22. In Indiana, once issued, a learner's permit remains valid for:

Indiana learner's permits are valid for two years from the date they are issued.

Q23. In Indiana, a driver's license issued to someone younger than 75 years old is valid for:

Indiana driver's licenses are valid for six years for holders younger than 75; the renewal period shortens for older drivers.

Q24. Unless a sign prohibits it, a right turn on red is allowed:

Right on red requires a full stop first, then yielding to everyone already entitled to go. A NO TURN ON RED sign removes the option entirely.

Q25. In Indiana, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.
