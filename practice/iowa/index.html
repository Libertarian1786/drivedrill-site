Q1. In Iowa, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q2. In Iowa, unless otherwise posted, the speed limit on an unsurfaced (gravel or dirt) secondary road from sunset until sunrise is:

Iowa's default limit on unsurfaced secondary roads is 50 mph from sunset until sunrise.

Q3. A pedestrian hybrid beacon over a mid-block crosswalk shows two steady red lights. What must you do?

A pedestrian hybrid beacon is dark until someone pushes the crossing button. Steady red means stop and stay stopped while pedestrians cross; only when it changes to flashing red may you go once your path is clear.

Q4. Good drivers look:

Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q5. In Iowa, you may not park closer to a fire hydrant than:

Iowa's no-parking list includes parking closer than 5 feet from a fire hydrant.

Q6. A person hurt in a crash is bleeding badly. What should you do until help arrives?

Stop serious bleeding by pressing thick cloth pads firmly on the wound, and keep up the pressure until help arrives.

Q7. In Iowa, operating while intoxicated (OWI) also includes driving with:

Iowa's OWI law covers driving with any amount of a controlled substance in your system, not just alcohol over the limit.

Q8. When towing a trailer you should:

Extra weight lengthens every distance. Speed limits are sometimes lower for vehicles that are towing.

Q9. Which driver mistake is a common cause of crashes with bicyclists?

A common driver-caused bicycle crash happens when a driver turns left in front of a bicyclist coming the other way. Look for bicycles before you turn and give them the right-of-way.

Q10. The Iowa manual says moving water can sweep away a vehicle, even an SUV or pickup, when it is as little as:

Iowa's manual warns that as little as 6 inches of water may sweep away a vehicle, including SUVs and pickups. Turn around.

Q11. In Iowa, when may you pass a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing without stopping first?

On a road of four or more lanes, only traffic behind the bus must stop. On a narrower road, traffic in both directions stops -- a physical median does not by itself excuse you. Overtaking a bus from behind always requires a stop.

Q12. At night, a vehicle with only one headlight is coming toward you. What should you do?

A single light could be a car with a burned-out headlight, so you cannot tell how wide it is. Drive as far to the right as you safely can until it passes.

Q13. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams reach much farther than low beams, but at highway speed you can still outrun them, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q14. A police officer pulls you over at night. What should you do after you stop?

At night, turn on the inside light so the officer can see into your car. Stay inside with your hands in view unless you are told to get out.

Q15. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle traveling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q16. What can you do to reduce the glare of oncoming headlights on your windshield at night?

A clean windshield, inside and out, helps cut the glare from oncoming headlights. A dirty windshield increases glare and hides the road, signs and other drivers.

Q17. In Iowa, what does a tornado warning mean?

A tornado watch means a tornado is possible. A warning means one is imminent, and everyone should seek shelter right away.

Q18. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q19. You are stranded in a snowstorm. What helps rescuers find your vehicle?

Turn on your hazard lights and tie a bright flag or cloth to the antenna or door handle so rescuers can spot the vehicle. Stay inside and out of traffic.

Q20. In Iowa, how must you pass a bicyclist riding in your lane?

Iowa's manual says you must completely change lanes to pass a bicyclist, then return only when you can see the bicyclist in your rearview mirror.

Q21. You need to move across several lanes of traffic on a highway. How should you do it?

Change lanes one at a time: signal, check your mirrors and blind spot, move into the next lane, then repeat. Cutting across several lanes at once surprises other drivers.

Q22. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q23. In Iowa, your rearview mirror must let you see behind you for at least:

Iowa's manual says you must be able to see at least 200 feet behind you in your rearview mirror.

Q24. In Iowa, which of these drivers must stop at a railroad crossing even when no train is in sight, unless an EXEMPT sign is posted?

Iowa lists drivers carrying passengers for hire, such as Uber or Lyft, among the drivers who must stop before the tracks unless an EXEMPT sign is posted.

Q25. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.
