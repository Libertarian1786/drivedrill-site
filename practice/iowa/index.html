Q1. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q2. In Iowa, a driver under 21 found operating while intoxicated with a BAC of at least .02 but under .08 will have driving privileges revoked for a minimum of:

An underage Iowa driver counts as operating while intoxicated at a BAC of .02 or more; the minimum revocation is 60 days, rising to a minimum of 180 days if the BAC is .08 or higher.

Q3. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q4. When following a motorcycle you should:

Motorcycles are easy to lose behind your own windshield pillar, and even slight contact can spill a rider. Give a motorcycle a full lane and more space than you would a car.

Q5. In Iowa, the Steer It-Clear It law requires that after a crash with no injuries, you:

Iowa's Steer It-Clear It law requires drivers involved in a crash with no injuries to move an operable vehicle out of the driving lanes if it can be done safely, reducing the risk of a secondary crash.

Q6. In Iowa, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q7. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

The license consequence is administrative and starts before any court date in most states, and the record follows you for years.

Q8. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q9. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q10. In Iowa's manual, staying awake for 18 hours straight is said to impair you as much as a blood alcohol concentration of:

Iowa's manual states that being awake for 18 hours straight impairs a driver about as much as a BAC of 0.08 percent, which is legally drunk.
