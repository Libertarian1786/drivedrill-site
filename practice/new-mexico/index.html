Q1. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q2. Your lane has a painted arrow that shows both a curved arrow and a straight arrow. What may you do from this lane?

A lane marked with both a curved and a straight arrow is a shared lane: you may turn in the direction the curved arrow points or continue straight.

Q3. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q4. You stop for a red light at a corner with a crosswalk. Where should your car be?

Stop before the crosswalk, not in it or hanging over it. A car in the crosswalk forces pedestrians, including people who are blind or use wheelchairs, to walk around it into traffic.

Q5. In New Mexico, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offense. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.

Q6. The New Mexico manual says alcohol reaches your brain within about:

Alcohol goes from your stomach into your blood and reaches your brain in 20 to 40 minutes.

Q7. You leave a brightly lit gas station and pull onto a dark road. What should you do?

Your eyes need time to adjust after bright light. Drive slowly until they do, so you can see the road and anything on it.

Q8. A very heavy downpour makes it too hard to see the road even with your wipers on. What should you do?

Get off the road to a safe spot and wait until the downpour eases, because you cannot drive safely when you cannot see. Pressing on blind puts you and other drivers at risk.

Q9. Where should a child in a rear-facing car seat never ride?

An inflating front airbag can strike a rear-facing car seat and seriously injure or kill the child. A rear-facing seat belongs in the back seat.

Q10. In New Mexico, when passing a bicyclist, a motorist should try to keep a clearance of at least:

New Mexico's manual tells motorists to keep a safe distance when passing a bicyclist, trying for at least five feet.

Q11. Standard drinks of beer, wine and distilled spirits:

A 12 oz beer, a 5 oz glass of wine and 1.5 oz of spirits each contain roughly the same alcohol, which is why counting drinks does not make you safe to drive.

Q12. In New Mexico, before a school bus crosses railroad tracks, its driver must:

New Mexico requires a school bus driver to open the bus doors before crossing railroad tracks, so the driver can listen for a train.

Q13. In New Mexico, to get an unrestricted license, a driver under 18 must have held a provisional license for the previous:

You must have held a provisional license for the 12 months right before you apply for the unrestricted license, with 30 days added for each traffic conviction (NMSA 66-5-9).

Q14. The driver ahead of you is signaling a left turn. What is true about passing that driver?

Never pass on the left of a driver who is signaling or making a left turn. If there is room and it is legal, you may pass on the right; otherwise slow down and wait.

Q15. In New Mexico, a driver convicted of a first DWI will also be ordered to:

Besides fines, court costs and possible jail, a first DWI conviction brings an order to get treatment and to install an ignition interlock device on your vehicle.

Q16. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q17. In New Mexico, a child under 24 months old, or weighing less than 60 pounds, must ride:

New Mexico requires children under 24 months old, or weighing less than 60 pounds, to be secured in an approved safety device.

Q18. In New Mexico, you must yield to an emergency vehicle using a siren, an air horn, or a flashing light that is:

You must yield to a police vehicle, fire engine, ambulance or other emergency vehicle using a siren, air horn, or red or blue flashing light. Pull to the right and follow any loudspeaker instructions.

Q19. Your car has lane-keeping and automatic braking features. What should you remember in heavy rain or fog?

Driver-assist features such as lane keeping and automatic braking may not work properly in rain, fog and other poor conditions. You are still responsible for watching the road and controlling the car.

Q20. You are turning left on a green arrow, and the arrow changes to a steady yellow arrow. What does that mean?

A steady yellow arrow means the protected turning time is ending and the signal is about to change. Stop if you can do so safely; if you are already in the intersection, finish the turn with care.

Q21. The New Mexico manual says to watch for antelope crossing the road especially:

Watch for elk in the western three-quarters of the state, antelope in the east or on open plains, and black bears and cougars in the mountains.

Q22. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q23. A motorcyclist has been hurt in a crash. What should you do about the rider's helmet?

Do not remove an injured rider's helmet. If the neck or spine is hurt, moving the head can cause more damage, so wait for trained help.

Q24. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q25. In New Mexico, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.
