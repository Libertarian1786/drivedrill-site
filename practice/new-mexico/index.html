Q1. Parking is prohibited:

Never park in a crosswalk, on a sidewalk, in an intersection, or where you would block a curb ramp.

Q2. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q3. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q4. A standard drink of beer, wine and distilled spirits:

A 12 oz beer, a 5 oz glass of wine and 1.5 oz of spirits each contain roughly the same alcohol, which is why counting drinks does not make you safe to drive.

Q5. In New Mexico, drivers may not park within how many feet of a fire hydrant?

New Mexico prohibits parking within 15 feet of a fire hydrant.

Q6. In New Mexico, a person supervising an instructional permit holder's practice driving must be:

New Mexico requires that a permit holder be supervised by another driver who is at least 21 years of age and licensed to drive that type of vehicle.

Q7. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q8. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q9. In New Mexico, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q10. New Mexico's no-parking rules keep a stopped vehicle how far back from a railroad crossing?

New Mexico prohibits parking within 50 feet of a railroad crossing.
