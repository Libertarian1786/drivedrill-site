Q1. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q2. In Minnesota, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q3. Speeding fines in a work zone are usually:

Most states increase work-zone fines and add penalties when workers are present, whether or not the crew is visible at that moment.

Q4. In Minnesota, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q5. A red WRONG WAY sign tells you that:

WRONG WAY usually appears after DO NOT ENTER on a ramp. Stop, pull over if you can do so safely, and back out or turn around rather than continuing.

Q6. Two parallel white lines across the road at an intersection mark:

That is a marked crosswalk. Stop before it, never in it, and yield to people crossing.

Q7. In Minnesota, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q8. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q9. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q10. In Minnesota, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it. The licence consequence is administrative and starts before any court date.
