Q1. In Maryland, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q2. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

States teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q3. In Maryland, when your high beams are on and a vehicle is coming toward you, you must switch to low beams at least:

Maryland requires drivers to change to low beams at least 500 feet before reaching an oncoming vehicle.

Q4. On a multi-lane highway, slower traffic should use:

Keep right except to pass. Blocking the left lane forces other drivers to pass on the right, which is where crashes happen.

Q5. In Maryland, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q6. In Maryland, you may not pass another vehicle if your view is obstructed within:

Maryland prohibits passing when your view is obstructed as you come within 100 feet of a bridge, elevated road, or tunnel.

Q7. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q8. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q9. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q10. A sign reading BRIDGE ICES BEFORE ROAD warns you that:

Bridges lose heat from above and below, so they freeze first. Slow down and avoid braking or steering suddenly on the deck.
