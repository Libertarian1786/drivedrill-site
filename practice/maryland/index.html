Q1. When you follow buses or delivery vans that stop often, the Maryland manual says to increase your following distance to:

Behind vehicles that stop often, such as buses and delivery vans, increase your following distance to 4 or 5 seconds, or more if needed.

Q2. In Maryland, a basic violation of the move-over law, with no crash, brings:

Violating Maryland's move-over law is a misdemeanor. With no crash, the penalty is a $110 fine and 1 point.

Q3. How should you wear the shoulder part of a lap and shoulder belt?

Wear the lap belt low across your hips and the shoulder belt across your chest. Never put the shoulder belt under your arm or behind your back; in a crash it cannot hold you there and can cause injuries.

Q4. Why should you never run your car's engine inside a closed garage?

Exhaust contains carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot see or smell that can kill. Never start or run your engine in a garage with the door closed.

Q5. A police officer pulls you over and walks up to your car. What should you do with your hands?

Stay in the car and keep both hands where the officer can see them, such as on top of the steering wheel. Do not reach for your license or anything else until the officer asks.

Q6. In Maryland, an applicant who is 25 years old or older must hold a Learner's Instructional Permit for at least:

Maryland applicants age 25 or older must hold their Learner's Instructional Permit for at least 45 days before they are eligible for a Provisional License, a shorter wait than younger applicants have.

Q7. A Maryland provisional driver under 18 is convicted of a third moving violation. How long is the license suspended?

A third offense brings a 180-day suspension, followed by 180 days of driving for work and school only.

Q8. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q9. In Maryland, when passing a bicyclist, you must leave a clearance of at least:

Maryland law requires drivers to leave at least 3 feet of space when passing a bicyclist.

Q10. In Maryland, a move-over violation that causes a crash, with no death or serious injury, brings:

A move-over violation that causes a crash brings a $150 fine and 3 points.

Q11. On an ice- or snow-covered road, how does driving downhill change the risk?

Going downhill on a slippery road makes a skid more likely and makes it harder to slow the vehicle. Slow down before you start down the hill and keep your speed low all the way down.

Q12. Your vehicle has four-wheel drive. How should you drive on an icy road?

Four-wheel drive does not make ice safe, because the vehicle can still skid and slide, so drive slowly and carefully. It can give a false sense of security.

Q13. What usually causes a locked-wheel skid?

A locked-wheel skid usually comes from braking too hard at too high a speed, so the wheels stop turning and slide. Ease off the brake to let the wheels roll again.

Q14. In Maryland, mopeds and scooters may be ridden on any road with a posted speed limit of:

Mopeds and scooters may be ridden on any road posted at 50 mph or less, on the road or on the shoulder.

Q15. You hit a parked car and cannot find its owner. What must you do?

If you damage an unattended vehicle, you must find the owner or leave a written note where it will be seen, with your name and how to reach you. Some states also require a police report.

Q16. According to the Maryland manual, what do flashing lights in a work zone mean?

Flashing lights in a work zone mean workers are present. Slow down, obey the signs and watch for workers.

Q17. Fog becomes so thick that you cannot see well enough to drive safely. What is the safest choice?

When fog is too thick to see the road, pull well off the road and wait for it to lift. Stopping or creeping in a travel lane risks being hit by drivers who cannot see you.

Q18. You hit and injure a horse with your car on a Maryland road. What must you do?

If you hit and injure a domestic animal, such as a dog, cat, horse or cow, you must immediately call the police.

Q19. What is the most common cause of skids?

Most skids happen because drivers go too fast for the road and weather conditions. Slowing down before you reach slippery spots prevents most of them.

Q20. You stop for a red light at a corner with a crosswalk. Where should your car be?

Stop before the crosswalk, not in it or hanging over it. A car in the crosswalk forces pedestrians, including people who are blind or use wheelchairs, to walk around it into traffic.

Q21. A bicyclist is riding at the right edge of your lane while an oncoming truck approaches. What is usually the best way to handle both?

When two hazards meet you at the same time, adjust your speed so you deal with one danger at a time. For example, slow down and let the truck pass before you pass the bicyclist.

Q22. A snowplow is working on the road ahead of you. What is the best choice?

Stay well back from a working snowplow, because it throws sand and snow and may stop or back up. Never pass it on the shoulder side.

Q23. A bicyclist is riding in a bike lane next to your lane in Maryland. When you pass, does the 3-foot passing rule apply?

Leave at least 3 feet when passing a bicycle in a bike lane, on the shoulder or in the same lane as your car.

Q24. At a railroad crossing, the gate goes up, but the red lights are still flashing. What should you do?

Do not cross the tracks until the red lights stop flashing, even if the gate rises. Another train may be coming.

Q25. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.
