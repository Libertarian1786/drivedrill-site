Q1. The back of your car starts to slide to the right. You should:

Steer into the skid: point the front wheels where you want the car to go, ease off the pedals, and make small corrections.

Q2. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q3. In Maryland, you may not pass another vehicle if your view is obstructed within:

Maryland prohibits passing when your view is obstructed as you come within 100 feet of a bridge, elevated road, or tunnel.

Q4. In Maryland, when your high beams are on and a vehicle is coming toward you, you must switch to low beams at least:

Maryland requires drivers to change to low beams at least 500 feet before reaching an oncoming vehicle.

Q5. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q6. Adjusting your side mirrors slightly outward compared with the usual setting:

Wider mirror angles shrink the blind spot, but a head check before changing lanes is still the move that catches what mirrors miss.

Q7. In Maryland, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q8. In Maryland, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q9. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.

Q10. You should slow down before entering a curve because:

Slow in, steady through, accelerate gently out. Braking mid-corner shifts weight and can break traction.
