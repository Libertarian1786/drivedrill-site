Q1. A green arrow pointing left means:

A green arrow is a protected turn. Still check the crosswalk for people before you move.

Q2. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q3. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q4. In Arkansas, a driver under 18 years of age who wants to use a cell phone while driving:

Arkansas bans cell phone use, including hands-free devices, for drivers under 18; the law restricts cell phone use to emergencies only for that age group.

Q5. In Arkansas, an instruction permit issued after passing the knowledge exam is valid for:

Arkansas issues the instruction permit for two years from the date the knowledge exam is passed.

Q6. In Arkansas, a driver convicted of illegally passing a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing faces a fine of at least:

Arkansas treats passing a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing as a misdemeanor carrying a minimum $250 fine, up to $1,000, and up to 90 days in jail.

Q7. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q8. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q9. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q10. In Arkansas, when following a motorcycle you should allow extra following distance of about:

Because a motorcyclist can be hurt by even slight contact, Arkansas's guide recommends four to six seconds of following distance so the rider has time to maneuver or stop.
