Q1. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q2. In Arkansas, a doubled fine for a moving traffic violation committed in a work zone can be as high as:

Work zone fines in Arkansas are doubled for moving violations such as speeding, reckless driving or distracted driving, and can reach as high as $5,000.

Q3. In Arkansas, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q4. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q5. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q6. In Arkansas, when following a motorcycle you should allow extra following distance of about:

Because a motorcyclist can be hurt by even slight contact, Arkansas's guide recommends four to six seconds of following distance so the rider has time to maneuver or stop.

Q7. In Arkansas, an instruction permit issued after passing the knowledge exam is valid for:

Arkansas issues the instruction permit for two years from the date the knowledge exam is passed.

Q8. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q9. Unless a sign prohibits it, a right turn on red is allowed:

Right on red requires a full stop first, then yielding to everyone already entitled to go. A NO TURN ON RED sign removes the option entirely.

Q10. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.
