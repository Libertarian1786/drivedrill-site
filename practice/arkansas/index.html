Q1. In Arkansas, when following a motorcycle you should allow extra following distance of about:

Because a motorcyclist can be hurt by even slight contact, Arkansas's guide recommends four to six seconds of following distance so the rider has time to maneuver or stop.

Q2. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q3. In Arkansas, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q4. In Arkansas, an instruction permit issued after passing the knowledge exam is valid for:

Arkansas issues the instruction permit for two years from the date the knowledge exam is passed.

Q5. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q6. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q7. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q8. In Arkansas, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q9. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q10. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q11. In Arkansas, when may you pass a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing without stopping first?

The median has to be a genuine 20 feet or more. A painted stripe, a narrow curb or a turn lane does not qualify, and overtaking a bus from behind always requires a stop.

Q12. A funeral procession with its lights on is passing through an intersection. You should:

Do not cut into or interrupt a procession, even if you have a green light, unless directed by an officer.

Q13. In Arkansas, a driver convicted of illegally passing a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing faces a fine of at least:

Arkansas treats passing a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing as a misdemeanor carrying a minimum $250 fine, up to $1,000, and up to 90 days in jail.

Q14. In Arkansas, a driver under 18 years of age who wants to use a cell phone while driving:

Arkansas bans cell phone use, including hands-free devices, for drivers under 18; the law restricts cell phone use to emergencies only for that age group.

Q15. In Arkansas, a doubled fine for a moving traffic violation committed in a work zone can be as high as:

Work zone fines in Arkansas are doubled for moving violations such as speeding, reckless driving or distracted driving, and can reach as high as $5,000.

Q16. Speeding fines in a work zone are usually:

Most states increase work-zone fines and add penalties when workers are present, whether or not the crew is visible at that moment.

Q17. In Arkansas, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q18. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q19. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q20. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q21. In Arkansas, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q22. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q23. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q24. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q25. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.
