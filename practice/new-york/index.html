Q1. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q2. A deer suddenly steps into the road ahead of you. What is usually the safest choice?

Swerving to miss an animal can send you into oncoming traffic or off the road, which is often worse than hitting it. Brake firmly, stay in control and keep your lane.

Q3. How often must a vehicle driven or parked on New York public roads be inspected?

No one may drive or park a vehicle on public highways unless it has been inspected at least once a year.

Q4. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q5. You stop behind another vehicle in traffic. How much room should you leave?

When you stop behind a vehicle, leave enough room to see its rear tires on the pavement. That gives you space to go around it if it stalls.

Q6. In New York, a 6-year-old who has outgrown a car seat should ride with:

Children ages 4 to 7 must use an appropriate child restraint, such as a booster seat with a lap and shoulder belt. A seat belt by itself does not count as a child restraint.

Q7. A motorcyclist has been hurt in a crash. What should you do about the rider's helmet?

Do not remove an injured rider's helmet. If the neck or spine is hurt, moving the head can cause more damage, so wait for trained help.

Q8. In New York, how many convictions for passing a stopped school bus within 3 years lead to a license revocation of at least 6 months?

The manual lists three passing-a-stopped-school-bus violations within three years as a reason for a revocation of at least six months.

Q9. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q10. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is unsafe and often against the law, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q11. How much regular beer counts as one standard drink?

One standard drink is a 12-ounce beer, which has about as much alcohol as a shot of liquor or a glass of wine. A larger can or glass of beer counts as more than one drink.

Q12. How soon after heavy rain can a flash flood develop?

A flash flood can build within minutes or a few hours of heavy rain, often with little warning. Stay alert for water on roads, bridges and low areas during and after a downpour.

Q13. After a DWI or DWAI conviction, or a chemical test refusal, New York charges a Driver Responsibility Assessment of:

You must pay a driver responsibility assessment of $250 each year for the next three years.

Q14. Unless a sign says otherwise, the speed limit on New York highways outside a city is:

55 mph is the statewide default; a higher limit applies only where it is posted.

Q15. Your car has airbags. Do you still need to wear your seat belt?

Airbags are designed to work together with seat belts, not to replace them. Without a belt you can be thrown into the opening airbag or out of the car.

Q16. On a road with no sidewalk, where should people walk?

Where there is no sidewalk, pedestrians should walk on the left side or shoulder, facing traffic, so they can see what is coming. Drivers should expect people walking there, especially at night.

Q17. At a two-lane roundabout with no signs showing otherwise, you plan to take the first exit to the right. Which lane should you use?

To turn right at a multi-lane roundabout, enter in the right lane and exit from the right lane. Signs and lane arrows before the roundabout show which lane goes where.

Q18. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q19. When a New York school bus stops with its red lights flashing, stop your vehicle at least how far from it?

Twenty feet gives children crossing in front of or behind the bus room to be seen. Wait until the lights stop flashing.

Q20. A 15-year-old with a valid driver license from another state is visiting New York. What does New York law allow?

The New York manual says people under age 16 cannot drive in New York State, even with a license from another state.

Q21. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q22. In New York, at a NO STOPPING sign, when may you stop?

A NO STOPPING sign means you can stop only to obey a traffic sign, signal or officer, or to avoid conflicts with other vehicles.

Q23. The water covering a road ahead looks shallow. What is true about it?

Floodwater can hide dips and wash away the road surface beneath it, so you cannot judge safety by how shallow it looks. Turn around rather than try to cross.

Q24. A New York overhead lane signal is flashing a yellow X. Drivers may use that lane:

A flashing yellow X means the lane can only be used for a left turn.

Q25. New York requires you to notify the DMV of a change of address within:

The DMV sends notices to the address it has on file, so keep it current.
