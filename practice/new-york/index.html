Q1. A lane in the middle of the road marked with a solid and a broken yellow line on each side is:

The two-way left turn lane is for turning left from either direction and for turning left onto the road. Never drive along it to travel or to pass.

Q2. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q3. Overdriving your headlights means:

Low beams show about 350 feet ahead. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q4. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q5. In New York, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q6. In New York you may not park within how far of a railroad crossing?

A parked vehicle near the tracks can block the view of the crossing for trains and for other drivers.

Q7. In New York, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q8. A sign showing a U-shaped arrow inside a red circle with a slash means:

Where a U-turn is prohibited by sign, it is illegal even if the road looks empty. Use the next intersection or a block around instead.

Q9. A school bus ahead has its yellow or amber flashing lights on. This means:

Amber lights come before red. Start slowing then, because the red lights and stop arm follow within seconds.

Q10. New York requires you to notify the DMV of a change of address within:

The DMV sends notices to the address it has on file, so keep it current.
