Q1. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q2. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q3. Driving with snow and ice on the roof of your vehicle in New York:

Ice sheets flying off a roof cause serious crashes. Clear the roof, hood, lights and all glass before you drive.

Q4. You are involved in a crash. You are required to:

Stopping, identifying yourself and rendering aid are legal duties everywhere. Leaving the scene turns a minor crash into a criminal charge.

Q5. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q6. When a New York school bus stops with its red lights flashing, stop your vehicle at least how far from it?

Twenty feet gives children crossing in front of or behind the bus room to be seen. Wait until the lights stop flashing.

Q7. Three speeding or misdemeanor traffic violations within 18 months in New York lead to:

The 18 months count from the dates you committed the violations, not the dates you were convicted.

Q8. Before the road test, a New York permit holder under 18 must complete:

The certification (form MV-262) is signed by a parent or guardian and must be presented at the road test.

Q9. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q10. A red warning light appears on the dashboard while you are driving. You should:

Red generally means stop now: oil pressure, temperature, brakes or charging. Amber usually means service soon.

Q11. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q12. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q13. Under New York's Zero Tolerance Law, a driver under 21 faces license action with a blood alcohol level of:

A finding under Zero Tolerance suspends the license for six months for a first offense. At 0.08% the driver faces the full DWI charge.

Q14. Commercial vehicles are prohibited on many New York parkways because:

Low clearance strikes on parkway bridges are common with rental trucks. Watch for the posted clearance and restriction signs.

Q15. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q16. When an airbag is fitted, you should sit:

Airbags deploy explosively. Handbooks ask for 10 inches or more, some 12. Distance plus a properly worn seat belt is what makes them protective rather than harmful.

Q17. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q18. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q19. New York requires you to notify the DMV of a change of address within:

The DMV sends notices to the address it has on file, so keep it current.

Q20. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q21. In New York, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q22. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q23. A New York junior or probationary license holder convicted of hand-held phone use or texting for the first time faces:

New drivers are held to a stricter penalty than adults for the same offense.

Q24. You smell smoke and see flames under the hood. You should:

Do not open the hood; air feeds the fire. Get people at least 100 feet away and let the fire department handle it.

Q25. New York requires seat belts to be worn by:

New York extended the requirement to back-seat passengers of all ages in 2020.
