Q1. New York requires you to notify the DMV of a change of address within:

The DMV sends notices to the address it has on file, so keep it current.

Q2. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q3. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q4. Speeding fines in a work zone are usually:

Most states increase work-zone fines and add penalties when workers are present, whether or not the crew is visible at that moment.

Q5. In New York, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q6. In New York, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q7. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q8. In New York you may not park within how far of a railroad crossing?

A parked vehicle near the tracks can block the view of the crossing for trains and for other drivers.

Q9. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q10. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.
