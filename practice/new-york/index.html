Q1. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q2. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q3. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q4. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q5. Before the road test, a New York permit holder under 18 must complete:

The certification (form MV-262) is signed by a parent or guardian and must be presented at the road test.

Q6. Three speeding or misdemeanor traffic violations within 18 months in New York lead to:

The 18 months count from the dates you committed the violations, not the dates you were convicted.

Q7. In New York, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q8. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q9. Before backing out of a parking space you should:

Cameras miss low objects and fast-moving children. Look behind the vehicle before you get in, then back slowly while checking all around.

Q10. New York requires you to notify the DMV of a change of address within:

The DMV sends notices to the address it has on file, so keep it current.
