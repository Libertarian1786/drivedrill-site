Q1. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q2. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q3. In New Hampshire, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q4. Your vehicle registration must be:

Expired registration is an easy stop for an officer and, in many states, grounds for towing.

Q5. According to the New Hampshire Driver's Manual, a loaded truck traveling 55 mph under ideal conditions needs a minimum stopping distance of about:

The manual notes that a loaded truck with good tires and properly adjusted brakes traveling 55 mph under ideal conditions needs a minimum of about 335 feet to stop, more than one and a half times a car's stopping distance.

Q6. In New Hampshire, a posted school zone speed limit is set at:

New Hampshire's posted school zones reduce the usual speed limit by 10 mph, in effect from 45 minutes before school opens until opening, and from closing until 45 minutes after closing.

Q7. In New Hampshire, how far in advance should a driver signal before turning, and before changing lanes on a highway?

The New Hampshire manual directs drivers to signal at least 100 feet before a turn, and at least 500 feet before changing lanes or exiting on the highway, so other drivers have time to react.

Q8. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q9. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q10. The pedestrian signal shows a flashing orange hand with a countdown while you wait to turn. It means:

The countdown is for people already crossing. Wait until the crosswalk is clear before completing your turn.
