Q1. When does a New Hampshire Youth Operator license expire?

Drivers 16 to 20 receive a Youth Operator license, which expires on the holder's 21st birthday.

Q2. Besides classroom and driving time, New Hampshire's driver education program includes how many hours of driving observation?

New Hampshire driver education includes 6 hours of driving observation with a certified driver education instructor.

Q3. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q4. With no speed sign posted, how fast may you drive in a rural residence district in New Hampshire?

New Hampshire sets 35 mph in rural residence districts, and on class V roads outside the compact part of a town, unless another limit is posted.

Q5. Why should you never run your car's engine inside a closed garage?

Exhaust contains carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot see or smell that can kill. Never start or run your engine in a garage with the door closed.

Q6. If you must stop on a New Hampshire rural highway, you should pull off as far as possible where other drivers have a clear view of your vehicle for:

On a rural highway, pull off the road as far as you can, at a place with a clear view of 200 feet in each direction.

Q7. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q8. A police officer pulls you over and walks up to your car. What should you do with your hands?

Stay in the car and keep both hands where the officer can see them, such as on top of the steering wheel. Do not reach for your license or anything else until the officer asks.

Q9. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q10. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q11. In New Hampshire, a crash with no injuries must be reported to the DMV when the property damage is more than:

If no one is hurt, a New Hampshire crash must be reported to the DMV when property damage totals more than $1,000. If you are not sure, the manual suggests filing anyway.

Q12. For a third or later motor vehicle offense (other than a registration offense) by a New Hampshire driver under 20 with an original license, the penalty is:

A third or later motor vehicle offense brings a suspension of at least 90 days and not more than 180 days, and an approved in-person driver program before reinstatement.

Q13. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

Q14. In New Hampshire, a habitual offender convicted of driving while barred from driving can face:

Driving while a habitual offender order is in effect is a felony in New Hampshire, punishable by up to 5 years in prison (RSA 262:23).

Q15. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q16. How should you wear the shoulder part of a lap and shoulder belt?

Wear the lap belt low across your hips and the shoulder belt across your chest. Never put the shoulder belt under your arm or behind your back; in a crash it cannot hold you there and can cause injuries.

Q17. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q18. Driving at night, you see red reflectors on the lane lines shining back at you in your headlights. What does this mean?

Road reflectors are made to shine red only toward drivers going the wrong way. If they shine red in your headlights, get out of the way and turn around as soon as it is safe.

Q19. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q20. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q21. When you apply for a New Hampshire driver's license, what must you do with valid licenses from other states?

Under the One License Concept, applicants must surrender all valid driver licenses issued by any other jurisdiction.

Q22. In New Hampshire, who must wear a helmet on a motorcycle?

New Hampshire law requires all motorcycle riders and passengers under the age of 18 to wear helmets.

Q23. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, so learn your state's distance. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q24. You are inside a roundabout when an ambulance with its siren on comes up behind you. What should you do?

Do not stop inside a roundabout for an emergency vehicle. Continue to your exit or the next one, leave the roundabout, and then pull over to the right and stop.

Q25. Under New Hampshire's hand-held device law, which of these is NOT allowed while driving?

The law bans inputting information into a GPS or navigation device by hand while driving. Hands-free use by adults, 911 calls and one-handed 2-way radios are allowed.
