Q1. A space cushion around your vehicle is useful because:

Space is time. The more of it you keep on all four sides, the more options you have when something goes wrong.

Q2. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump to build pressure, downshift for engine braking, apply the parking brake gradually, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q3. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q4. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q5. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q6. In New Hampshire, a person declared a Habitual Offender by the Director of Motor Vehicles has their driving privileges revoked for:

After a hearing, a driver found to be a Habitual Offender in New Hampshire has their driving privileges revoked by the Director for 1 to 4 years, and driving during that revocation can bring imprisonment.

Q7. According to the New Hampshire Driver's Manual, a loaded truck traveling 55 mph under ideal conditions needs a minimum stopping distance of about:

The manual notes that a loaded truck with good tires and properly adjusted brakes traveling 55 mph under ideal conditions needs a minimum of about 335 feet to stop, more than one and a half times a car's stopping distance.

Q8. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q9. In New Hampshire, the automated knowledge test for a driver's license consists of how many multiple-choice questions, and when does it end automatically?

New Hampshire's automated knowledge test has 40 multiple-choice questions on the rules of the road, and the test automatically ends if more than 8 questions are answered incorrectly.

Q10. In New Hampshire, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q11. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q12. In New Hampshire, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q13. In New Hampshire, how close may you approach a school bus that is stopped with its red lights flashing and stop arm extended, on an undivided road?

New Hampshire requires drivers to stop at least 25 feet away in any direction from a school bus stopped with its red lights flashing and stop arm extended, unless the roadway is separated by a physical barrier.

Q14. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q15. In New Hampshire, the highest speed limit, set on the portion of I-93 from mile marker 45 to the Vermont border, is:

Most four-lane divided interstates and turnpikes in the state are 65 mph. Slower traffic must still keep to at least the 45 mph interstate minimum.

Q16. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q17. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q18. In New Hampshire, a posted school zone speed limit is set at:

New Hampshire's posted school zones reduce the usual speed limit by 10 mph, in effect from 45 minutes before school opens until opening, and from closing until 45 minutes after closing.

Q19. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q20. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q21. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q22. In New Hampshire, how far in advance should a driver signal before turning, and before changing lanes on a highway?

The New Hampshire manual directs drivers to signal at least 100 feet before a turn, and at least 500 feet before changing lanes or exiting on the highway, so other drivers have time to react.

Q23. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.

Q24. In New Hampshire, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q25. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.
