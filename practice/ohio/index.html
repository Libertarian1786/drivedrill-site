Q1. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q2. Driving around a lowered railroad crossing gate is:

Gates come down before a train arrives, and trains cannot stop quickly. If a gate stays down with no train, call the number posted at the crossing.

Q3. Ohio's Digest of Motor Vehicle Laws recommends keeping a following distance of at least:

Increase the gap in bad weather and behind motorcycles and large trucks.

Q4. An Ohio driver aged 16 with a probationary license may not drive between:

At 17 the window narrows to 1 a.m. to 5 a.m., and the restriction ends at 18.

Q5. A warning sign with two arrows joining into one means:

At a merge, drivers on both roads share responsibility: adjust speed and space so the traffic streams blend without anyone stopping in a travel lane.

Q6. In Ohio you must stop at least 10 feet from a school bus with red lights flashing unless:

On a road of four or more lanes, only traffic behind the bus must stop. On roads with fewer lanes, both directions stop.

Q7. You are being passed by a large truck. You should:

Hold a steady speed and keep right within your lane. Air turbulence can push a small car, so keep both hands on the wheel.

Q8. In Ohio, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q9. A police officer is directing traffic at an intersection where the light is green. You should:

Directions from an officer or flagger override signs and signals every time.

Q10. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.
