Q1. Ohio's vehicle equipment rules require tires to have a tread depth of at least:

Ohio requires tires with a minimum of 1/16-inch tread depth.

Q2. Under Ohio law, a blood, breath or urine sample used as OVI evidence must be taken within how long of the alleged violation?

Ohio law (ORC 4511.19) counts a test sample withdrawn within three hours of the time of the alleged violation. The online Digest still says two hours.

Q3. How does moving floodwater affect SUVs and pickup trucks?

Moving floodwater about two feet deep can carry away most vehicles, including SUVs and pickup trucks. Do not assume a heavy vehicle is safe in it.

Q4. An Ohio driver under 18 must complete before the driving test:

The 50 hours come on top of the required driver education course, and a parent or guardian certifies them.

Q5. In Ohio, an OVUAC (driving after underage alcohol use) can bring a license suspension of:

An OVUAC can result in a license suspension of three months to two years, plus a remedial course and retesting.

Q6. Your car is stuck in snow. What is the best way to try to free it?

Rock the car slowly forward and back without spinning the tires. Spinning the wheels only digs the car in deeper.

Q7. How soon after heavy rain can a flash flood develop?

A flash flood can build within minutes or a few hours of heavy rain, often with little warning. Stay alert for water on roads, bridges and low areas during and after a downpour.

Q8. When an airbag is fitted, you should sit:

Airbags deploy explosively. Handbooks ask for 10 inches or more, some 12. Distance plus a properly worn seat belt is what makes them protective rather than harmful.

Q9. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q10. After you move, you must usually:

Each state's law sets a short deadline. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q11. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q12. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q13. In Ohio, who must complete driver education and 50 hours of logged practice to get a license?

Ohio requires driver education and the logged practice hours for drivers under age 21, not just under 18.

Q14. After an Ohio crash involving injury or property damage, you must:

Ohio also requires proof of insurance at the scene; without it the BMV can suspend your license and registration.

Q15. Ohio requires headlights to be on:

Ohio also requires headlights whenever wipers are in continuous use because of rain, snow or sleet.

Q16. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q17. After passing Ohio's vision and knowledge tests, how long do you have to buy your temporary instruction permit (TIPIC) before you must start over?

If a TIPIC is not purchased within 60 days, the applicant must start over with the vision screening and knowledge test.

Q18. Your vehicle has four-wheel drive. How should you drive on an icy road?

Four-wheel drive does not make ice safe, because the vehicle can still skid and slide, so drive slowly and carefully. It can give a false sense of security.

Q19. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q20. In Ohio, may you let a friend drive your car if the car has no insurance?

It is illegal for a vehicle owner to allow anyone else to drive the owner's vehicle without insurance.

Q21. A motorcyclist ahead of you slows down, but no brake light comes on. Why can that happen?

Motorcyclists often slow down by downshifting or rolling off the throttle, which does not turn on the brake light. Leave extra space and watch the rider, not just the light.

Q22. Under Ohio's Move Over law, which of these stopped vehicles with warning lights must you move over or slow down for?

Ohio's Move Over law covers stopped police and emergency vehicles, road service vehicles, and waste collection, highway maintenance and public utility commission vehicles displaying warning lights.

Q23. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. The law often also requires them in rain, fog or snow, or whenever the wipers are on.

Q24. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q25. You stop behind a large truck that is stopped on an uphill grade. Why should you leave extra space?

A large truck can drift back as it starts up from a stop on a hill. Stop well back so you have room if it rolls toward you.
