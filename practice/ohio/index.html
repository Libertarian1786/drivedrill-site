Q1. In the first 12 months with an Ohio probationary license, a driver under 18 may carry:

The passenger limit is one of the strongest protections for new drivers, because crash risk climbs sharply with teen passengers.

Q2. When parking on an Ohio street, your vehicle must be facing the direction of traffic and no more than:

Parking against traffic is illegal in Ohio as well as unsafe, because you must cross the oncoming lane to leave.

Q3. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q4. During a level 3 snow emergency declared by a county sheriff in Ohio:

Level 1 means roads are hazardous, level 2 means only necessary travel, and level 3 closes the roads to everyone but emergency personnel.

Q5. In Ohio, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q6. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q7. Adjusting your side mirrors slightly outward compared with the usual setting:

Wider mirror angles shrink the blind spot, but a head check before changing lanes is still the move that catches what mirrors miss.

Q8. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q9. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q10. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q11. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q12. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q13. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q14. In Ohio, a driver under 21 commits OVUAC (operating a vehicle after underage consumption) at a blood alcohol level of:

The under-21 threshold is 0.02%. A conviction suspends the license and, for a driver under 18, can delay full licensing.

Q15. After an Ohio crash involving injury or property damage, you must:

Ohio also requires proof of insurance at the scene; without it the BMV can suspend your license and registration.

Q16. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q17. An Ohio driver under 18 must complete before the driving test:

The 50 hours come on top of the required driver education course, and a parent or guardian certifies them.

Q18. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q19. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q20. The maximum speed limit on Ohio rural interstate highways is:

70 mph is the maximum on rural interstates; urban interstates are commonly 65 mph and some city freeways are lower.

Q21. In Ohio, you may cross a solid yellow line to pass a slow-moving vehicle only when it is traveling:

The exception is for slow-moving vehicles such as bicycles or vehicles showing a slow-moving vehicle sign. You must still be able to pass safely.

Q22. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q23. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q24. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.

Q25. Ohio law prohibits parking within how far of a fire hydrant?

Ohio's distance is shorter than the 15 feet many states use, which is why it is worth learning for the Ohio test.
