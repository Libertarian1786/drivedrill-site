Q1. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q2. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q3. In Ohio you must stop at least 10 feet from a school bus with red lights flashing unless:

On a road of four or more lanes, only traffic behind the bus must stop. On roads with fewer lanes, both directions stop.

Q4. You are being passed by a large truck. You should:

Hold a steady speed and keep right within your lane. Air turbulence can push a small car, so keep both hands on the wheel.

Q5. Ohio's Digest of Motor Vehicle Laws recommends keeping a following distance of at least:

Increase the gap in bad weather and behind motorcycles and large trucks.

Q6. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q7. An Ohio driver aged 16 with a probationary license may not drive between:

At 17 the window narrows to 1 a.m. to 5 a.m., and the restriction ends at 18.

Q8. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q9. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q10. In Ohio, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.
