Q1. In Maine, demerit points on your driving record are erased when they:

Maine erases demerit points from a driver's record once they become one year old.

Q2. In Maine, a provisional license holder who is convicted of a moving violation while driving on that license faces a suspension of:

A moving violation conviction while holding a provisional license in Maine results in a 30-day suspension.

Q3. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q4. Passing on the right is allowed when:

You may pass on the right on multi-lane roads or when a vehicle is turning left, but never by driving onto the shoulder.

Q5. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q6. Your right wheels drift onto a soft shoulder. You should:

Yanking the wheel at speed flips the car back across the road. Slow first, then ease back on with a gentle steering input.

Q7. A pedestrian steps into an unmarked crosswalk at an intersection. You must:

Every intersection has a crosswalk whether or not it is painted. Yield to people crossing there.

Q8. In Maine, the manual recommends keeping your vehicle how far behind the one in front under normal conditions?

Maine's manual recommends keeping at least a 4-second gap behind the vehicle ahead.

Q9. In Maine, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q10. A steady yellow traffic light means:

Yellow is the warning that red is next. Stop if you can do it safely; if you are already so close that stopping would be abrupt or unsafe, continue through with care.
