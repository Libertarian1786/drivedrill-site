Q1. In Maine, a driver's license for someone under 65 is valid until:

A Maine license is valid until your sixth birthday after the date of issue, or your fourth birthday after issue if you are 65 or older.

Q2. In Maine, the fine for speeding in a school zone is:

Maine charges twice the usual fine for going over the posted speed limit in a school zone.

Q3. A white diamond is painted in a freeway lane. What does it mean?

A diamond symbol marks a high-occupancy vehicle (HOV), or carpool, lane. Signs tell you how many people must be in the vehicle and when the rule applies.

Q4. In Maine, a driver is involved in a crash in which someone is killed. What does the law require?

Every driver involved in a fatal crash, or one where a death is likely, must submit to a chemical test. Failing to do so brings a one-year suspension.

Q5. In Maine, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q6. On sections of the Maine Turnpike and I-95 with three lanes in one direction, where may trucks (not pickups) drive?

Where the Maine Turnpike or I-95 has three or more lanes in one direction, trucks other than pickups may use only the two farthest right-hand lanes.

Q7. Under Maine's violation-free credit system, how do drivers earn credit points?

Maine awards one credit point for each calendar year free of convictions or suspensions, and a driver can build up to four of them.

Q8. A crash with a large animal is about to happen. What does the Maine manual tell you to do?

Brake and steer straight. Letting up on the brakes just before impact lets the front of the car rise slightly, which may keep the animal off the windshield. Duck down to protect yourself.

Q9. In Maine, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q10. In Maine, the drivers must report a crash to the police or sheriff if anyone is hurt or the property damage is at least:

Report a crash with any injury, or with property damage of $2,000 or more, to the police or the local sheriff. Not reporting can bring criminal charges and a suspension.

Q11. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q12. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q13. The driver ahead of you is signaling a left turn. What is true about passing that driver?

Never pass on the left of a driver who is signaling or making a left turn. If there is room and it is legal, you may pass on the right; otherwise slow down and wait.

Q14. Good drivers look:

Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q15. Your vehicle has four-wheel drive. How should you drive on an icy road?

Four-wheel drive does not make ice safe, because the vehicle can still skid and slide, so drive slowly and carefully. It can give a false sense of security.

Q16. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q17. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q18. If you double your speed, about how much longer is your braking distance?

Braking distance grows much faster than speed. Doubling your speed makes the braking distance, and the force of a crash, about four times greater.

Q19. You are inside a roundabout when an ambulance with its siren on comes up behind you. What should you do?

Do not stop inside a roundabout for an emergency vehicle. Continue to your exit or the next one, leave the roundabout, and then pull over to the right and stop.

Q20. You see a large puddle across your lane on a wet road. What is the best way to handle it?

Slow down and go around large puddles when it is safe to do so. Standing water can cause hydroplaning, can hide a pothole, and can make your brakes work poorly.

Q21. Two solid white lines side by side separate two lanes going the same way. What do they mean?

Double solid white lines are a barrier between lanes going the same way, such as beside a carpool lane. Do not cross them to change lanes; wait until they end or become broken.

Q22. According to the Maine manual, how should a child under age 2 ride?

Infants under 2 should always ride in the back seat facing the rear. Children 2 and older face forward.

Q23. A space cushion around your vehicle is useful because:

Space is time. The more of it you keep on all four sides, the more options you have when something goes wrong.

Q24. The water covering a road ahead looks shallow. What is true about it?

Floodwater can hide dips and wash away the road surface beneath it, so you cannot judge safety by how shallow it looks. Turn around rather than try to cross.

Q25. You stop for a red light at a corner with a crosswalk. Where should your car be?

Stop before the crosswalk, not in it or hanging over it. A car in the crosswalk forces pedestrians, including people who are blind or use wheelchairs, to walk around it into traffic.
