Q1. In South Carolina, when may you pass a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing without stopping first?

Either condition is enough on its own. On an undivided road of fewer than four lanes, traffic in both directions stops, and overtaking a bus from behind always requires a stop.

Q2. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q3. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q4. In South Carolina, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q5. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

Q6. In South Carolina's driver manual, the old 2 and 10 o'clock steering hand position is discouraged mainly because:

South Carolina's manual specifically warns that the 2 and 10 o'clock hand position is not recommended because it can be dangerous in vehicles equipped with airbags.

Q7. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q8. In South Carolina, if you fail the driving skills test on your first attempt, you must wait before being retested:

South Carolina requires a wait of at least two business days after a first failed skills test before you can retest; the wait gets longer on later failed attempts.

Q9. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q10. An anti-lock braking system is designed to:

ABS preserves steering control. On gravel or deep snow it may even lengthen the stop, but you can still steer around the hazard.
