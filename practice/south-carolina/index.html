25 sample questions from DriveDrill's 449-question South Carolina bank. Pick an answer to see if you got it right, and why.

Q1. Fluorescent yellow-green warning signs usually warn you about:

Fluorescent yellow-green is used for warning signs about schools, school buses, pedestrians and bicycles, so you notice where these people may be.

Q2. South Carolina's driver education course for young applicants includes:

The South Carolina manual says the driver education course is 8 hours in the classroom and 6 hours of driving.

Q3. According to South Carolina's driver manual, at 50 mph on dry pavement with good brakes, braking distance alone can take about:

The manual gives about 158 feet as the braking distance at 50 mph on dry pavement with good brakes, not counting perception and reaction distance.

Q4. A driver holds their left arm out the window, bent at the elbow and pointing down. What does that mean?

When a driver's left arm points down from the window, the driver is slowing down or stopping.

Q5. In South Carolina, does the law to stop for a school bus with flashing red lights also apply on private roads?

South Carolina law (56-5-2770) requires stopping for a stopped school bus with flashing red lights on a highway or a private road.

Q6. In South Carolina, completing an approved 8-hour defensive driving course can take up to how many points off your record?

An approved 8-hour defensive driving course taken in South Carolina can cut up to 4 points, and a reduction is allowed only once in 3 years.

Q7. You are on an on-ramp joining a highway, and a yellow ADDED LANE sign is posted. What does it mean for you?

An ADDED LANE sign means two roads come together and a lane is added, so the entering lane keeps going and you are not required to merge. Still watch for traffic changing lanes.

Q8. In South Carolina, is a driver required by law to use the zipper merge where two lanes become one?

The South Carolina manual explains the zipper merge but says the law does not require drivers to use it.

Q9. Your vehicle has four-wheel drive. How should you drive on an icy road?

Four-wheel drive does not make ice safe, because the vehicle can still skid and slide, so drive slowly and carefully. It can give a false sense of security.

Q10. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q11. Under South Carolina's hands-free law, a second offense brings:

A second or later violation of the Hands-Free and Distracted Driving Act costs $200 and adds 2 points to your driving record.

Q12. How does driving faster change what you can see?

The faster you drive, the narrower your field of vision becomes, so hazards at the sides of the road are harder to notice. Slow down where people or cars may come from the side.

Q13. What usually causes a locked-wheel skid?

A locked-wheel skid usually comes from braking too hard at too high a speed, so the wheels stop turning and slide. Ease off the brake to let the wheels roll again.

Q14. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q15. A pedestrian starts across the street, and your light turns green before they reach the other side. What must you do?

If the light changes after a pedestrian has entered the crosswalk, the pedestrian still has the right-of-way. Wait until they have finished crossing before you go.

Q16. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q17. In South Carolina, if you fail the driving skills test on your first attempt, you must wait before being retested:

South Carolina requires a wait of at least two business days after a first failed skills test before you can retest; the wait gets longer on later failed attempts.

Q18. In South Carolina, a beginner's permit holder driving between 6 a.m. and midnight must have which supervisor in the front seat?

From 6 a.m. to midnight, the supervisor must be a licensed driver at least 21 years old with at least 1 year of driving experience, seated in the front.

Q19. Two solid white lines side by side separate two lanes going the same way. What do they mean?

Double solid white lines are a barrier between lanes going the same way, such as beside a carpool lane. Do not cross them to change lanes; wait until they end or become broken.

Q20. You are at a flashing red light. What should you remember about the traffic on the cross street?

Traffic on the cross street often has a flashing yellow light and may not have to stop at all. Do not pull out until there is a safe gap.

Q21. If you fail the South Carolina skills test on your second attempt, how long must you wait to try again?

After a second failed skills test, South Carolina makes you wait 7 calendar days. After a third failure, the wait is longer.

Q22. Do traffic violations you commit in another state count on your South Carolina driving record?

Your South Carolina record includes violations committed outside the state.

Q23. A white diamond is painted in a freeway lane. What does it mean?

A diamond symbol marks a high-occupancy vehicle (HOV), or carpool, lane. Signs tell you how many people must be in the vehicle and when the rule applies.

Q24. When the lights at a railroad crossing start to flash, about how much warning do you usually have before the train arrives?

Crossing signals are set to give at least about 20 seconds of warning before the train reaches the crossing. When the lights start flashing, stop; there is not enough time to try to beat the train.

Q25. You come to a stop sign, but no white stop line is painted on the road. Where should you stop?

Stop at the stop line if there is one. If there is no stop line, stop before the crosswalk; if there is no crosswalk either, stop before you enter the intersection, where you can see the cross traffic.
