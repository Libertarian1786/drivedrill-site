Q1. Your car stalls in rising floodwater. If you can leave it safely, what should you do?

If your car stalls in floodwater, leave it as soon as you safely can and move to higher ground. Floodwater can rise quickly and sweep a car and its passengers away, and trying to move a stalled vehicle has cost lives.

Q2. On Alaska's point table, leaving the scene of a crash carries:

Alaska's point table gives 9 points for leaving the scene of a crash.

Q3. A snowplow is working on the road ahead of you. What is the best choice?

Stay well back from a working snowplow, because it throws sand and snow and may stop or back up. Never pass it on the shoulder side.

Q4. You are stopped, waiting for a gap in oncoming traffic so you can turn left. How should you keep your front wheels?

Keep your wheels straight until it is safe to start your turn. If you are hit from behind while your wheels are turned left, you could be pushed into oncoming traffic.

Q5. After an Alaska crash that must be reported, which drivers must send proof of insurance?

The Alaska manual says proof of insurance is required from all the drivers in the crash, whoever caused it.

Q6. Fog becomes so thick that you cannot see well enough to drive safely. What is the safest choice?

When fog is too thick to see the road, pull well off the road and wait for it to lift. Stopping or creeping in a travel lane risks being hit by drivers who cannot see you.

Q7. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q8. A driver holds their left arm out the window, bent at the elbow and pointing down. What does that mean?

When a driver's left arm points down from the window, the driver is slowing down or stopping.

Q9. You stop at a red light far behind the stop line. Why can that be a problem at some signals?

Many signals use sensors in the pavement near the stop line to detect waiting cars. If you stop too far back, the signal may not know you are there.

Q10. At a railroad crossing, the gate goes up, but the red lights are still flashing. What should you do?

Do not cross the tracks until the red lights stop flashing, even if the gate rises. Another train may be coming.

Q11. Strong, gusty winds are blowing across the highway. What should you do about cruise control?

In high winds, do not use cruise control. Keep your own foot on the accelerator so you can react right away when a gust hits.

Q12. What can you do to reduce the glare of oncoming headlights on your windshield at night?

A clean windshield, inside and out, helps cut the glare from oncoming headlights. A dirty windshield increases glare and hides the road, signs and other drivers.

Q13. What does Alaska law require you to do with your turn signal after you finish a turn or lane change?

Alaska law requires you to cancel your turn signal after you use it, so other drivers are not misled.

Q14. Before an Alaska driver's license is given back after a suspension, what must the driver usually file?

The Alaska manual says that after a suspension you must file proof of future financial responsibility. This is an insurance filing form, often called an SR-22.

Q15. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q16. In Alaska, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q17. In Alaska, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit posted on the state's rural interstates. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q18. Alaska's statutory speed limit for a school zone, when no other limit is posted, is:

Alaska's speed limit in a school zone is 20 mph unless another limit is posted.

Q19. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q20. In Alaska, if police did not investigate a crash that must be reported, how soon must you report it to the DMV?

Alaska drivers must report such a crash to the Division of Motor Vehicles within 10 days. A report is not needed if police investigated.

Q21. In Alaska, the driver manual suggests that when backing your vehicle, your speed should not exceed:

Backing is one of the more dangerous maneuvers because visibility is limited, so the manual suggests keeping speed very low while watching all sides of the vehicle.

Q22. How soon after heavy rain can a flash flood develop?

A flash flood can build within minutes or a few hours of heavy rain, often with little warning. Stay alert for water on roads, bridges and low areas during and after a downpour.

Q23. In Alaska, on a road that is not a business, residential, alley or school zone, and no limit is posted, the speed limit is:

Alaska's statutory speed on any other roadway is 55 mph, unless a different limit is posted.

Q24. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q25. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.
