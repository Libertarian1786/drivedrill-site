Q1. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q2. Worn tire tread is dangerous mainly because it:

Tread channels water away. Once it is shallow, wet grip falls off sharply. Check with a tread gauge or the built-in wear bars.

Q3. In Alaska, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q4. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q5. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q6. In Alaska, a person learning to drive on an instruction permit must be accompanied by a licensed driver who:

Alaska requires the supervising licensed driver for an instruction permit holder to be at least 21 years old and to have at least a year of experience driving that same type or class of vehicle.

Q7. According to the Alaska Driver Manual, if you double your speed from 20 to 40 mph, the impact force of a crash:

The manual explains that striking power does not increase in direct proportion to speed; doubling speed from 20 to 40 mph makes the impact and braking distance 4 times greater, not just double.

Q8. Good drivers look:

Handbooks give figures from 10 to 30 seconds ahead. Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q9. In Alaska, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q10. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.
