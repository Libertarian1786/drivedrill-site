Q1. In Alaska, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q2. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q3. In Alaska, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q4. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q5. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q6. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q7. When an airbag is fitted, you should sit:

Airbags deploy explosively. Handbooks ask for 10 inches or more, some 12. Distance plus a properly worn seat belt is what makes them protective rather than harmful.

Q8. In Alaska, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q9. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q10. In Alaska, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Q11. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q12. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, commonly 10 to 15 feet. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q13. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q14. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q15. In Alaska, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it.

Q16. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q17. In Alaska, the driver manual suggests that when backing your vehicle, your speed should not exceed:

Backing is one of the more dangerous maneuvers because visibility is limited, so the manual suggests keeping speed very low while watching all sides of the vehicle.

Q18. In Alaska, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q19. According to the Alaska Driver Manual, if you double your speed from 20 to 40 mph, the impact force of a crash:

The manual explains that striking power does not increase in direct proportion to speed; doubling speed from 20 to 40 mph makes the impact and braking distance 4 times greater, not just double.

Q20. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q21. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q22. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q23. In Alaska, a person learning to drive on an instruction permit must be accompanied by a licensed driver who:

Alaska requires the supervising licensed driver for an instruction permit holder to be at least 21 years old and to have at least a year of experience driving that same type or class of vehicle.

Q24. Under Alaska law, a child must ride in a rear-facing car seat if the child is:

Alaska's child passenger safety law requires a rear-facing car seat for any child under age 1, and also for an older child who still weighs less than 20 pounds.

Q25. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.
