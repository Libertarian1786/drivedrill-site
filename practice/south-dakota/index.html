Q1. In South Dakota, when may you pass a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing without stopping first?

A center turn lane does not count toward the four. On anything narrower, traffic in both directions stops, and overtaking a bus from behind always requires a stop.

Q2. In South Dakota, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q3. In South Dakota, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q4. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.

Q5. A motorcycle is entitled to:

Never share a lane with a motorcycle. Riders move within the lane to avoid road hazards and to stay visible.

Q6. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q7. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q8. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q9. In South Dakota, under the point system, a driver's first license suspension lasts:

South Dakota's point system sets escalating suspension periods, starting with 60 days for a first suspension.

Q10. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.
