Q1. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.

Q2. In South Dakota, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offense. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.

Q3. In South Dakota, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q4. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q5. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q6. Unless a sign prohibits it, a right turn on red is allowed:

Right on red requires a full stop first, then yielding to everyone already entitled to go. A NO TURN ON RED sign removes the option entirely.

Q7. A funeral procession with its lights on is passing through an intersection. You should:

Do not cut into or interrupt a procession, even if you have a green light, unless directed by an officer.

Q8. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q9. In South Dakota, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q10. In South Dakota, under the point system, a driver's first license suspension lasts:

South Dakota's point system sets escalating suspension periods, starting with 60 days for a first suspension.
