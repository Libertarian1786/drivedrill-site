Q1. In California, the speed limit at a blind intersection, in an alley, or within 100 feet of a railroad crossing where you cannot see the track is:

15 mph covers blind intersections, alleys and blind railroad crossings. A blind intersection is one where you cannot see 100 feet in each direction in the last 100 feet before crossing.

Q2. During the first 12 months with a California provisional license, a driver under 18 may not:

The first 12 months also restrict driving between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. without a licensed adult 25 or older in the vehicle.

Q3. Refusing a chemical test after a lawful DUI arrest in California results in:

Refusal carries its own suspension under implied consent, separate from anything the court does, and the refusal can be used as evidence.

Q4. In California, a driver under 21 may have their license suspended for driving with a blood alcohol concentration of:

California's zero tolerance threshold is 0.01%, which can be reached by cough syrup or mouthwash. The administrative suspension is one year.

Q5. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q6. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle traveling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q7. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q8. In California, how far may you travel in a two-way center left turn lane before turning?

Enter the center left turn lane no more than 200 feet before your turn. It is not a travel lane.

Q9. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q10. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q11. California's Move Over law requires you to:

Since January 1, 2026, the law covers any stopped vehicle with flashing hazard lights or another warning device such as cones or flares, not only emergency vehicles, tow trucks and highway maintenance vehicles. If a lane change is unsafe or impracticable, slow to a reasonable and prudent speed as you pass.

Q12. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q13. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q14. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump to build pressure, downshift for engine braking, apply the parking brake gradually, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q15. A space cushion around your vehicle is useful because:

Space is time. The more of it you keep on all four sides, the more options you have when something goes wrong.

Q16. In California, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

California requires hands-free operation for all drivers. Drivers under 18 may not use a phone at all, hands-free or not.

Q17. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q18. On a two-lane California road, if five or more vehicles are lined up behind you, you must:

The turnout rule applies to any slow-moving vehicle holding up a line of traffic where passing is unsafe.

Q19. Unless otherwise posted, the speed limit in a California residential or business district is:

25 mph is the default for residential and business districts, and it also applies in school zones when children are present unless posted lower.

Q20. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q21. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q22. In California, a vehicle towing a trailer may not exceed:

Trailers and large trucks are held to 55 mph in California even where other traffic may do 65 or 70.

Q23. Your vehicle registration must be:

Expired registration is an easy stop for an officer and, in many states, grounds for towing.

Q24. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q25. After a crash with injury or significant damage you must usually:

Every state sets a damage threshold and a deadline for reporting. Exchange information at the scene and file the report on time.
