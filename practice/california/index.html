Q1. California's handbook says that when rain or snow means you cannot see farther than 100 feet, you should not drive faster than:

At 30 mph you can still stop within the distance you can see. Any faster, and you are driving into road you cannot see yet.

Q2. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump the pedal to build pressure. If that does not work, apply the parking brake gradually and shift to lower gears for engine braking, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q3. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q4. Most traffic convictions and collisions stay on a California driving record for:

California's handbook says traffic convictions and collisions stay on your record for 36 months or longer, depending on the type of conviction.

Q5. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q6. A U-turn in a California residential district is legal only if no vehicle is approaching within:

The same 200 feet also applies to visibility: never make a U-turn where you cannot see clearly for 200 feet in each direction.

Q7. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q8. How should you wear the shoulder part of a lap and shoulder belt?

Wear the lap belt low across your hips and the shoulder belt across your chest. Never put the shoulder belt under your arm or behind your back; in a crash it cannot hold you there and can cause injuries.

Q9. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q10. A pedestrian hybrid beacon shows two red lights flashing back and forth. What may you do?

Alternating flashing red on a pedestrian hybrid beacon works like a stop sign: stop, then go if the pedestrians are out of your path and it is safe. The beacon has no green light.

Q11. In California, you should signal at least how far before you turn?

California's handbook says to signal at least 100 feet before you turn.

Q12. A white bicycle symbol with two arrowheads above it is painted in a travel lane. What does this marking tell you?

This is a shared-lane marking, or sharrow. It shows that bicyclists may ride in the lane you are driving in and where they are likely to ride, so watch for them and give them room.

Q13. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is safer, and the law often requires it.

Q14. When a train is coming and you must stop at a railroad crossing, stop no closer to the nearest rail than:

Fifteen feet keeps you clear of the train's overhang and of the gate arm. Stopping no farther than 50 feet back also lets you still see the tracks.

Q15. In California, how far may you travel in a two-way center left turn lane before turning?

Enter the center left turn lane no more than 200 feet before your turn. It is not a travel lane.

Q16. California's minimum liability insurance must cover at least:

California's handbook says insurance must cover at least $30,000 for a single death or injury, $60,000 for death or injury to more than one person, and $15,000 for property damage. These limits took effect in 2025.

Q17. In California, a curb painted red means:

California's handbook says a red curb means no stopping, standing or parking. Buses may stop at a red zone marked for buses only.

Q18. Which driver mistake is a common cause of crashes with bicyclists?

A common driver-caused bicycle crash happens when a driver turns left in front of a bicyclist coming the other way. Look for bicycles before you turn and give them the right-of-way.

Q19. You see a large puddle across your lane on a wet road. What is the best way to handle it?

Slow down and go around large puddles when it is safe to do so. Standing water can cause hydroplaning, can hide a pothole, and can make your brakes work poorly.

Q20. In California, two sets of solid double yellow lines spaced two or more feet apart are:

California's handbook says two sets of double yellow lines two or more feet apart are a barrier. Do not drive over it, turn left or make a U-turn across it, except at designated openings.

Q21. In California, a vehicle towing a trailer may not exceed:

Trailers and large trucks are held to 55 mph in California, even where other traffic may go faster.

Q22. You start driving on a snow-covered road. How can you find out how much traction your tires have?

Start out slowly, then test your brakes gently once the car is moving to feel how much traction you have. A gentle test warns you before you need to stop.

Q23. A driver holds their left arm out the window, bent at the elbow and pointing down. What does that mean?

When a driver's left arm points down from the window, the driver is slowing down or stopping.

Q24. In California you may not park within:

California sets hydrant clearance at 15 feet. Also avoid parking within 3 feet of a sidewalk ramp or in a bike lane.

Q25. A car moves into the space in front of you and is much too close. What should you do first?

If a vehicle merges in front of you too closely, ease off the accelerator to rebuild your following distance. Braking hard could cause the driver behind you to hit you.
