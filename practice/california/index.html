Q1. During the first 12 months, a California provisional driver under 18 may not drive between:

There are documented exceptions for work, school, medical need and family necessity, each of which needs a signed note.

Q2. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.

Q3. A wide white line across your lane at an intersection is:

Stop at the line, not past it. If your view is blocked once stopped, edge forward slowly only after stopping at the line.

Q4. During the first 12 months with a California provisional license, a driver under 18 may not:

The first 12 months also restrict driving between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. without a licensed adult 25 or older in the vehicle.

Q5. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q6. A sign at a crosswalk reading STATE LAW: YIELD TO PEDESTRIANS placed in the middle of the road means:

In-street yield signs mark crossings where drivers regularly fail to stop. Yield to anyone in the crosswalk, and never pass a vehicle stopped at one.

Q7. In California, the speed limit at a blind intersection, in an alley, or within 100 feet of a railroad crossing where you cannot see the track is:

15 mph covers blind intersections, alleys and blind railroad crossings. A blind intersection is one where you cannot see 100 feet in each direction in the last 100 feet before crossing.

Q8. Refusing a chemical test after a lawful DUI arrest in California results in:

Refusal carries its own suspension under implied consent, separate from anything the court does, and the refusal can be used as evidence.

Q9. A school bus ahead has its yellow or amber flashing lights on. This means:

Amber lights come before red. Start slowing then, because the red lights and stop arm follow within seconds.

Q10. A left turn on red from a one-way street onto another one-way street is:

Most states permit this turn after a complete stop and yielding, but it is banned in some cities, so obey any posted sign.
