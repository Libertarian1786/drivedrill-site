Q1. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q2. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown guide signs mark recreational and cultural destinations such as parks, trailheads, campgrounds and historic sites.

Q3. In Virginia, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q4. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q5. In Virginia, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q6. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q7. A broken yellow center line means:

Yellow separates traffic moving in opposite directions. Broken on your side means passing is allowed when you can see far enough ahead and the gap is safe.

Q8. In Virginia, if you refuse a breath test or test at .08 percent or higher when charged with DUI, your driving privilege is automatically suspended for how long on a first offense?

Virginia's administrative license suspension for refusing a test or testing at .08 or higher is seven days for a first offense, longer for repeat offenses, on top of any court penalty.

Q9. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q10. In Virginia, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.
