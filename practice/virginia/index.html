Q1. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q2. In Virginia, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q3. In Virginia, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q4. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q5. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q6. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q7. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump to build pressure, downshift for engine braking, apply the parking brake gradually, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q8. In Virginia, when driving at night and following another vehicle, you should switch to low-beam headlights once you are within:

The manual instructs drivers to use low beams whenever they are within 200 feet of the vehicle they are following, to avoid blinding that driver.

Q9. In Virginia, if you refuse a breath test or test at .08 percent or higher when charged with DUI, your driving privilege is automatically suspended for how long on a first offense?

Virginia's administrative license suspension for refusing a test or testing at .08 or higher is seven days for a first offense, longer for repeat offenses, on top of any court penalty.

Q10. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.
