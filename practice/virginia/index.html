Q1. In Virginia, when passing a bicyclist, state law requires motorists to leave a clearance of at least:

Virginia law requires drivers to pass cyclists with at least three feet of clearance, changing lanes if the lane is too narrow to allow it.

Q2. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q3. In Virginia, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q4. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q5. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q6. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q7. In Virginia, if you refuse a breath test or test at .08 percent or higher when charged with DUI, your driving privilege is automatically suspended for how long on a first offense?

Virginia's administrative license suspension for refusing a test or testing at .08 or higher is seven days for a first offense, longer for repeat offenses, on top of any court penalty.

Q8. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q9. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q10. In Virginia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q11. In Virginia, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q12. In Virginia, unless a sign states otherwise, the maximum speed limit in school, business, and residential areas is:

Virginia law sets 25 mph as the default limit for school, business, and residential areas when no sign posts a different limit, with 35 mph on unpaved roads and 55 mph on all other roads.

Q13. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q14. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q15. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q16. In Virginia, you must never follow an emergency vehicle with its lights flashing closer than:

Virginia law prohibits following an emergency vehicle with flashing lights any closer than 500 feet.

Q17. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q18. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q19. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q20. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q21. In Virginia, for policies effective on or after January 1, 2022, the minimum liability insurance coverage for injury or death of one person is:

Virginia raised the minimum liability coverage for injury or death of one person to $30,000 for policies effective on or after January 1, 2022.

Q22. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q23. In Virginia, when driving at night and following another vehicle, you should switch to low-beam headlights once you are within:

The manual instructs drivers to use low beams whenever they are within 200 feet of the vehicle they are following, to avoid blinding that driver.

Q24. In Virginia, a driver under 21 convicted of driving after illegally consuming alcohol, with a BAC of at least .02 but under .08 percent, faces a license suspension of:

For a first offense in this BAC range, Virginia law suspends the young driver's privilege for one year from the date of conviction, along with a minimum fine or community service.

Q25. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.
