Free Mississippi permit practice test: 25 questions

25 sample questions from DriveDrill's 234-question Mississippi bank. Pick an answer to see if you got it right, and why.

Q1. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q2. You may park in a space marked with the International Symbol of Access:

Accessible spaces need a valid placard or plate, and the person it belongs to must be involved in the trip. Fines are steep and vehicles are towed.

Q3. In Mississippi, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q4. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q5. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q6. In Mississippi, when a gate is down, a signal is flashing or a train is approaching, you must stop:

You must stop even if you do not see a train, and stay stopped until it is safe to cross.

Q7. In Mississippi, a crash must be reported to law enforcement if it involves injury, death, or apparent property damage exceeding:

Mississippi requires drivers to report a crash to law enforcement if it involves injury, death, or apparent property damage exceeding $500.00.

Q8. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q9. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q10. In Mississippi, when a school bus ahead of you has its red lights flashing, how far away must you come to a complete stop?

Mississippi law requires drivers to come to a complete stop at least ten feet away from a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing.

Q11. In Mississippi, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q12. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q13. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q14. In Mississippi, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offense. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.

Q15. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q16. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q17. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q18. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.

Q19. In Mississippi, the minimum liability insurance coverage required for injury to a single person in a single-vehicle accident is:

Mississippi's minimum insurance amounts include $25,000 for a single person in a single vehicle accident.

Q20. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q21. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q22. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q23. In Mississippi, the maximum speed limit in a school zone is:

Mississippi's manual sets the maximum speed for any vehicle in a school zone at 15 mph.

Q24. In Mississippi, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q25. Under Mississippi's Squatted Vehicle Law, it is unlawful to drive a passenger vehicle if the front fender has been raised how much higher than the rear fender?

Mississippi's Squatted Vehicle Law makes it unlawful to drive a passenger vehicle if the front fender height is raised four or more inches greater than the rear fender.
