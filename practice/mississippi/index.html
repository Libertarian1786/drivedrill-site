Q1. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q2. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q3. A sign showing a truck on a downward slope warns of:

On a steep downgrade, shift to a lower gear before you start down and use steady light braking so your brakes do not overheat and fade.

Q4. In Mississippi, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q5. In Mississippi, you are required by law to dim your headlights to low beams when you come within how many feet of an oncoming vehicle?

Mississippi law requires drivers to use low beams when approaching within five hundred feet of an oncoming vehicle.

Q6. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.

Q7. In Mississippi, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so roughly one drink can reach it. The licence consequence is administrative and starts before any court date.

Q8. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q9. In Mississippi, a Regular Learner's Permit holder may only drive when accompanied by a licensed driver who is:

A Mississippi learner's permit entitles the holder to drive only when accompanied by a licensed driver aged twenty-one or older who is physically occupying the seat next to them.

Q10. In a work zone you should:

Rear-end collisions are the most common work-zone crash. More space is what prevents them.
