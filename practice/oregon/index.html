Q1. You stop at a red light far behind the stop line. Why can that be a problem at some signals?

Many signals use sensors in the pavement near the stop line to detect waiting cars. If you stop too far back, the signal may not know you are there.

Q2. At a two-lane roundabout with no signs showing otherwise, you plan to take the first exit to the right. Which lane should you use?

To turn right at a multi-lane roundabout, enter in the right lane and exit from the right lane. Signs and lane arrows before the roundabout show which lane goes where.

Q3. In Oregon, you may not park within how many feet of a fire hydrant?

Oregon does not allow parking within 10 feet of a fire hydrant.

Q4. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q5. Parking is prohibited:

Never park in a crosswalk, on a sidewalk, in an intersection, or where you would block a curb ramp.

Q6. The water covering a road ahead looks shallow. What is true about it?

Floodwater can hide dips and wash away the road surface beneath it, so you cannot judge safety by how shallow it looks. Turn around rather than try to cross.

Q7. In Oregon, you may not park within how many feet of the nearest rail of a railroad or light rail crossing?

Oregon does not allow parking within 50 feet of the nearest rail of a railroad or light rail crossing.

Q8. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q9. In Oregon, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q10. When you glance over your shoulder to check your blind spot, what should you avoid?

When you check your blind spot, turn only your head. Turning your whole body or the steering wheel can pull the car out of its lane.

Q11. In Oregon, a streetcar in the lane next to you is going the same way you are. You should:

Never turn in front of a streetcar or train going your way from an adjacent lane, even if it is stopped.

Q12. In Oregon, if your disabled vehicle cannot be seen from 200 feet away, warning devices such as flares or reflectors must be placed:

When a disabled vehicle cannot be seen from 200 feet, place warning devices at least 200 feet from it in each direction.

Q13. In Oregon, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

70 mph is allowed on parts of I-84 and I-82 in eastern Oregon; most other rural interstates are 65 mph. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q14. Under Oregon's Open Container law, any open bottle or container of alcohol in the vehicle should be:

Oregon's Open Container law treats a bottle as open if its seal is broken even if it has been reclosed, so any open alcohol container belongs in the trunk, out of the passenger area.

Q15. In Oregon, at an intersection with a bike box (a painted area for bicycles in front of the stop line), on a red light you must stop:

You must stop for a traffic signal behind the bike box. Do not stop in the box; it is for bicyclists.

Q16. In Oregon, a RIGHT TURN PERMITTED WITHOUT STOPPING sign under a stop sign means:

That sign under a stop sign lets traffic turning right make the turn without stopping. Drivers going straight or turning left must still stop.

Q17. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q18. You are on an on-ramp joining a highway, and a yellow ADDED LANE sign is posted. What does it mean for you?

An ADDED LANE sign means two roads come together and a lane is added, so the entering lane keeps going and you are not required to merge. Still watch for traffic changing lanes.

Q19. The diagonal stripes on a road-work barricade slope down toward the right. Which way should you go?

Barricade stripes slope down toward the side traffic should pass on. Stripes that slope down to the right mean bear to the right.

Q20. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q21. In Oregon, a load that sticks out 4 feet or more beyond the rear of your vehicle must have at its end:

A red flag at least 12 inches square must be shown at the end of any load extending 4 feet or more beyond the rear. In poor visibility, a red light replaces the flag.

Q22. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q23. What should you know about light rail trains running on city streets?

Light rail trains are very quiet, often quieter than buses and cars. Look both ways before you cross the tracks, and never turn in front of a train.

Q24. At what times are deer and other animals most likely to be on or near the road?

Animals such as deer are most active around dawn and dusk. Slow down and watch the sides of the road at those times, especially where animal crossing signs are posted.

Q25. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, so learn your state's distance. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.
