Q1. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q2. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q3. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q4. In Oregon, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q5. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.

Q6. In Oregon, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q7. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q8. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q9. Under Oregon's child passenger restraint law, a child must ride in an approved child safety seat until:

Oregon's child safety seat law uses an age-or-height test, and the child can only move to the adult seat belt alone once it fits them correctly on its own.

Q10. In Oregon, after an emergency vehicle using its lights or siren passes you, you must:

Oregon requires drivers to keep well back from an emergency vehicle even after it has passed, giving responders room to work and travel safely.
