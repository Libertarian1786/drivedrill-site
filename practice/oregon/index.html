Q1. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q2. In Oregon, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practise across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a licence in a weekend.

Q3. In Oregon, after an emergency vehicle using its lights or siren passes you, you must:

Oregon requires drivers to keep well back from an emergency vehicle even after it has passed, giving responders room to work and travel safely.

Q4. When you park facing downhill on a road with no curb, turn your wheels:

With no curb to catch the tire, point the wheels so a rolling vehicle goes off the road rather than into traffic.

Q5. You are being passed by a large truck. You should:

Hold a steady speed and keep right within your lane. Air turbulence can push a small car, so keep both hands on the wheel.

Q6. In Oregon, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q7. Under Oregon's child passenger restraint law, a child must ride in an approved child safety seat until:

Oregon's child safety seat law uses an age-or-height test, and the child can only move to the adult seat belt alone once it fits them correctly on its own.

Q8. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q9. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.

Q10. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.
