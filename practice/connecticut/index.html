Q1. In Connecticut, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q2. Under Connecticut's Vulnerable User Law, drivers passing a pedestrian, bicyclist, wheelchair user or highway worker must allow a clearance of at least:

This clearance is meant to protect vulnerable road users who have little protection if they are struck.

Q3. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q4. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q5. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q6. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q7. Overdriving your headlights means:

Low beams show about 350 feet ahead. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q8. In Connecticut, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q9. In Connecticut, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q10. A yellow sign showing a walking figure means:

Pedestrian crossing warnings ask you to slow down and be ready to stop. People on foot have the right of way in marked and unmarked crosswalks.
