Q1. Under Connecticut's Vulnerable User Law, drivers passing a pedestrian, bicyclist, wheelchair user or highway worker must allow a clearance of at least:

This clearance is meant to protect vulnerable road users who have little protection if they are struck.

Q2. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

States teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q3. An unsecured load in the back of a pickup truck is:

You are responsible for anything that falls off your vehicle. Tie loads down and cover loose material.

Q4. In Connecticut, parking is prohibited within this distance of a stop sign:

This distance keeps the sign visible and gives other drivers room to see and react to traffic at the intersection.

Q5. In Connecticut, a driver age 24 or younger who commits this many moving or suspension violations must complete the DMV operator retraining program:

Repeated violations at a young age are treated as a pattern worth addressing through additional training.

Q6. In Connecticut, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q7. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q8. In Connecticut, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q9. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q10. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q11. An open container of alcohol in the passenger area of the vehicle is:

Open container laws apply to the whole passenger compartment. Keep sealed alcohol in the trunk or a locked area.

Q12. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q13. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q14. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q15. According to Connecticut's driver manual, safely passing another vehicle at 55 mph requires an opening of roughly:

It is easy to misjudge how fast an oncoming vehicle is closing that distance, so drivers should wait until they are sure there is enough room.

Q16. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q17. Unless a sign prohibits it, a right turn on red is allowed:

Right on red requires a full stop first, then yielding to everyone already entitled to go. A NO TURN ON RED sign removes the option entirely.

Q18. In Connecticut, a learner's permit remains valid until you obtain a driver's license, or for what maximum period from its issue date, whichever comes first?

This means a permit holder who does not complete licensing within two years must reapply for a new permit.

Q19. In Connecticut, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q20. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q21. In Connecticut, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q22. In Connecticut, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q23. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q24. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q25. Your vehicle registration must be:

Expired registration is an easy stop for an officer and, in many states, grounds for towing.
