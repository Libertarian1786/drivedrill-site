Q1. A funeral procession with its lights on is passing through an intersection. You should:

Do not cut into or interrupt a procession, even if you have a green light, unless directed by an officer.

Q2. In Connecticut, a driver 25 or older may be required to take the operator retraining program after how many moving-violation convictions?

Drivers 25 and older with three or more convictions for moving or suspension violations must complete the DMV-certified operator retraining program.

Q3. In Connecticut, when a teen gets a learner's permit, a parent or guardian must also:

A parent or legal guardian must take 2 hours of instruction on the laws for drivers under 18 and the dangers of teen driving.

Q4. In Connecticut, a first-time driver over 21 who fails a chemical BAC test after a DUI arrest faces a license suspension of at least:

Connecticut requires both a minimum license suspension and an ignition interlock device as a condition of reinstatement.

Q5. In Connecticut, may you use a hand-held phone while you are stopped at a red light?

The hand-held ban covers a car that is briefly stopped for traffic, road conditions or a sign or signal. You may use the phone once you are safely parked on the side or shoulder.

Q6. The driver ahead of you is signaling a left turn. What is true about passing that driver?

Never pass on the left of a driver who is signaling or making a left turn. If there is room and it is legal, you may pass on the right; otherwise slow down and wait.

Q7. In Connecticut, a pedestrian stands at the curb at a crosswalk and raises a hand toward oncoming traffic. What must you do?

Connecticut law treats a raised hand and arm toward traffic, or a foot, cane, wheelchair or leashed dog moved into the crosswalk entrance, as a pedestrian crossing. You must yield, at marked and unmarked crosswalks.

Q8. At a crash scene, when should you move an injured person?

Do not move an injured person unless it is absolutely necessary, for example to get them away from fire or oncoming traffic. Moving them can make a neck or back injury worse.

Q9. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q10. A bicyclist is riding toward an intersection and has not signaled. What should you assume?

At intersections, assume a bicyclist is going straight unless they signal otherwise. Do not turn across their path.

Q11. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q12. In Connecticut, a 16- or 17-year-old driver breaks the passenger or curfew rules for the first time. What happens to the license?

A first violation of the teen passenger or curfew limits means a 30-day suspension. A second one is punished more harshly.

Q13. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q14. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

Handbooks teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q15. You must slow down for a hazard that drivers behind you cannot see yet. How can you warn them?

Lightly tapping the brake pedal three or four times flashes your brake lights and warns drivers behind you. You can also use your emergency flashers.

Q16. You are about to turn at a corner, but you realize you are not in the proper lane for the turn. What should you do?

Do not turn from the wrong lane or cut across other traffic at the last moment. Drive on to the next intersection, or around the block, and make the turn from the proper lane.

Q17. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q18. In Connecticut, when parking along a roadway, your vehicle may not be parked more than this distance from the curb:

Parking too far from the curb can obstruct traffic and is listed among Connecticut's no-parking violations.

Q19. The minimum vision Connecticut requires for every class of license, with or without glasses, is:

You need at least 20/40 vision, with or without glasses or contact lenses. With only one good eye, that eye must still reach 20/40.

Q20. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q21. A police officer pulls you over and walks up to your car. What should you do with your hands?

Stay in the car and keep both hands where the officer can see them, such as on top of the steering wheel. Do not reach for your license or anything else until the officer asks.

Q22. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q23. In Connecticut, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q24. In Connecticut, who must take the 8-hour Safe Driving Practices course before getting a first license?

Connecticut requires anyone who has never held a license in Connecticut or a valid license from another state to take the course, whatever their age.

Q25. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.
