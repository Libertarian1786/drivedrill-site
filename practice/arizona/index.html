Q1. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q2. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q3. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q4. In Arizona, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it. The licence consequence is administrative and starts before any court date.

Q5. Good drivers look:

Handbooks give figures from 10 to 30 seconds ahead. Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q6. In Arizona, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q7. In Arizona, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q8. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q9. In Arizona, when sharing a lane with a bicycle, you must allow at least how much clearance between your vehicle and the bicycle?

Arizona's guidance for sharing a lane with a bicycle sets a minimum clearance of 3 feet.

Q10. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.
