Q1. Fog becomes so thick that you cannot see well enough to drive safely. What is the safest choice?

When fog is too thick to see the road, pull well off the road and wait for it to lift. Stopping or creeping in a travel lane risks being hit by drivers who cannot see you.

Q2. In Arizona, you plan to turn into a driveway just past an intersection. When should you signal?

The Arizona manual says that if you plan to turn beyond an intersection, do not signal until you are in the intersection, so other drivers do not think you are turning at the corner.

Q3. Under Arizona law, a driver commits aggressive driving by speeding and also doing how many of the listed violations (such as tailgating or unsafe lane changes)?

Arizona's aggressive driving law applies when a driver exceeds the posted speed limit and commits 2 of the listed violations, such as running a signal, passing on the right, unsafe lane changes, tailgating or not yielding to emergency vehicles.

Q4. You need to move across several lanes of traffic on a highway. How should you do it?

Change lanes one at a time: signal, check your mirrors and blind spot, move into the next lane, then repeat. Cutting across several lanes at once surprises other drivers.

Q5. Can you be convicted of driving under the influence if no breath or blood test was done?

You can be arrested and convicted of DUI without a BAC reading. Officers and courts can rely on other evidence, such as how you drove and how you did on field sobriety tests.

Q6. In Arizona, when may you park on a freeway or interstate highway?

The Arizona manual says you may not park on any freeway or interstate highway except in an emergency.

Q7. About how long does it take your body to get rid of the alcohol in one standard drink?

Your body removes alcohol at about one drink per hour, and nothing speeds that up. Coffee, food or a cold shower will not make you sober faster.

Q8. Under Arizona's residency rules, which of these makes you a resident right away, so you need an Arizona license?

Arizona treats you as a resident at once if, among other things, you are registered to vote in the state or you work in Arizona.

Q9. Good drivers look:

Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q10. In Arizona, who is responsible for requiring all vehicle passengers under age 16 to buckle their seat belts?

Arizona's safety belt law places the duty on the vehicle's operator to require every passenger under 16 to buckle up, not just to wear a belt themselves.

Q11. The Arizona manual says that at an intersection with no traffic lights, stop signs or yield signs, you should:

The Arizona manual says to treat an intersection with no traffic lights, stop signs or yield signs as a 4-way stop and come to a complete stop before entering.

Q12. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump the pedal to build pressure. If that does not work, apply the parking brake gradually and shift to lower gears for engine braking, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q13. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q14. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q15. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q16. In Arizona, what does the manual say about crossing a wash that has water running in it?

The Arizona manual says not to cross flooded washes. Water can stall your engine or sweep your vehicle away.

Q17. You see a large puddle across your lane on a wet road. What is the best way to handle it?

Slow down and go around large puddles when it is safe to do so. Standing water can cause hydroplaning, can hide a pothole, and can make your brakes work poorly.

Q18. At some railroad crossings, white lines or a white X pattern are painted on the road on both sides of the tracks. What does that area mark?

These markings show the dynamic envelope, the space a train and its overhang take up as it passes. Never stop inside it; wait behind the stop line until there is room for your whole car past the tracks.

Q19. You are on a narrow mountain road, and a sharp curve ahead blocks your view. What should you do?

Sound your horn as you approach a blind curve on a narrow road so oncoming drivers know you are there. Keep to the right and do not count on them to see or yield to you.

Q20. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q21. In Arizona, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it.

Q22. A left turn on red from a one-way street onto another one-way street is:

Where the law allows this turn, you must first stop completely and yield. Some states and cities ban it, so learn your state's rule and obey any posted sign.

Q23. In Arizona, if you refuse to submit to a blood, breath or urine test after being arrested for DUI, you will:

Refusing the test itself triggers an automatic license suspension, separate from any DUI conviction; a second refusal within the following years brings a longer suspension.

Q24. In Arizona, what does a white curb mean?

In Arizona, a white curb means you may stop only long enough to pick up or drop off passengers.

Q25. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.
