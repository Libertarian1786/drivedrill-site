Q1. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q2. An anti-lock braking system is designed to:

ABS preserves steering control. On gravel or deep snow it may even lengthen the stop, but you can still steer around the hazard.

Q3. In Arizona, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it. The licence consequence is administrative and starts before any court date.

Q4. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

Q5. In Arizona, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q6. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q7. A sign reading NARROW BRIDGE means:

Narrow bridge warnings mean less room than usual. Slow down, keep to your side, and watch for oncoming vehicles that may drift toward the center.

Q8. In Arizona, when sharing a lane with a bicycle, you must allow at least how much clearance between your vehicle and the bicycle?

Arizona's guidance for sharing a lane with a bicycle sets a minimum clearance of 3 feet.

Q9. In Arizona, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q10. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.
