Q1. A red warning light appears on the dashboard while you are driving. You should:

Red generally means stop now: oil pressure, temperature, brakes or charging. Amber usually means service soon.

Q2. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q3. In Arizona, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it.

Q4. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q5. In Arizona, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q6. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q7. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q8. In Arizona, when sharing a lane with a bicycle, you must allow at least how much clearance between your vehicle and the bicycle?

Arizona's guidance for sharing a lane with a bicycle sets a minimum clearance of 3 feet.

Q9. In Arizona, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q10. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.
