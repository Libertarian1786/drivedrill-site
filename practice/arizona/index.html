Q1. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q2. After a crash with injury or significant damage you must usually:

Every state sets a damage threshold and a deadline for reporting. Exchange information at the scene and file the report on time.

Q3. Parking is prohibited:

Never park in a crosswalk, on a sidewalk, in an intersection, or where you would block a curb ramp.

Q4. In Arizona, the law requires you to signal before a turn at least how far ahead?

Arizona law sets a specific distance for signaling a turn so other drivers have time to react.

Q5. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q6. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q7. In Arizona, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q8. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q9. In Arizona, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q10. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q11. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q12. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q13. In Arizona, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q14. In Arizona, a first conviction for failing to show proof of financial responsibility (insurance) results in a license, registration and plate suspension of:

Arizona suspends the license, vehicle registration and plates for 3 months on a first violation, with longer suspensions for repeat violations within 36 months.

Q15. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q16. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q17. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump to build pressure, downshift for engine braking, apply the parking brake gradually, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q18. In Arizona, if no speed limit sign is posted, the default limit approaching a school crosswalk is:

Arizona's unposted statutory speed limits set 15 mph for a school crosswalk, the lowest of the default limits.

Q19. In Arizona, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q20. In Arizona, who is responsible for requiring all vehicle passengers under age 16 to buckle their seat belts?

Arizona's safety belt law places the duty on the vehicle's operator to require every passenger under 16 to buckle up, not just to wear a belt themselves.

Q21. In Arizona, when sharing a lane with a bicycle, you must allow at least how much clearance between your vehicle and the bicycle?

Arizona's guidance for sharing a lane with a bicycle sets a minimum clearance of 3 feet.

Q22. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q23. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q24. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q25. In Arizona, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it.
