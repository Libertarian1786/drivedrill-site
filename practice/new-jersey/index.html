Q1. Counting seconds after the vehicle ahead passes a fixed point is a way to:

Pick a sign or pole; when the vehicle ahead passes it, start counting. If you reach it before your count is up, drop back. States set the minimum anywhere from two to four seconds in good conditions, and every state says to add time for rain, night and large vehicles.

Q2. In New Jersey, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q3. In New Jersey, a Basic license applicant must pass a vision screening showing at least:

New Jersey requires Basic license applicants to show at least 20/50 vision, with or without corrective lenses.

Q4. An unsecured load in the back of a pickup truck is:

You are responsible for anything that falls off your vehicle. Tie loads down and cover loose material.

Q5. In New Jersey, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q6. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.

Q7. Two solid yellow lines between lanes of traffic mean:

Double solid yellow lines prohibit passing in both directions. You may cross them only to turn left into or out of a driveway or street where it is legal.

Q8. A sign reading NARROW BRIDGE means:

Narrow bridge warnings mean less room than usual. Slow down, keep to your side, and watch for oncoming vehicles that may drift toward the center.

Q9. When you park facing uphill next to a curb, you should turn your front wheels:

Uphill with a curb: wheels away from the curb, then let the car settle back so the tire rests against it. Downhill: wheels toward the curb.

Q10. In New Jersey, can a GDL permit or probationary license holder use a hands-free cell phone while driving?

New Jersey bars GDL permit and probationary license holders from using any handheld or hands-free cellular telephone while driving; doing so violates GDL restrictions.
