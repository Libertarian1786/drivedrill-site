Q1. In New Jersey, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q2. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.

Q3. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q4. After a crash with injury or significant damage you must usually:

Every state sets a damage threshold and a deadline for reporting. Exchange information at the scene and file the report on time.

Q5. In New Jersey, a Basic license applicant must pass a vision screening showing at least:

New Jersey requires Basic license applicants to show at least 20/50 vision, with or without corrective lenses.

Q6. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q7. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q8. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q9. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q10. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q11. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q12. A green arrow pointing left means:

A green arrow is a protected turn. Still check the crosswalk for people before you move.

Q13. In New Jersey, a motorist who accumulates how many points in a 24-month period receives a Notice of Scheduled Suspension by mail?

New Jersey mails a Notice of Scheduled Suspension to motorists who accumulate between 12 and 14 points in a 24-month period.

Q14. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q15. In New Jersey, an adult supervising a GDL permit holder's practice driving must be at least:

New Jersey requires the supervising driver for a permit holder to be at least 21 years old and to have held a valid license for at least three years.

Q16. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q17. In New Jersey, drivers should not pass another vehicle within how many feet of a railroad crossing?

The New Jersey manual instructs drivers not to pass other vehicles within 100 feet of a railroad crossing.

Q18. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q19. Your vehicle registration must be:

Expired registration is an easy stop for an officer and, in many states, grounds for towing.

Q20. In New Jersey, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q21. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q22. In New Jersey, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q23. In New Jersey, can a GDL permit or probationary license holder use a hands-free cell phone while driving?

New Jersey bars GDL permit and probationary license holders from using any handheld or hands-free cellular telephone while driving; doing so violates GDL restrictions.

Q24. In New Jersey, headlights must be turned on whenever visibility drops to:

New Jersey law requires headlights whenever visibility is 500 feet or less, in addition to the usual sunset-to-sunrise requirement.

Q25. In New Jersey, how many points can come off a motorist's driving record for each full year without a violation or suspension?

New Jersey subtracts up to three points from a motorist's total for every year the motorist goes without a violation or suspension.
