Free New Jersey permit practice test: 25 questions

25 sample questions from DriveDrill's 498-question New Jersey bank. Pick an answer to see if you got it right, and why.

Q1. Good drivers look:

Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q2. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q3. A pedestrian is crossing in a New Jersey crosswalk in front of you. When may you move on?

Wait until the pedestrian has safely cleared your lane and the next lane going in your direction.

Q4. In a New Jersey car with a back seat, where must a child under 8 who is shorter than 57 inches ride?

New Jersey law says children under eight who are less than 57 inches tall must ride in a rear seat. Only a vehicle with no rear seat allows otherwise.

Q5. You come to a flooded road, and you know a different way to get where you are going. What should you do?

Turn around and take another route when a road ahead is flooded. You cannot tell how deep the water is or whether the road is still there under it, and a vehicle can be swept away.

Q6. You are on an on-ramp joining a highway, and a yellow ADDED LANE sign is posted. What does it mean for you?

An ADDED LANE sign means two roads come together and a lane is added, so the entering lane keeps going and you are not required to merge. Still watch for traffic changing lanes.

Q7. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.

Q8. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q9. What does New Jersey's Maggie's Law make illegal?

Maggie's Law (2003) makes it illegal to knowingly drive while impaired by lack of sleep and treats it as recklessness under the vehicular homicide law.

Q10. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q11. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q12. An open container of alcohol in the passenger area of the vehicle is:

Open container laws apply to the whole passenger compartment. Keep sealed alcohol in the trunk or a locked area.

Q13. A pedestrian starts across the street, and your light turns green before they reach the other side. What must you do?

If the light changes after a pedestrian has entered the crosswalk, the pedestrian still has the right-of-way. Wait until they have finished crossing before you go.

Q14. Compared with daylight, how does darkness change when you can see an object on the road?

At night you must be closer to an object than in daylight before you can see it. Slow down so you still have time to react when you do see it.

Q15. What is a reversible lane?

Reversible lanes carry traffic one way at some times and the other way at other times, usually to handle rush-hour traffic. Check the signs and lane-use signals before you enter one.

Q16. How does driving faster change what you can see?

The faster you drive, the narrower your field of vision becomes, so hazards at the sides of the road are harder to notice. Slow down where people or cars may come from the side.

Q17. When the lights at a railroad crossing start to flash, about how much warning do you usually have before the train arrives?

Crossing signals are set to give at least about 20 seconds of warning before the train reaches the crossing. When the lights start flashing, stop; there is not enough time to try to beat the train.

Q18. In New Jersey, what color lights do volunteer fire and rescue members use on their own cars when answering an emergency call?

Volunteer fire and rescue squad members, with emergency vehicle identification, use blue lights when responding to an emergency call.

Q19. At a New Jersey stop sign, you must come to a complete stop within how far of the nearest crosswalk or stop line?

A motorist approaching a stop sign must come to a complete stop within five feet of the nearest crosswalk or stop line.

Q20. In New Jersey, the speed limit in school zones and in business or residential districts is:

New Jersey's speed table sets 25 mph for school zones and business or residential districts.

Q21. Breaking New Jersey's child passenger restraint law brings a fine of:

Violating the child restraint law carries a fine of not less than $50 nor more than $75.

Q22. New Jersey law limits leaving your engine idling to:

New Jersey requires motorists to restrict idling to three minutes or less; owners can be fined $250 to $1,000 per violation.

Q23. A New Jersey bus signals to pull back into traffic from a bus stop. What must you do?

State law requires all non-emergency vehicles to yield to buses re-entering traffic; the fine is $50 to $200 or up to 15 days in jail.

Q24. In New Jersey, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent, guardian or supervising driver age 21 or older certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q25. What is the most common cause of skids?

Most skids happen because drivers go too fast for the road and weather conditions. Slowing down before you reach slippery spots prevents most of them.
