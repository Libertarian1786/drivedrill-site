Q1. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q2. A lane-use signal hanging over a highway lane shows a steady red X. What does it mean?

Lane-use signals hang over lanes whose use can change, such as reversible lanes. A steady red X means the lane is closed to you: do not drive in it.

Q3. A Michigan driver who builds up 12 or more points within two years must:

Twelve or more points within two years means a driver assessment reexamination, which can put the license at risk.

Q4. In Michigan, a car may enter a bike lane to turn right only when it is within how far of the corner or driveway?

Drivers may not drive, pass or turn right in a bike lane unless they are within 200 feet of a corner or driveway, and they must never park in one.

Q5. In Michigan, unless a sign shows a different limit, the general speed limit on limited-access freeways is:

Michigan's general freeway limit is 70 mph. Some rural freeway stretches are posted at 75 mph, and where they are, the signs say so. The posted sign always governs.

Q6. What shape is a Michigan state highway route marker?

Michigan state highway signs are diamond-shaped with black text on a white background.

Q7. A bicyclist is riding at the right edge of your lane while an oncoming truck approaches. What is usually the best way to handle both?

When two hazards meet you at the same time, adjust your speed so you deal with one danger at a time. For example, slow down and let the truck pass before you pass the bicyclist.

Q8. On a road with no sidewalk, where should people walk?

Where there is no sidewalk, pedestrians should walk on the left side or shoulder, facing traffic, so they can see what is coming. Drivers should expect people walking there, especially at night.

Q9. You are turning left on a green arrow, and the arrow changes to a steady yellow arrow. What does that mean?

A steady yellow arrow means the protected turning time is ending and the signal is about to change. Stop if you can do so safely; if you are already in the intersection, finish the turn with care.

Q10. How soon after heavy rain can a flash flood develop?

A flash flood can build within minutes or a few hours of heavy rain, often with little warning. Stay alert for water on roads, bridges and low areas during and after a downpour.

Q11. Michigan's driving skills test has three sections. What happens if you fail one?

Each section must be passed in sequence; if you fail any section the test ends, and on your next attempt you start over from the beginning.

Q12. You are starting your car from a stop on a snow-covered road. What should you do?

Start slowly and accelerate smoothly on snow, because starting too fast makes the drive wheels spin. Spinning tires lose even more grip.

Q13. In Michigan, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q14. Michigan law requires headlights whenever you cannot see clearly for more than:

Michigan requires headlights whenever it is difficult to see more than 500 feet away. In rain, use low beams.

Q15. Fluorescent yellow-green warning signs usually warn you about:

Fluorescent yellow-green is used for warning signs about schools, school buses, pedestrians and bicycles, so you notice where these people may be.

Q16. What is the most common cause of skids?

Most skids happen because drivers go too fast for the road and weather conditions. Slowing down before you reach slippery spots prevents most of them.

Q17. A deer suddenly steps into the road ahead of you. What is usually the safest choice?

Swerving to miss an animal can send you into oncoming traffic or off the road, which is often worse than hitting it. Brake firmly, stay in control and keep your lane.

Q18. A new Michigan resident has a valid license from another U.S. state. Which tests must they pass to get a Michigan license?

Drivers coming from another U.S. state, Canada, Germany or South Korea need no driving skills or written test.

Q19. A police officer pulls you over at night. What should you do after you stop?

At night, turn on the inside light so the officer can see into your car. Stay inside with your hands in view unless you are told to get out.

Q20. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q21. About how long does it take your body to get rid of the alcohol in one standard drink?

Your body removes alcohol at about one drink per hour, and nothing speeds that up. Coffee, food or a cold shower will not make you sober faster.

Q22. A motorcyclist has been hurt in a crash. What should you do about the rider's helmet?

Do not remove an injured rider's helmet. If the neck or spine is hurt, moving the head can cause more damage, so wait for trained help.

Q23. A motorcyclist ahead of you slows down, but no brake light comes on. Why can that happen?

Motorcyclists often slow down by downshifting or rolling off the throttle, which does not turn on the brake light. Leave extra space and watch the rider, not just the light.

Q24. In Michigan, when may you pass a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing without stopping first?

Michigan excuses oncoming traffic only on a road split into two roadways by a raised median, a physical barrier or a divider built to keep traffic from crossing, and not where there is a crosswalk. A painted line or flat center lane is not a divider, so traffic in both directions must stop however many lanes the road has. Traffic behind the bus always stops.

Q25. In Michigan, it is illegal to leave a child alone in a vehicle, if there is any risk of harm, when the child is under:

Michigan makes it illegal to leave a child under 6 alone in a vehicle if there is any risk of harm.
