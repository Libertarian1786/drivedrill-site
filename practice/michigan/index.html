Q1. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q2. In Michigan, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q3. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q4. In Michigan, when parking along a curb, your vehicle must not be parked:

Michigan law says the curb should never be more than a foot (12 inches) from your parked vehicle.

Q5. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q6. In Michigan, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q7. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q8. In Michigan, you may not park within how many feet of the nearest rail of a railroad crossing?

Michigan prohibits parking within 50 feet of the nearest rail of a railroad crossing.

Q9. After a crash with injury or significant damage you must usually:

Every state sets a damage threshold and a deadline for reporting. Exchange information at the scene and file the report on time.

Q10. In a work zone you should:

Rear-end collisions are the most common work-zone crash. More space is what prevents them.
