Q1. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q2. In Tennessee, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q3. In Tennessee, the handbook says reading or sending a text takes your eyes off the road for a minimum of:

Tennessee's manual notes that reading or sending a text takes your eyes off the road for a minimum of 5 seconds.

Q4. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q5. In Tennessee, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q6. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q7. In Tennessee, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q8. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q9. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q10. Worn tire tread is dangerous mainly because it:

Tread channels water away. Once it is shallow, wet grip falls off sharply. Check with a tread gauge or the built-in wear bars.
