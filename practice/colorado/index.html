Q1. In Colorado, before entering, exiting or driving in a roundabout, drivers must yield the right-of-way to:

Colorado law singles out large vehicles like trucks, buses, emergency vehicles and RVs for priority in roundabouts because they need more room to maneuver.

Q2. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.

Q3. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle traveling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q4. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q5. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q6. In Colorado, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q7. In Colorado, if you refuse a chemical test after being lawfully arrested for impaired driving, your license is revoked for:

Colorado's express consent law means refusing a blood, breath, saliva or urine test results in a one-year revocation and a two-year ignition interlock requirement.

Q8. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q9. In Colorado's driver handbook, the average rate at which blood alcohol concentration drops over time is about:

Only time removes alcohol from the body, and the handbook gives this average elimination rate as a reference.

Q10. In Colorado, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q11. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q12. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q13. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q14. In Colorado, a first conviction for Driving While Ability Impaired (DWAI) applies at a blood alcohol content of:

Colorado's DWAI standard applies at a lower blood alcohol level than a full DUI conviction.

Q15. A wide white line across your lane at an intersection is:

Stop at the line, not past it. If your view is blocked once stopped, edge forward slowly only after stopping at the line.

Q16. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q17. In Colorado, wearing earphones that cover all or part of both ears while driving is:

Colorado's handbook states plainly that you may not operate a motor vehicle while wearing earphones.

Q18. In Colorado, when parallel parking where it is permitted, your vehicle must be within what distance of the curb?

Parking closer to the curb keeps traffic from being blocked or slowed by your vehicle.

Q19. Adjusting your side mirrors slightly outward compared with the usual setting:

Wider mirror angles shrink the blind spot, but a head check before changing lanes is still the move that catches what mirrors miss.

Q20. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q21. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q22. Speeding fines in a work zone are usually:

Most states increase work-zone fines and add penalties when workers are present, whether or not the crew is visible at that moment.

Q23. In Colorado, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q24. Colorado law requires you to turn on your headlights from sunset to sunrise, and also whenever visibility drops below:

Low visibility conditions can make headlights necessary even outside the normal sunset-to-sunrise window.

Q25. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.
