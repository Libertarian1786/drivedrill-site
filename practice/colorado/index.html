Q1. In Colorado, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q2. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q3. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q4. In Colorado, a first conviction for Driving While Ability Impaired (DWAI) applies at a blood alcohol content of:

Colorado's DWAI standard applies at a lower blood alcohol level than a full DUI conviction.

Q5. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q6. In Colorado, when parallel parking where it is permitted, your vehicle must be within what distance of the curb?

Parking closer to the curb keeps traffic from being blocked or slowed by your vehicle.

Q7. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q8. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q9. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q10. In Colorado, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.
