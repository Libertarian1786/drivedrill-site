Q1. You are on a road that ends at a T intersection with no signs. You should:

At a T intersection the road that ends yields to the road that continues.

Q2. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q3. In Colorado, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q4. In Colorado, if your high beams are on, you must dim them before coming within this distance of an oncoming vehicle:

Failing to dim high beams in time can blind an oncoming driver with glare.

Q5. In Colorado, a first conviction for Driving While Ability Impaired (DWAI) applies at a blood alcohol content of:

Colorado's DWAI standard applies at a lower blood alcohol level than a full DUI conviction.

Q6. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q7. Grooves cut into the shoulder that make a loud noise when you drive over them are there to:

Rumble strips are a drowsiness and drift warning. If you hit one, ease back into the lane smoothly and consider stopping to rest.

Q8. In Colorado, if you cannot safely move out of the lane next to a stopped emergency vehicle, you must slow to:

Changing lanes is the first choice. The speed rule is what applies when traffic or the road makes a lane change unsafe.

Q9. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q10. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.
