Free Texas permit practice test: 25 questions

25 sample questions from DriveDrill's 544-question Texas bank. Pick an answer to see if you got it right, and why.

Q1. In Texas, stopping or parking on the street side of a car already parked at the curb (double parking) is:

The Texas handbook says not to park, stop or stand on the road side of any vehicle stopped or parked at the edge or curb of a street.

Q2. On a Texas frontage road beside a controlled-access highway, who has the right-of-way at a ramp?

The Texas handbook says the frontage-road driver must yield to vehicles entering the frontage road from the highway and to vehicles leaving it to enter the highway.

Q3. When a long vehicle turns a corner, how do its rear wheels move compared with its front wheels?

In a turn, the rear wheels follow a shorter path than the front wheels, and the longer the vehicle, the bigger the difference. That is why trucks swing wide and why you should not squeeze in beside a turning truck.

Q4. A snowplow is working on the road ahead of you. What is the best choice?

Stay well back from a working snowplow, because it throws sand and snow and may stop or back up. Never pass it on the shoulder side.

Q5. In Texas, a bicycle ridden at night must have a white front lamp visible from at least:

The Texas handbook says a bicycle used at night needs a front lamp with white light visible from at least 500 feet and a red rear reflector visible from 50 to 300 feet.

Q6. You are starting your car from a stop on a snow-covered road. What should you do?

Start slowly and accelerate smoothly on snow, because starting too fast makes the drive wheels spin. Spinning tires lose even more grip.

Q7. When following a motorcycle in Texas, allow a following distance of at least:

The slightest contact can spill a rider. The extra seconds give the motorcyclist time to maneuver or stop in an emergency.

Q8. A vehicle is coming straight at you in your lane. What should you do?

If a vehicle comes at you in your lane, sound your horn and flash your lights, brake, and steer to the right. Do not swerve left, because the other driver may move back into their own lane.

Q9. Under Texas rules, nothing on your car may stick out beyond its body or fenders by more than:

The Texas handbook lists anything extending more than 3 inches beyond the left side or 6 inches beyond the right side of the body, running board or fenders as not allowed.

Q10. A Texas learner license issued to a minor expires:

The Texas handbook says a learner license expires on the minor's 18th birthday.

Q11. You leave a brightly lit gas station and pull onto a dark road. What should you do?

Your eyes need time to adjust after bright light. Drive slowly until they do, so you can see the road and anything on it.

Q12. A deer suddenly steps into the road ahead of you. What is usually the safest choice?

Swerving to miss an animal can send you into oncoming traffic or off the road, which is often worse than hitting it. Brake firmly, stay in control and keep your lane.

Q13. In Texas, when may you pass on the right using a paved shoulder?

The Texas handbook says you may pass on a paved shoulder when the vehicle you are passing is slowing or stopped on the main road, disabled, or preparing to turn left. Never pass by driving off the pavement.

Q14. Texas's minimum liability insurance is:

The Texas handbook gives the minimum as $30,000 for injury or death of one person, $60,000 for two or more people, and $25,000 for property damage.

Q15. A bicyclist is riding toward an intersection and has not signaled. What should you assume?

At intersections, assume a bicyclist is going straight unless they signal otherwise. Do not turn across their path.

Q16. Is it safe to leave a child or pet in a parked car on a warm day if a window is cracked open?

A parked car heats up fast, even with a window slightly open, and children and pets can die from the heat. Never leave them alone in a parked car.

Q17. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle traveling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q18. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is safer, and the law often requires it.

Q19. When it rains hard enough that your windshield wipers are on continuously, you should also:

Wipers on, lights on: it helps other drivers see you as much as it helps your own vision, and the law often requires it.

Q20. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q21. In Texas, which cars must have an exhaust emission system to reduce air pollution?

The Texas handbook says all 1968 or later models must have an exhaust emission system to help reduce air pollution.

Q22. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q23. A police officer pulls you over at night. What should you do after you stop?

At night, turn on the inside light so the officer can see into your car. Stay inside with your hands in view unless you are told to get out.

Q24. The driver ahead of you is signaling a left turn. What is true about passing that driver?

Never pass on the left of a driver who is signaling or making a left turn. If there is room and it is legal, you may pass on the right; otherwise slow down and wait.

Q25. In Texas, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.
