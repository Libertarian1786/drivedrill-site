Q1. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q2. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q3. After a crash in Texas you must notify law enforcement immediately if:

Stop, help the injured, exchange information, and stay until you have met those duties. Leaving the scene is a criminal offense.

Q4. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q5. The minimum age to apply for a Texas learner license is:

At 15 you may apply once you have completed the first 6 hours of driver education classroom instruction (or all 24 hours) and have your driver education certificate (DE-964). A provisional license follows at 16.

Q6. A Texas driver under 18 may use a wireless device while driving:

This is stricter than the all-driver texting ban. For a driver under 18 even a hands-free call is illegal unless it is an emergency.

Q7. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q8. In Texas, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q9. A flagger in a work zone holds up a sign reading SLOW. You should:

Flaggers have the same authority as signs and signals in a work zone. Follow their direction and watch for workers on foot.

Q10. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.
