Q1. A Texas driver under 18 may use a wireless device while driving:

This is stricter than the all-driver texting ban. For a driver under 18 even a hands-free call is illegal unless it is an emergency.

Q2. The minimum age to apply for a Texas learner license is:

At 15 you may apply once you have completed the first 6 hours of driver education classroom instruction (or all 24 hours) and have your driver education certificate (DE-964). A provisional license follows at 16.

Q3. During the first 12 months with a Texas provisional license, a driver under 18 may not:

The same period prohibits driving between midnight and 5 a.m. except for work, school or a medical emergency, and bans all wireless device use.

Q4. An anti-lock braking system is designed to:

ABS preserves steering control. On gravel or deep snow it may even lengthen the stop, but you can still steer around the hazard.

Q5. After a crash in Texas you must notify law enforcement immediately if:

Stop, help the injured, exchange information, and stay until you have met those duties. Leaving the scene is a criminal offense.

Q6. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q7. Texas does not allow you to follow a fire truck answering an alarm, or an ambulance with its lights on, any closer than:

500 feet keeps you clear of sudden stops and of the emergency scene itself.

Q8. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q9. Using a hand-held phone in a Texas school crossing zone is:

Hand-held use in an active school zone is prohibited, and drivers under 18 may not use a wireless device at all.

Q10. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q11. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q12. In Texas, a child must be in a child safety seat system until:

Under 8 and shorter than 4 feet 9 inches means a car seat or booster, in a seat that fits the child's size.

Q13. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q14. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.

Q15. In Texas, a driver under 21 may be charged with driving under the influence of alcohol by a minor with:

Texas is a true zero tolerance state for minors: any detectable alcohol is enough, and the license suspension follows the first offense.

Q16. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q17. Your hood flies open and blocks your view. You should:

Use the gap under the hood or the side window, signal, slow gradually and stop off the road.

Q18. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q19. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q20. In Texas you may not park, occupied or not, within how many feet of the nearest rail of a railroad crossing?

A parked vehicle near the tracks can block a train crew's view and trap other drivers in the crossing.

Q21. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q22. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q23. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q24. The Texas handbook warns that twelve inches of moving water is enough to:

Six inches reaches the bottom of most cars, twelve floats many, and two feet of rushing water carries away pickups and SUVs. Turn around.

Q25. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.
