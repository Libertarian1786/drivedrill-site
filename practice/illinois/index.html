Q1. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q2. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q3. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q4. Worn tire tread is dangerous mainly because it:

Tread channels water away. Once it is shallow, wet grip falls off sharply. Check with a tread gauge or the built-in wear bars.

Q5. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q6. Illinois requires you to notify the Secretary of State of an address change within:

You can do it at a DMV office, online at ilsos.gov, or by mail.

Q7. An Illinois driver aged 15 to 17 must hold an instruction permit for at least:

The permit itself is valid for 24 months.

Q8. The minimum age for an Illinois instruction permit is:

Applicants aged 15 to 17 must be enrolled in an approved driver education course and hold the permit for at least nine months.

Q9. Scott's Law, Illinois' move over law, requires you to:

Illinois penalties for violating Scott's Law are severe, including large fines and license suspension when a violation causes injury.

Q10. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.
