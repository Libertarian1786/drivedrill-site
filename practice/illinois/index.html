Q1. The Illinois school zone speed limit, on school days between 6:30 a.m. and 4 p.m. when children are present and signs are posted, is:

Outside those hours, or with no children present, the ordinary posted limit applies.

Q2. A police officer is directing traffic at an intersection where the light is green. You should:

Directions from an officer or flagger override signs and signals every time.

Q3. A steady yellow traffic light means:

Yellow is the warning that red is next. Stop if you can do it safely; if you are already so close that stopping would be abrupt or unsafe, continue through with care.

Q4. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q5. The minimum age for an Illinois instruction permit is:

Applicants aged 15 to 17 must be enrolled in an approved driver education course and hold the permit for at least nine months.

Q6. In Illinois, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q7. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

States teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q8. An Illinois driver under 18 may not drive between:

The curfew starts an hour later on Friday and Saturday. Exceptions cover work, school activities and emergencies, and local curfews may be stricter.

Q9. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q10. A standard drink of beer, wine and distilled spirits:

A 12 oz beer, a 5 oz glass of wine and 1.5 oz of spirits each contain roughly the same alcohol, which is why counting drinks does not make you safe to drive.
