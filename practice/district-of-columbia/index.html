Q1. You are being passed by a large truck. You should:

Hold a steady speed and keep right within your lane. Air turbulence can push a small car, so keep both hands on the wheel.

Q2. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q3. Absent a posted sign, District of Columbia law caps a school zone's speed at:

DC's manual sets the unposted school zone speed limit at 15 miles per hour.

Q4. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q5. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q6. In the District of Columbia, a driver with a provisional license may carry:

The learner stage allows no other passengers. The provisional stage relaxes that to one experienced adult in front plus close family.

Q7. In the District of Columbia, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q8. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q9. In the District of Columbia, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q10. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.
