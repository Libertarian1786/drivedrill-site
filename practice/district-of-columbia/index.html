Q1. Absent a posted sign, District of Columbia law caps a school zone's speed at:

DC's manual sets the unposted school zone speed limit at 15 miles per hour.

Q2. At an intersection with no signs or signals at all, you should:

Uncontrolled intersections still have rules: yield to traffic already in the intersection, and to the vehicle on your right when you arrive together.

Q3. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q4. In the District of Columbia, a driver with a provisional license may carry:

The learner stage allows no other passengers. The provisional stage relaxes that to one experienced adult in front plus close family.

Q5. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q6. In the District of Columbia, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q7. Turning left from a one-way street onto another one-way street, you should:

Left turns between one-way streets are made from the leftmost lane into the leftmost lane.

Q8. In the District of Columbia, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q9. You are being passed by a large truck. You should:

Hold a steady speed and keep right within your lane. Air turbulence can push a small car, so keep both hands on the wheel.

Q10. A lane in the middle of the road marked with a solid and a broken yellow line on each side is:

The two-way left turn lane is for turning left from either direction and for turning left onto the road. Never drive along it to travel or to pass.
