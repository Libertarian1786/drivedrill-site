Q1. In the District of Columbia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q2. You need to move across several lanes of traffic on a highway. How should you do it?

Change lanes one at a time: signal, check your mirrors and blind spot, move into the next lane, then repeat. Cutting across several lanes at once surprises other drivers.

Q3. In the District of Columbia, how close may you follow a fire truck that is answering an emergency?

Stay at least 500 feet behind fire apparatus on an emergency run.

Q4. What is a reversible lane?

Reversible lanes carry traffic one way at some times and the other way at other times, usually to handle rush-hour traffic. Check the signs and lane-use signals before you enter one.

Q5. In the District of Columbia, before advancing from a provisional license to a full driver license, an applicant must complete:

DC's manual requires certifying at least 10 hours of night driving experience, accompanied by a licensed driver 21 or older, before advancing to a full driver license.

Q6. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q7. In the District of Columbia, you must turn on your headlights:

D.C. also requires headlights whenever you cannot clearly see people or vehicles 500 feet away.

Q8. In the District of Columbia, when may you cancel the insurance on a registered car you no longer drive?

Keep insurance on a car as long as it is registered. Surrender the tags to the DMV before you cancel the policy.

Q9. Why do drivers often misjudge an approaching large truck?

Because of its large size, a truck often appears to be going slower than it really is. Allow more time than you think before you pull out in front of one.

Q10. In the District of Columbia, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q11. In the District of Columbia, when may you use your horn in a posted Quiet Zone?

Never sound the horn in a posted Quiet Zone unless there is an emergency.

Q12. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q13. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q14. In the District of Columbia, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q15. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q16. Where should a child in a rear-facing car seat never ride?

An inflating front airbag can strike a rear-facing car seat and seriously injure or kill the child. A rear-facing seat belongs in the back seat.

Q17. You have just driven through deep water, and your brakes feel weak. How can you dry them?

After driving through water, test your brakes. If they are wet, drive slowly in a low gear and press the brake pedal lightly until they work normally again.

Q18. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q19. You must slow down for a hazard that drivers behind you cannot see yet. How can you warn them?

Lightly tapping the brake pedal three or four times flashes your brake lights and warns drivers behind you. You can also use your emergency flashers.

Q20. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q21. In the District of Columbia, you may not stand or park within how many feet of a driveway or alley?

Leave driveways and alleys clear so people can get in and out.

Q22. A large truck is backing up into a loading dock across your path. What should you do?

Truck drivers have a large blind spot behind the trailer and may not see you at all. Never cross behind a truck that is backing up; wait until it is done.

Q23. A pedestrian hybrid beacon over a mid-block crosswalk shows two steady red lights. What must you do?

A pedestrian hybrid beacon is dark until someone pushes the crossing button. Steady red means stop and stay stopped while pedestrians cross; only when it changes to flashing red may you go once your path is clear.

Q24. At some railroad crossings, white lines or a white X pattern are painted on the road on both sides of the tracks. What does that area mark?

These markings show the dynamic envelope, the space a train and its overhang take up as it passes. Never stop inside it; wait behind the stop line until there is room for your whole car past the tracks.

Q25. In the District of Columbia, when a snow emergency is declared, what must cars on snow emergency routes have?

On designated snow emergency routes during a declared snow emergency, cars must use chains, snow tires or radial tires.
