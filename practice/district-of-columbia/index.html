Q1. In the District of Columbia, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q2. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q3. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q4. In the District of Columbia, when a school vehicle has stopped with its alternating flashing red lights on, you must stop:

DC's manual requires all vehicles to stop at least 20 feet from the front or rear of a stopped school vehicle operating its alternating flashing red lights.

Q5. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q6. In the District of Columbia, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it.

Q7. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q8. In the District of Columbia, before turning you must signal continuously for at least:

DC law requires a turn signal, arm, or hand signal continuously for at least 100 feet before turning.

Q9. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q10. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.
