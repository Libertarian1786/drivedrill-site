Q1. A passenger in your car throws trash out the window in Pennsylvania. Who can be fined?

As the driver, you can be fined for litter thrown from your vehicle, even when a passenger threw it. If the driver is not identified, the vehicle's registered owner can be held responsible.

Q2. Pennsylvania law requires headlights whenever you cannot see people or vehicles on the highway for a distance of:

Pennsylvania law requires headlights when you cannot see people or vehicles on the highway 1,000 feet ahead. That applies because of poor light or bad weather such as rain, snow, fog or smoke.

Q3. Why should you leave extra room when you stop behind a horse-drawn vehicle in Pennsylvania?

The manual warns that after stopping, horse-drawn vehicles often roll backward, so leave plenty of space.

Q4. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q5. In Pennsylvania, a permit driver under 18 must wait at least how many months after getting the permit before taking the road test?

A permit driver under 18 must wait 6 months from the permit issue date before taking the road test. The 65 logged driving hours are required as well.

Q6. In Pennsylvania, if you hand your license to PennDOT before the date in your suspension notice, the suspension:

A suspension begins on the effective date in the PennDOT notice, no matter when you give up the license. Handing it in early does not start the suspension early.

Q7. In Pennsylvania, a general-impairment DUI that also involves a crash with injury, death or property damage is sentenced under:

A general-impairment DUI that causes a crash with injury, death or property damage is sentenced under the high rate penalties, the middle tier. Those are the same penalties used for a blood alcohol level of .10 to .159.

Q8. A highway has a posted minimum speed, but you cannot safely drive that fast. What should you do?

If you cannot or do not want to drive at or above the posted minimum speed, do not use that highway. Take another route.

Q9. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

Q10. You are stranded in a snowstorm and run the engine for heat. What should you check to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning?

Keep the tailpipe clear of snow whenever the engine is running, because a blocked pipe can send carbon monoxide into the car. Run the engine only for short periods, with a window open slightly.

Q11. If police do not investigate a reportable Pennsylvania crash, you must send form AA-600 to PennDOT within:

The Driver's Accident Report form is your own record of the crash, and the deadline is short.

Q12. When you glance over your shoulder to check your blind spot, what should you avoid?

When you check your blind spot, turn only your head. Turning your whole body or the steering wheel can pull the car out of its lane.

Q13. A police officer pulls you over at night. What should you do after you stop?

At night, turn on the inside light so the officer can see into your car. Stay inside with your hands in view unless you are told to get out.

Q14. In Pennsylvania, when may you stop or park a vehicle on a highway bridge or in a highway tunnel?

In Pennsylvania you may not stop, stand or park on a bridge or other elevated structure, or in a highway tunnel, except in an emergency or when a police officer tells you to. Flashers, staying in the car or hugging the wall do not make it legal.

Q15. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.

Q16. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q17. At a railroad crossing, a small blue sign shows a phone number and a crossing ID number. When should you use it?

The blue Emergency Notification System (ENS) sign gives the railroad's emergency number and the crossing's ID. Call it if a vehicle is stalled on the tracks or the signals or gates are not working, so trains can be warned; call 911 too if anyone is in danger.

Q18. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.

Q19. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q20. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q21. Which of these is an emergency vehicle under Pennsylvania's manual when it uses red lights and a siren?

Emergency vehicles include fire department vehicles, police cars, ambulances, blood delivery vehicles and specially equipped rescue vehicles.

Q22. In Pennsylvania, seat belt use is required for:

Children under 2 must ride in a rear-facing seat, and under 4 in an approved restraint anywhere in the vehicle.

Q23. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q24. A deer suddenly steps into the road ahead of you. What is usually the safest choice?

Swerving to miss an animal can send you into oncoming traffic or off the road, which is often worse than hitting it. Brake firmly, stay in control and keep your lane.

Q25. On an ice- or snow-covered road, how does driving downhill change the risk?

Going downhill on a slippery road makes a skid more likely and makes it harder to slow the vehicle. Slow down before you start down the hill and keep your speed low all the way down.
