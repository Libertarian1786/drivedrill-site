Q1. In Pennsylvania, after you move or change your name, you must notify PennDOT within:

The rule applies even when you are moving out of Pennsylvania.

Q2. The maximum speed limit posted on Pennsylvania highways is:

Most interstates are posted at 65 or 70 mph. Speed in a work zone is enforced with automated cameras on some corridors.

Q3. In a Pennsylvania school zone, while the yellow signals are flashing, you must drive no faster than:

Exceeding the school-zone limit also adds three points to your record.

Q4. Refusing chemical testing after a DUI arrest in Pennsylvania results in:

The refusal suspension is imposed by PennDOT regardless of what happens in court, and a second refusal brings 18 months.

Q5. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q6. A steady red arrow means:

A red arrow prohibits that movement entirely. Wait for a green or flashing yellow arrow, even if no traffic is coming.

Q7. In Pennsylvania, a driver under 21 can be charged with DUI at a blood alcohol level of:

The under-21 threshold is 0.02%, and a conviction brings a license suspension plus mandatory alcohol highway safety school.

Q8. Pennsylvania law requires headlights to be on:

Daytime running lights are not enough on their own, because they do not switch on the tail lights.

Q9. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q10. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q11. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.

Q12. When passing a bicyclist in Pennsylvania you must leave at least:

If you cannot leave four feet safely, wait behind the cyclist until you can.

Q13. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q14. Your car stalls on a busy highway. You should:

Off the road, hazards on, and out of the traffic side of the car. Standing beside a disabled vehicle on the traffic side is one of the most dangerous places on a highway.

Q15. In Pennsylvania, seat belt use is required for:

Children under 2 must ride in a rear-facing seat, and under 4 in an approved restraint anywhere in the vehicle.

Q16. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.

Q17. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q18. When a train is coming and you must stop at a railroad crossing, stop no closer to the nearest rail than:

Fifteen feet keeps you clear of the train's overhang and of the gate arm. Most states also say to stop no farther than 50 feet back, so you can still see the tracks.

Q19. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

States teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q20. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q21. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q22. Under Pennsylvania's Act 18, using a hand-held mobile device while driving is:

Pennsylvania moved to a hands-free standard for all drivers. Texting was already banned as a primary offense.

Q23. When parking along a curb in Pennsylvania, your vehicle may be no more than:

Parking as close as possible keeps your car out of the travel lane and your doors out of passing traffic.

Q24. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q25. Before backing out of a parking space you should:

Cameras miss low objects and fast-moving children. Look behind the vehicle before you get in, then back slowly while checking all around.
