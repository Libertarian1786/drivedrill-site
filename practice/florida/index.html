Q1. Speeding fines in a work zone are usually:

Most states increase work-zone fines and add penalties when workers are present, whether or not the crew is visible at that moment.

Q2. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q3. Before taking the Florida driving skills test, a learner must hold the permit for:

The 50 hours of supervised driving, 10 of them after dark, are certified by a parent or guardian.

Q4. Beginning October 1, 2026, you must report a Florida crash to law enforcement immediately if:

Florida's SB 488 (Chapter 2026-39) raised the property-damage threshold from $500 to $2,000 starting October 1, 2026. When a crash damages property but needs no law enforcement report, the driver must send a written report to the department within 10 days.

Q5. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q6. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q7. Florida requires every registered vehicle to carry:

Florida is a no-fault state: PIP pays your own medical costs regardless of fault, and PDL pays for damage you cause to others' property.

Q8. The standard Florida driver license for a car is:

Class E covers any non-commercial vehicle under 26,001 pounds. Commercial classes are A, B and C.

Q9. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q10. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.
