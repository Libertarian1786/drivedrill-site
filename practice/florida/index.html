Free Florida permit practice test: 25 questions

25 sample questions from DriveDrill's 553-question Florida bank. Pick an answer to see if you got it right, and why.

Q1. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q2. On a Florida road (not a bike path), bicyclists riding together may ride no more than:

Florida law says bicyclists on a roadway or in a bike lane may not ride more than two abreast, except on a bicycle path.

Q3. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q4. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

Handbooks teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q5. To ride a moped in Florida, you need:

Florida's handbook says moped operators must have a Class E driver license. A motorcycle endorsement is not required.

Q6. How soon after heavy rain can a flash flood develop?

A flash flood can build within minutes or a few hours of heavy rain, often with little warning. Stay alert for water on roads, bridges and low areas during and after a downpour.

Q7. A Florida driver under 21 is caught for the first time with a BAC of 0.02 or higher. The license is suspended for:

Florida law suspends a driver under 21 for 6 months for a first BAC of 0.02 or higher, and for 1 year if it happened before.

Q8. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q9. In Florida, bicycle riders and passengers must wear a helmet if they are under:

Florida law requires bicycle riders and passengers under 16 to wear a helmet that meets federal safety standards.

Q10. You have just driven through deep water, and your brakes feel weak. How can you dry them?

After driving through water, test your brakes. If they are wet, drive slowly in a low gear and press the brake pedal lightly until they work normally again.

Q11. Your car has a problem and you need to stop. Which place should you avoid?

Never stop just over a hill or just around a curve. Drivers coming up behind you cannot see your car in time to stop or go around it.

Q12. Florida's handbook says that on route signs, even-numbered routes run:

Florida's handbook says east-west routes have even numbers and north-south routes have odd numbers.

Q13. Strong, gusty winds are blowing across the highway. What should you do about cruise control?

In high winds, do not use cruise control. Keep your own foot on the accelerator so you can react right away when a gust hits.

Q14. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q15. The diagonal stripes on a road-work barricade slope down toward the right. Which way should you go?

Barricade stripes slope down toward the side traffic should pass on. Stripes that slope down to the right mean bear to the right.

Q16. Florida law requires you to signal a turn continuously for at least the last:

Florida law says a turn signal must be given continuously for at least the last 100 feet before the turn.

Q17. You are following a bus or a truck with a hazardous materials placard toward a railroad crossing. What should you expect it to do?

Buses, school buses and vehicles carrying hazardous materials must stop at railroad crossings even when no train is in sight. Be ready to stop behind them.

Q18. You start driving on a snow-covered road. How can you find out how much traction your tires have?

Start out slowly, then test your brakes gently once the car is moving to feel how much traction you have. A gentle test warns you before you need to stop.

Q19. In Florida, speeding fines are doubled in:

Florida law doubles speeding fines in school zones and in posted construction zones when workers are present or operating equipment.

Q20. Florida's handbook says crashes between deer and vehicles happen most often:

Florida's handbook says deer and vehicle crashes occur most often from October to December.

Q21. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q22. When you glance over your shoulder to check your blind spot, what should you avoid?

When you check your blind spot, turn only your head. Turning your whole body or the steering wheel can pull the car out of its lane.

Q23. Compared with daylight, how does darkness change when you can see an object on the road?

At night you must be closer to an object than in daylight before you can see it. Slow down so you still have time to react when you do see it.

Q24. Before you walk away from your parked car in Florida, the law requires you to:

Florida law says you may not leave a vehicle unattended without stopping the engine, locking the ignition and removing the key.

Q25. In Florida, when a bicyclist or skateboarder is in a crosswalk or driveway, they are treated as:

Florida's handbook says that when in a crosswalk or driveway, bicyclists, skateboarders and skaters are considered pedestrians.
