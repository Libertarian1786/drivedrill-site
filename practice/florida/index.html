Q1. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q2. In Florida, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q3. During an official evacuation, Florida may:

Follow official directions and signs. Normal traffic laws still apply unless an officer directs otherwise.

Q4. A left turn on red from a one-way street onto another one-way street is:

Most states permit this turn after a complete stop and yielding, but it is banned in some cities, so obey any posted sign.

Q5. The minimum age for a Florida learner license is:

A learner license at 15 requires the traffic law and substance abuse course plus the knowledge exam and parental consent.

Q6. Florida requires every registered vehicle to carry:

Florida is a no-fault state: PIP pays your own medical costs regardless of fault, and PDL pays for damage you cause to others' property.

Q7. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q8. When passing a bicycle in Florida you must leave:

Three feet is the minimum, and Florida requires drivers to change lanes to pass where the lane is too narrow to share.

Q9. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q10. In Florida, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q11. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q12. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q13. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q14. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.

Q15. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q16. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q17. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q18. The standard Florida driver license for a car is:

Class E covers any non-commercial vehicle under 26,001 pounds. Commercial classes are A, B and C.

Q19. Beginning October 1, 2026, you must report a Florida crash to law enforcement immediately if:

Florida's SB 488 (Chapter 2026-39) raised the property-damage threshold from $500 to $2,000 starting October 1, 2026. When a crash damages property but needs no law enforcement report, the driver must send a written report to the department within 10 days.

Q20. You may park in a space marked with the International Symbol of Access:

Accessible spaces need a valid placard or plate, and the person it belongs to must be involved in the trip. Fines are steep and vehicles are towed.

Q21. You arrive at an intersection where the traffic signal is completely dark because of a power failure. You should:

A dark signal is treated as an all-way stop. Stop, then take turns in the order vehicles arrived.

Q22. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.

Q23. Before taking the Florida driving skills test, a learner must hold the permit for:

The 50 hours of supervised driving, 10 of them after dark, are certified by a parent or guardian.

Q24. In Florida, 12 points within 12 months results in:

The suspensions grow with the total: 18 points in 18 months brings three months, and 24 points in 36 months brings a year.

Q25. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.
