Q1. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q2. In a work zone you should:

Rear-end collisions are the most common work-zone crash. More space is what prevents them.

Q3. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q4. Before taking the Florida driving skills test, a learner must hold the permit for:

The 50 hours of supervised driving, 10 of them after dark, are certified by a parent or guardian.

Q5. In Florida you may not park:

Florida also prohibits parking within 20 feet of a crosswalk at an intersection and within 30 feet of a flashing signal or stop sign.

Q6. You must report a Florida crash to law enforcement immediately if:

Florida raised the property-damage threshold from $500 to $2,000 on October 1, 2026 (SB 488). If an officer does not investigate, the driver must file a self-report with the department within 10 days.

Q7. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.

Q8. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q9. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q10. The minimum age for a Florida learner license is:

A learner license at 15 requires the traffic law and substance abuse course plus the knowledge exam and parental consent.
