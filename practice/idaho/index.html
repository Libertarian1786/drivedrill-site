Q1. Your right wheels drift onto a soft shoulder. You should:

Yanking the wheel at speed flips the car back across the road. Slow first, then ease back on with a gentle steering input.

Q2. In Idaho, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q3. In Idaho, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q4. You arrive at an intersection where the traffic signal is completely dark because of a power failure. You should:

A dark signal is treated as an all-way stop. Stop, then take turns in the order vehicles arrived.

Q5. In Idaho, a vehicle traveling slower than the normal speed of traffic on a rural or two-lane highway and delaying three or more vehicles behind it must:

Idaho law requires a vehicle traveling slower than the normal speed of traffic that is delaying three or more other vehicles on a rural area or two-lane highway to turn off the road wherever it is safe to let them pass.

Q6. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q7. A sign showing a truck on a downward slope warns of:

On a steep downgrade, shift to a lower gear before you start down and use steady light braking so your brakes do not overheat and fade.

Q8. Driving around a lowered railroad crossing gate is:

Gates come down before a train arrives, and trains cannot stop quickly. If a gate stays down with no train, call the number posted at the crossing.

Q9. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q10. In Idaho, at what distance must you dim your headlamps before meeting an oncoming vehicle?

Idaho law requires headlamps to be dimmed 500 feet before meeting another vehicle, and 200 feet before overtaking one.
