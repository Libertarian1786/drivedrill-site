Q1. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.

Q2. In Idaho, at what distance must you dim your headlamps before meeting an oncoming vehicle?

Idaho law requires headlamps to be dimmed 500 feet before meeting another vehicle, and 200 feet before overtaking one.

Q3. A flagger in a work zone holds up a sign reading SLOW. You should:

Flaggers have the same authority as signs and signals in a work zone. Follow their direction and watch for workers on foot.

Q4. In Idaho, a vehicle traveling slower than the normal speed of traffic on a rural or two-lane highway and delaying three or more vehicles behind it must:

Idaho law requires a vehicle traveling slower than the normal speed of traffic that is delaying three or more other vehicles on a rural area or two-lane highway to turn off the road wherever it is safe to let them pass.

Q5. A police officer is directing traffic at an intersection where the light is green. You should:

Directions from an officer or flagger override signs and signals every time.

Q6. In Idaho, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a secondary enforcement state, so the belt alone is not grounds for the stop. That is a rule about enforcement, not about safety -- the crash risk is the same, and the ticket still follows any other stop.

Q7. A front tire blows out at highway speed. You should:

Braking or sudden steering after a blowout can spin the car. Grip the wheel, come off the gas, then ease onto the shoulder once you are slow.

Q8. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q9. In Idaho, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q10. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.
