Q1. In Idaho, unless otherwise posted, the maximum speed in a residential, business or urban district is:

Idaho's maximum speed in any residential, business or urban district is 35 mph unless another limit is posted.

Q2. On a long trip, how often should you stop to take a break?

On long trips, stop at least every two hours or every 100 miles to rest and stretch. Do not wait until you feel drowsy.

Q3. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q4. A small gasoline fire starts at your car. What should you never use on it?

Water spreads a gasoline fire instead of putting it out. Use a fire extinguisher only if the fire is small, and otherwise get everyone away and call 911.

Q5. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q6. You are stranded in a snowstorm. What helps rescuers find your vehicle?

Turn on your hazard lights and tie a bright flag or cloth to the antenna or door handle so rescuers can spot the vehicle. Stay inside and out of traffic.

Q7. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q8. Does Idaho require an annual safety inspection for passenger cars?

Idaho does not require an annual safety inspection, but an officer may stop and cite you if your vehicle is unsafe.

Q9. In an Idaho business or residential area, your turn signal must start at least how far before you turn?

Idaho requires signals to start at least 100 feet before a turn in business or residential areas.

Q10. A snowplow is working on the road ahead of you. What is the best choice?

Stay well back from a working snowplow, because it throws sand and snow and may stop or back up. Never pass it on the shoulder side.

Q11. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q12. In which situation should you leave extra space behind a motorcycle?

Metal surfaces such as bridge gratings and railroad tracks, and gravel, give a motorcycle poor traction. Follow farther back so you have room if the rider has to slow or swerve.

Q13. Your lane has a painted arrow that shows both a curved arrow and a straight arrow. What may you do from this lane?

A lane marked with both a curved and a straight arrow is a shared lane: you may turn in the direction the curved arrow points or continue straight.

Q14. In the United States, which way does traffic travel around a roundabout?

Traffic in a roundabout moves counterclockwise, to the right of the center island. Never drive around it the wrong way, even to reach an exit on your left.

Q15. In Idaho, you may not make a U-turn where approaching drivers cannot see your vehicle from within:

Idaho bars U-turns where approaching vehicles from either direction cannot see you within 500 feet, and in no-passing zones.

Q16. In Idaho, you leave your car unattended on the paved shoulder, off the traveled lanes. By when must it be moved?

Unattended vehicles off the traveled lanes but on the paved shoulder must be removed before darkness in Idaho, or they can be towed at the owner's expense.

Q17. In Idaho, a pass on a two-lane road must be finished before you come within what distance of oncoming traffic?

Idaho's handbook says to complete a pass before coming within 200 feet of oncoming traffic.

Q18. In Idaho, the one-passenger-under-17 restriction during the first 6 months of a license does not apply if the passenger is:

Idaho's restriction limiting a newly licensed driver to one passenger under 17 during the first 6 months does not apply when the passenger is related to the driver by blood, adoption, or marriage.

Q19. In Idaho, where a bike lane line becomes dashed before an intersection, a driver turning right should:

Dashed bike lane lines mean merging is allowed. Check for bicyclists first; when turning right you may occupy the bike lane in the dashed area.

Q20. After you move, you must usually:

Each state's law sets a short deadline. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q21. In Idaho, you may not park within what distance of railroad tracks?

Idaho bars parking within 50 feet of railroad tracks.

Q22. A pedestrian starts across the street, and your light turns green before they reach the other side. What must you do?

If the light changes after a pedestrian has entered the crosswalk, the pedestrian still has the right-of-way. Wait until they have finished crossing before you go.

Q23. Fog becomes so thick that you cannot see well enough to drive safely. What is the safest choice?

When fog is too thick to see the road, pull well off the road and wait for it to lift. Stopping or creeping in a travel lane risks being hit by drivers who cannot see you.

Q24. In Idaho, a driver fails a BAC test for the first time. The administrative license suspension is:

Idaho suspends driving privileges for 90 days for a first failed test, with no driving at all in the first 30 days, and then requires an interlock for a year.

Q25. When an airbag is fitted, you should sit:

Airbags deploy explosively. Handbooks ask for 10 inches or more, some 12. Distance plus a properly worn seat belt is what makes them protective rather than harmful.
