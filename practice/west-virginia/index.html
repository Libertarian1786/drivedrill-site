Q1. When an airbag is fitted, you should sit:

Airbags deploy explosively. Handbooks ask for 10 inches or more, some 12. Distance plus a properly worn seat belt is what makes them protective rather than harmful.

Q2. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q3. When a train is coming and you must stop at a railroad crossing, stop no closer to the nearest rail than:

Fifteen feet keeps you clear of the train's overhang and of the gate arm. Most states also say to stop no farther than 50 feet back, so you can still see the tracks.

Q4. In West Virginia, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q5. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q6. Before backing out of a parking space you should:

Cameras miss low objects and fast-moving children. Look behind the vehicle before you get in, then back slowly while checking all around.

Q7. Hazard lights should generally be used:

In most states hazard lights while driving normally are prohibited because they hide your turn signals.

Q8. In West Virginia, how many points on your driving record make your license subject to suspension?

West Virginia's point system tracks moving violations, and once you accumulate 12 points your driver's license becomes subject to suspension.

Q9. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q10. In West Virginia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q11. An open container of alcohol in the passenger area of the vehicle is:

Open container laws apply to the whole passenger compartment. Keep sealed alcohol in the trunk or a locked area.

Q12. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q13. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q14. You arrive at an intersection where the traffic signal is completely dark because of a power failure. You should:

A dark signal is treated as an all-way stop. Stop, then take turns in the order vehicles arrived.

Q15. In West Virginia, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q16. A space cushion around your vehicle is useful because:

Space is time. The more of it you keep on all four sides, the more options you have when something goes wrong.

Q17. In West Virginia, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q18. In West Virginia, a school zone extends how far along the street or highway from the school grounds?

West Virginia defines a school zone as the school property plus the street or highway extending 125 feet from the school grounds.

Q19. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q20. In West Virginia, the handbook says a driver's concentration drops by what percentage when using a wireless communication device?

West Virginia's handbook cites statistics that a driver's concentration drops by 20 to 33 percent when using a wireless communication device, which is why hands-free use is required.

Q21. In West Virginia, if you cannot safely move out of the lane next to a stopped emergency vehicle, you must slow to:

Changing lanes is the first choice. The speed rule is what applies when traffic or the road makes a lane change unsafe.

Q22. In West Virginia, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q23. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q24. In West Virginia, it is against the law to make a U-turn near a curve or the crest of a hill unless an approaching vehicle can see you from at least how far away?

West Virginia law bars turning around near a curve or hilltop where an approaching vehicle cannot see you from at least 500 feet in either direction.

Q25. After a school bus turns off its red lights, you should still:

Children are unpredictable right after they get off a bus. Move off slowly and keep scanning both sides.
