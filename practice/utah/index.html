Q1. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q2. Compared with daylight, how does darkness change when you can see an object on the road?

At night you must be closer to an object than in daylight before you can see it. Slow down so you still have time to react when you do see it.

Q3. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is safer, and the law often requires it.

Q4. Can you be convicted of driving under the influence if no breath or blood test was done?

You can be arrested and convicted of DUI without a BAC reading. Officers and courts can rely on other evidence, such as how you drove and how you did on field sobriety tests.

Q5. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q6. A signal at the end of a freeway on-ramp lets cars onto the freeway one at a time, switching between red and green. What must you do?

A ramp meter controls how quickly cars join the freeway. Stop at the line while it is red and go when it turns green.

Q7. In Utah, you may not make a U-turn on a curve or hilltop where you cannot see or be seen from both directions for:

Utah bans U-turns on a curve or hilltop where you cannot see or be seen from both directions for 500 feet.

Q8. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle traveling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q9. You may park in a space marked with the International Symbol of Access:

Accessible spaces need a valid placard or plate, and the person it belongs to must be involved in the trip. Fines are steep and vehicles are towed.

Q10. In Utah, during the first six months after a 16-year-old's license is issued, the only passengers allowed are:

The limit ends six months after the license is issued. Crash risk for a new driver rises with each extra young passenger.

Q11. In Utah, a motor-assisted scooter may not be ridden on a highway with a posted speed limit above:

Motor-assisted scooters may not be ridden on highways posted above 15 mph, on roads with 4 or more lanes, or in parking structures.

Q12. How long do alcohol- and drug-related violations stay on a Utah driving record?

Most moving violations show for 3 years, but alcohol- and drug-related violations, suspensions and revocations stay on the record for 10 years.

Q13. In Utah, which of these is a reason you may drive in a marked bicycle lane?

Do not stop, park or drive in a bicycle lane or path unless entering or leaving an alley or driveway, on police direction, or in an emergency.

Q14. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q15. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.

Q16. Fog becomes so thick that you cannot see well enough to drive safely. What is the safest choice?

When fog is too thick to see the road, pull well off the road and wait for it to lift. Stopping or creeping in a travel lane risks being hit by drivers who cannot see you.

Q17. Your car breaks down in the Utah desert and help is not a few minutes' walk away. What should you do?

If your vehicle breaks down in the desert, stay with it unless help is only a few minutes' walk away.

Q18. Wide, short broken white lines separate your lane from the next lane near a freeway exit. What do they usually mean?

Large broken lane lines usually mark a lane that is ending or exiting. If you do not want to leave the freeway, move out of that lane early.

Q19. You stop for a pedestrian in a Utah crosswalk. How long must you stay stopped?

Vehicles must stay stopped until the pedestrian has crossed at least the driver's half of the road.

Q20. In Utah, may you hold a driver license and a state identification card at the same time?

Utah law does not allow a person to have a driver license and an identification card at the same time.

Q21. Compared with ice at a much colder temperature, ice at about the freezing point is:

Ice near the freezing point is more slippery than much colder ice, because a thin layer of water sits on top of it. Take extra care when the temperature hovers around freezing.

Q22. You are on a narrow mountain road, and a sharp curve ahead blocks your view. What should you do?

Sound your horn as you approach a blind curve on a narrow road so oncoming drivers know you are there. Keep to the right and do not count on them to see or yield to you.

Q23. In Utah, a driver 21 or older who is not driving a commercial vehicle breaks the law with a blood alcohol concentration over:

Utah was the first state to set its limit at 0.05%, below the 0.08% used elsewhere. Commercial drivers are held to 0.04%, and you can be charged below the limit if you are impaired.

Q24. A highway has a posted minimum speed, but you cannot safely drive that fast. What should you do?

If you cannot or do not want to drive at or above the posted minimum speed, do not use that highway. Take another route.

Q25. In Utah, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offense. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.
