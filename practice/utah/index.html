Q1. A flagger in a work zone holds up a sign reading SLOW. You should:

Flaggers have the same authority as signs and signals in a work zone. Follow their direction and watch for workers on foot.

Q2. You are being passed by a large truck. You should:

Hold a steady speed and keep right within your lane. Air turbulence can push a small car, so keep both hands on the wheel.

Q3. In Utah, what is a driver license issued to a person under 21 years of age called?

Utah's handbook defines a Provisional License as a Utah license issued to any person under 21 years of age.

Q4. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q5. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q6. In Utah, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q7. Where a state uses a point system, it is used to:

Points accumulate with each conviction and are removed over time. Reaching the threshold brings a suspension. A few states, such as Minnesota, act on specific violations instead of points.

Q8. Blood alcohol concentration depends on:

Two people drinking the same amount can end up at very different levels, so nobody can judge their own BAC reliably.

Q9. In Utah, a driver 21 or older who is not driving a commercial vehicle breaks the law with a blood alcohol concentration over:

Utah was the first state to set its limit at 0.05%, below the 0.08% used elsewhere. Commercial drivers are held to 0.04%, and you can be charged below the limit if you are impaired.

Q10. In Utah, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.
