Q1. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q2. In North Carolina, refusing to take a required chemical test of your breath or blood after being charged with DWI results in:

Refusing the chemical test in North Carolina triggers an immediate revocation of at least 30 days on top of a separate, minimum 12-month revocation by the DMV.

Q3. In North Carolina, accumulating this many driver license points may require you to attend a driver improvement clinic:

Reaching seven points can trigger assignment to a driver improvement clinic, and completing it removes three points from the driving record.

Q4. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q5. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q6. In North Carolina, the maximum speed limit for a school bus is:

North Carolina sets the maximum speed limit for a school bus at 45 mph.

Q7. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q8. In North Carolina, besides sunset to sunrise, the law requires headlights whenever visibility drops to:

North Carolina law requires headlights from sunset to sunrise, and also any time visibility is 400 feet or less.

Q9. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q10. In North Carolina, a one-year driver license revocation applies to a person convicted of:

North Carolina requires a one-year license revocation for alcohol-related violations involving underage people, including giving alcohol to someone underage.

Q11. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q12. In North Carolina, a first conviction for Driving While Impaired results in:

North Carolina's DWI penalties escalate with each conviction, starting with a mandatory one-year license revocation for a first offense.

Q13. In North Carolina, a tractor-trailer's blind spot, or No-Zone, can extend behind the truck for up to:

Truckers have serious blind spots, or No-Zones, that reach up to 200 feet behind a tractor-trailer, so lingering there makes you invisible to the driver.

Q14. In North Carolina, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q15. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q16. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q17. Parking is prohibited:

Never park in a crosswalk, on a sidewalk, in an intersection, or where you would block a curb ramp.

Q18. In North Carolina, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q19. In North Carolina, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q20. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q21. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q22. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q23. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q24. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q25. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.
