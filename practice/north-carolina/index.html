Q1. Two lanes merge into one because of road work. The most efficient approach is to:

The zipper merge keeps queues shorter and speeds more even. Take turns at the merge point rather than fighting over it.

Q2. In North Carolina, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q3. A school bus ahead of you stops and puts out its red flashing lights and stop arm. You must:

Red flashing lights and an extended stop arm mean children are getting on or off. Stop well back and wait until the bus withdraws the arm and moves.

Q4. In North Carolina, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q5. In North Carolina, the handbook's rule of thumb for following distance behind the vehicle ahead is called:

North Carolina's handbook teaches drivers to allow two seconds between the time the vehicle ahead passes a point and the time their own vehicle reaches that same point.

Q6. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q7. In North Carolina, a tractor-trailer's blind spot, or No-Zone, can extend behind the truck for up to:

Truckers have serious blind spots, or No-Zones, that reach up to 200 feet behind a tractor-trailer, so lingering there makes you invisible to the driver.

Q8. A red warning light appears on the dashboard while you are driving. You should:

Red generally means stop now: oil pressure, temperature, brakes or charging. Amber usually means service soon.

Q9. Turning left from a one-way street onto another one-way street, you should:

Left turns between one-way streets are made from the leftmost lane into the leftmost lane.

Q10. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.
