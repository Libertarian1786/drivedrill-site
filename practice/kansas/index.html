Q1. You come to a stop sign, but no white stop line is painted on the road. Where should you stop?

Stop at the stop line if there is one. If there is no stop line, stop before the crosswalk; if there is no crosswalk either, stop before you enter the intersection, where you can see the cross traffic.

Q2. In Kansas, a child aged 4 to 8 must ride in a child safety seat or booster seat unless the child:

Children under 4 must be in a child safety seat, and children 8 and older must wear a seat belt. The driver is responsible for making sure every child is restrained.

Q3. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q4. A Kansas driver with an instruction permit or restricted license may use a wireless device while driving:

Kansas bars permit and restricted-license holders from using wireless devices while driving except to report illegal activity or summon emergency help.

Q5. In Kansas, unless another limit is posted, the speed limit on a state or federal highway is:

Kansas sets 65 mph as the limit on state and federal highways unless a different speed is posted.

Q6. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle traveling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q7. The recommended hand position on the steering wheel is:

Handbooks teach 9 and 3, 8 and 4, or lower still. Each gives good control and keeps your arms out of the airbag's path; 10 and 2 puts them where it deploys.

Q8. You are following a bus or a truck with a hazardous materials placard toward a railroad crossing. What should you expect it to do?

Buses, school buses and vehicles carrying hazardous materials must stop at railroad crossings even when no train is in sight. Be ready to stop behind them.

Q9. You are stranded in your car in a snowstorm and no help is in sight. What should you do?

Stay with your vehicle, because most deaths in these situations happen when people leave their cars, get lost and are exposed to the cold. The car is shelter and is easier for rescuers to find.

Q10. You are stranded in a snowstorm. What helps rescuers find your vehicle?

Turn on your hazard lights and tie a bright flag or cloth to the antenna or door handle so rescuers can spot the vehicle. Stay inside and out of traffic.

Q11. You start driving on a snow-covered road. How can you find out how much traction your tires have?

Start out slowly, then test your brakes gently once the car is moving to feel how much traction you have. A gentle test warns you before you need to stop.

Q12. In Kansas, a 15-year-old applying for a restricted license must have completed how many hours of supervised driving?

A 15-year-old in Kansas needs 25 hours of adult-supervised driving, plus an approved driver's education course, to get a restricted license.

Q13. In Kansas, vehicles may legally be equipped with studded snow tires during what part of the year?

Kansas allows vehicles to be equipped with studded snow tires from November 1st to April 1st each year.

Q14. Under a Kansas farm permit, a young driver may drive for which purpose?

A Kansas farm permit allows driving to and from, or for, farm work, and directly between home and school.

Q15. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q16. For a first DUI conviction in Kansas, the fine is:

Kansas fines a first DUI conviction from $750 to $1,000, plus court costs.

Q17. Where should a child in a rear-facing car seat never ride?

An inflating front airbag can strike a rear-facing car seat and seriously injure or kill the child. A rear-facing seat belongs in the back seat.

Q18. In Kansas, a school bus stops with red lights flashing on the other side of a road that has a center turning lane. What must you do?

Kansas requires you to stop for a school bus when there is a center turning lane, whether the bus is on your side or the opposite side.

Q19. In Kansas, state law requires that a turn or lane-change signal be given how far before the move, at minimum?

Kansas law requires signals to be given at least 100 feet before the actual move or turn.

Q20. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q21. When towing a trailer you should:

Extra weight lengthens every distance. Speed limits are sometimes lower for vehicles that are towing.

Q22. You are on a narrow mountain road, and a sharp curve ahead blocks your view. What should you do?

Sound your horn as you approach a blind curve on a narrow road so oncoming drivers know you are there. Keep to the right and do not count on them to see or yield to you.

Q23. Your vehicle has four-wheel drive. How should you drive on an icy road?

Four-wheel drive does not make ice safe, because the vehicle can still skid and slide, so drive slowly and carefully. It can give a false sense of security.

Q24. Two solid white lines side by side separate two lanes going the same way. What do they mean?

Double solid white lines are a barrier between lanes going the same way, such as beside a carpool lane. Do not cross them to change lanes; wait until they end or become broken.

Q25. In Kansas, when entering a roundabout or traffic circle, the handbook advises slowing to about:

Kansas's handbook advises slowing your speed to about 15 to 20 mph when entering a roundabout or traffic circle.
