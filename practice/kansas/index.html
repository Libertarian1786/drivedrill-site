Q1. In Kansas, you may not park within a certain distance of a traffic control signal, stop sign or flashing beacon. That distance is:

Kansas prohibits parking within 30 feet approaching any traffic control signal, stop sign, or flashing beacon.

Q2. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q3. In Kansas, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q4. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q5. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q6. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q7. In Kansas, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q8. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q9. In Kansas, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q10. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.
