Q1. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q2. In Kansas, a newly licensed driver under 18 generally may not drive between:

Documented exceptions usually cover work, school activities, emergencies and driving with a licensed adult. Carry the paperwork if you rely on one.

Q3. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q4. In Kansas, you may not park within a certain distance of a traffic control signal, stop sign or flashing beacon. That distance is:

Kansas prohibits parking within 30 feet approaching any traffic control signal, stop sign, or flashing beacon.

Q5. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q6. In Kansas, unless otherwise posted, the maximum speed limit in an urban district (in towns or cities) is:

Kansas law sets a default maximum of 30 mph in any urban district when no other speed is posted.

Q7. A red warning light appears on the dashboard while you are driving. You should:

Red generally means stop now: oil pressure, temperature, brakes or charging. Amber usually means service soon.

Q8. In Kansas, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q9. In Kansas, a new resident who moves to the state must obtain a Kansas driver's license within:

Kansas requires anyone moving to the state to obtain a Kansas driver's license within 90 days of moving there.

Q10. The best response to feeling drowsy while driving is to:

Drowsy driving impairs you much like alcohol, and micro-sleeps happen without warning. Only sleep fixes it.

Q11. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q12. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q13. In Kansas, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q14. In Kansas, the minimum age to hold an unrestricted (fully privileged) Non-Commercial Class C driver's license is:

Kansas lists the Non-Commercial Class C driver's license as available to those 17 years of age or older, and the Unrestricted Driver's License requires a minimum age of 17.

Q15. You arrive at an intersection where the traffic signal is completely dark because of a power failure. You should:

A dark signal is treated as an all-way stop. Stop, then take turns in the order vehicles arrived.

Q16. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q17. In Kansas, refusing a lawfully requested chemical test under the implied consent law results in a suspension of your license, for a first refusal, of:

Kansas's implied consent law imposes a mandatory 1-year suspension for a first refusal to submit to chemical testing, with longer suspensions for repeat refusals.

Q18. A left turn on red from a one-way street onto another one-way street is:

Most states permit this turn after a complete stop and yielding, but it is banned in some cities, so obey any posted sign.

Q19. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q20. In Kansas, unless otherwise posted, the maximum speed limit on a county or township road is:

Kansas sets a default maximum of 55 mph on county or township roads when no other speed is posted.

Q21. In Kansas, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q22. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q23. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q24. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q25. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.
