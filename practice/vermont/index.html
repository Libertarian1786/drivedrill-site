Q1. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q2. In Vermont, at night you should always be able to see how far ahead of your vehicle?

Vermont's manual says you always need to be able to see 4 seconds ahead at night; driving 40 mph on low beams exceeds that distance, which means you are overdriving your headlights.

Q3. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q4. In Vermont, a driver who reaches a total of how many points on their driving record will be notified that their license is to be suspended?

Vermont's point system sends a suspension notice once a driver accumulates a total of 10 points on their driving record.

Q5. In Vermont, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q6. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, commonly 10 to 15 feet. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q7. Adjusting your side mirrors slightly outward compared with the usual setting:

Wider mirror angles shrink the blind spot, but a head check before changing lanes is still the move that catches what mirrors miss.

Q8. In Vermont, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q9. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q10. You smell smoke and see flames under the hood. You should:

Do not open the hood; air feeds the fire. Get people at least 100 feet away and let the fire department handle it.
