Q1. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q2. Fluorescent yellow-green warning signs usually warn you about:

Fluorescent yellow-green is used for warning signs about schools, school buses, pedestrians and bicycles, so you notice where these people may be.

Q3. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q4. In Vermont, when must you send a written crash report to the DMV, and how soon?

Vermont law (23 V.S.A. § 1129) says that if anyone is injured or total property damage is $3,000 or more, you must send a written crash report to the DMV within 72 hours.

Q5. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q6. In Vermont, a learner permit holder may drive only with which person riding in the front seat?

The Vermont manual says a permit holder must have a licensed and unimpaired parent or guardian, a licensed or certified driver education instructor, or a licensed and unimpaired person 25 or older riding in the front seat.

Q7. In Vermont, which of these violations puts no points on your driving record?

The Vermont manual says points are not assessed for parking or defective equipment violations.

Q8. You hit a parked car and cannot find its owner. What must you do?

If you damage an unattended vehicle, you must find the owner or leave a written note where it will be seen, with your name and how to reach you. Some states also require a police report.

Q9. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q10. In Vermont, a person under 21 is cited for the first time for possessing alcohol and does not complete the Youth Substance Awareness Safety Program. The penalty is:

Under current Vermont law (7 V.S.A. § 656), a person under 21 who does not complete the Youth Substance Awareness Safety Program after a first offense faces a $300 civil penalty and a 30-day license suspension. The 2024 manual, which calls it the Teen Alcohol Safety Program, still gives the older suspension.

Q11. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q12. Why do drivers often misjudge an approaching large truck?

Because of its large size, a truck often appears to be going slower than it really is. Allow more time than you think before you pull out in front of one.

Q13. In Vermont, after you change your name or address, you must report it to the DMV within:

The Vermont manual says that if you change your name or address, you must report the change to the DMV within 30 days.

Q14. You have just driven through deep water, and your brakes feel weak. How can you dry them?

After driving through water, test your brakes. If they are wet, drive slowly in a low gear and press the brake pedal lightly until they work normally again.

Q15. In Vermont, a school bus is stopped with its red lights flashing in a school yard, not on a public road. What must you do?

The Vermont manual says you must never pass a stopped school bus with its red lights flashing from any direction, even in a school yard.

Q16. A yellow warning sign reads SOFT SHOULDER. What does it warn you about?

SOFT SHOULDER warns that the dirt or gravel beside the pavement is soft. Stay on the pavement; if your wheels drift onto a soft shoulder, the car can be pulled off the road and you can lose control.

Q17. In Vermont, the Purchase and Use Tax on a vehicle you buy is:

The Vermont manual says the Purchase and Use Tax is 6% of the price you paid or the current fair market value, whichever is greater.

Q18. In Vermont, when stopping on a rural highway, you must not stop where your vehicle cannot be seen from at least how far away in each direction?

The Vermont manual says that on a rural highway you must get all wheels off the traveled road and must not stop where your vehicle cannot be seen for at least 150 feet in either direction.

Q19. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q20. Which habit helps keep carbon monoxide from leaking into your car?

Have the exhaust system checked regularly so a leak does not let carbon monoxide into the car. Do not drive with all the windows shut tight, and never warm up the engine in a closed garage.

Q21. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q22. In Vermont, an applicant under 18 can get a learner permit only with a clean driving record for the previous:

The Vermont manual says a permit applicant under 18 must have kept a clean driving record for the previous two years.

Q23. In Vermont, your driver's license expired a few weeks ago. When does it stop being valid for driving?

The Vermont manual says a Vermont license that has been expired for more than 14 days is not a valid license.

Q24. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.

Q25. A motorcyclist ahead of you slows down, but no brake light comes on. Why can that happen?

Motorcyclists often slow down by downshifting or rolling off the throttle, which does not turn on the brake light. Leave extra space and watch the rider, not just the light.
