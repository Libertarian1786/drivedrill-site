Q1. Speeding fines in a work zone are usually:

Most states increase work-zone fines and add penalties when workers are present, whether or not the crew is visible at that moment.

Q2. The basic speed law means:

The posted limit is the maximum for perfect conditions. In rain, fog, traffic or at night, the legal safe speed is lower.

Q3. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q4. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q5. In Vermont, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q6. In Vermont, a driver who reaches a total of how many points on their driving record will be notified that their license is to be suspended?

Vermont's point system sends a suspension notice once a driver accumulates a total of 10 points on their driving record.

Q7. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q8. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q9. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q10. In Vermont, at night you should always be able to see how far ahead of your vehicle?

Vermont's manual says you always need to be able to see 4 seconds ahead at night; driving 40 mph on low beams exceeds that distance, which means you are overdriving your headlights.

Q11. Unless a sign prohibits it, a right turn on red is allowed:

Right on red requires a full stop first, then yielding to everyone already entitled to go. A NO TURN ON RED sign removes the option entirely.

Q12. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q13. In Vermont, a child over 2 but under 5 years of age must be secured in:

Vermont's manual requires a child over 2 but under 5 years old to be properly secured in a federally approved rear-facing or forward-facing car seat with a harness.

Q14. In Vermont, a child under two years of age must ride in:

Vermont law requires a child under two years of age to be properly secured in a federally approved rear-facing car seat with a harness.

Q15. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q16. In Vermont, during the restricted period, a young driver may carry:

Crash risk for a teen driver climbs sharply with each additional teen passenger, which is what this rule is built around.

Q17. In Vermont, if you miss your exit ramp on the interstate, you should:

Vermont's manual says that if you miss your exit, you should never stop and back up or use the emergency turnaround, since those crossovers are for emergency and maintenance vehicles only.

Q18. Seat belts work best because they:

Belts keep you behind the wheel and away from the windshield, and they position you correctly for the airbag.

Q19. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q20. Before backing out of a parking space you should:

Cameras miss low objects and fast-moving children. Look behind the vehicle before you get in, then back slowly while checking all around.

Q21. In Vermont, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q22. In Vermont, if you are unable to safely change lanes away from a stopped emergency, tow, or repair vehicle with flashing lights, you must:

Vermont's manual says that if you are unable to change lanes away from a stopped vehicle with warning lights, you must slow down to 20 mph below the speed limit to go by it safely.

Q23. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q24. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, commonly 10 to 15 feet. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q25. In Vermont, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.
