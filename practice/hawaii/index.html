Q1. In Hawaii, passing is prohibited when you are approaching within a set distance of an intersection or railroad crossing. That distance is:

Hawaii's no-passing rules prohibit passing when approaching within 100 feet of, or while traversing, an intersection or railroad crossing.

Q2. In Hawaii, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

The state requires hands-free operation for every driver. Novice drivers are usually barred from using a phone at all, even hands-free.

Q3. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q4. In Hawaii, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q5. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q6. In Hawaii, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q7. In Hawaii, when a school bus stops on a highway with its red signal lamps flashing, which vehicles must stop?

Hawaii law requires the driver of any motor vehicle on the same highway, in the lane occupied by the school bus and in all lanes adjacent to that lane, to stop before reaching the bus.

Q8. Worn tire tread is dangerous mainly because it:

Tread channels water away. Once it is shallow, wet grip falls off sharply. Check with a tread gauge or the built-in wear bars.

Q9. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q10. In Hawaii, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q11. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, commonly 10 to 15 feet. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q12. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q13. In Hawaii, having how many persons in the front seat of a vehicle may constitute illegal interference with the driver?

The handbook states that driving with more than three persons in the front seat of a vehicle may constitute interference with the driver, and such interference is prohibited.

Q14. A green arrow pointing left means:

A green arrow is a protected turn. Still check the crosswalk for people before you move.

Q15. A standard drink of beer, wine and distilled spirits:

A 12 oz beer, a 5 oz glass of wine and 1.5 oz of spirits each contain roughly the same alcohol, which is why counting drinks does not make you safe to drive.

Q16. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q17. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q18. The horn should be used to:

A horn is a safety device. Using it out of frustration escalates situations and, in some places, is a citation.

Q19. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q20. In heavy fog you should drive with:

High beams reflect off fog and blind you. Use low beams or fog lights, slow down, and pull well off the road if you cannot see.

Q21. In Hawaii, leaving a child unattended in a motor vehicle is prohibited by law when the child is under nine years old and left for:

Hawaii law prohibits a driver or any adult passenger from leaving a child under nine years of age unattended in a motor vehicle for five minutes or longer, because of risks like heat exposure.

Q22. In Hawaii, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q23. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q24. In Hawaii, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q25. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.
