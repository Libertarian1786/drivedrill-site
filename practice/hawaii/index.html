Q1. You are on a narrow mountain road, and a sharp curve ahead blocks your view. What should you do?

Sound your horn as you approach a blind curve on a narrow road so oncoming drivers know you are there. Keep to the right and do not count on them to see or yield to you.

Q2. Fluorescent yellow-green warning signs usually warn you about:

Fluorescent yellow-green is used for warning signs about schools, school buses, pedestrians and bicycles, so you notice where these people may be.

Q3. In Hawaii, you must stop at a railroad crossing when a train sounds its warning within about:

When a train within about 1,500 feet sounds its horn, or is close enough to be a hazard, stop and wait until it is safe.

Q4. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q5. In Hawaii, drinking or carrying an open alcoholic drink on a public highway or sidewalk is:

Hawaii forbids drinking or possessing alcoholic beverages on any public highway or public sidewalk.

Q6. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, so learn your state's distance. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q7. In Hawaii, what is true about a tow-away zone during its posted hours?

During the posted hours you may not park or even stop for a moment in a tow-away zone, unless other traffic blocks you. The police will tow the car.

Q8. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q9. You are turning left on a green arrow, and the arrow changes to a steady yellow arrow. What does that mean?

A steady yellow arrow means the protected turning time is ending and the signal is about to change. Stop if you can do so safely; if you are already in the intersection, finish the turn with care.

Q10. Hawaii's driver manual says that before you pass another car, you should:

On the open road, a short tap of the horn tells the driver ahead that you are about to pass.

Q11. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q12. You come to a flooded road, and you know a different way to get where you are going. What should you do?

Turn around and take another route when a road ahead is flooded. You cannot tell how deep the water is or whether the road is still there under it, and a vehicle can be swept away.

Q13. You hit a parked car and cannot find its owner. What must you do?

If you damage an unattended vehicle, you must find the owner or leave a written note where it will be seen, with your name and how to reach you. Some states also require a police report.

Q14. In Hawaii, a registered vehicle owner who moves must notify the county in writing within:

Owners must report a new address to the county agency in writing within 30 days of the change.

Q15. Hawaii's manual says that in fog, if you cannot see at least two car lengths ahead, you should:

When you cannot see about 40 feet ahead, get completely off the road, stop and use your hazard lights until it clears.

Q16. A small sign under a railroad crossbuck reads 2 TRACKS. What does it tell you?

When more than one track crosses the road, a sign under the crossbuck shows the number of tracks.

Q17. In Hawaii, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q18. The water covering a road ahead looks shallow. What is true about it?

Floodwater can hide dips and wash away the road surface beneath it, so you cannot judge safety by how shallow it looks. Turn around rather than try to cross.

Q19. A snowplow is working on the road ahead of you. What is the best choice?

Stay well back from a working snowplow, because it throws sand and snow and may stop or back up. Never pass it on the shoulder side.

Q20. You are at a flashing red light. What should you remember about the traffic on the cross street?

Traffic on the cross street often has a flashing yellow light and may not have to stop at all. Do not pull out until there is a safe gap.

Q21. In Hawaii, having how many persons in the front seat of a vehicle may constitute illegal interference with the driver?

The handbook states that driving with more than three persons in the front seat of a vehicle may constitute interference with the driver, and such interference is prohibited.

Q22. In Hawaii, what must you show to pass a vehicle safety inspection?

Without a Hawaii insurance identification card, the vehicle cannot pass the safety inspection.

Q23. Your car has airbags. Do you still need to wear your seat belt?

Airbags are designed to work together with seat belts, not to replace them. Without a belt you can be thrown into the opening airbag or out of the car.

Q24. In Hawaii, your trailer blocks the view through your inside mirror. What do you need?

When a trailer blocks the inside mirror, you must have properly sized outside mirrors on both sides.

Q25. A person hurt in a crash is bleeding badly. What should you do until help arrives?

Stop serious bleeding by pressing thick cloth pads firmly on the wound, and keep up the pressure until help arrives.
