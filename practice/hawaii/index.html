Q1. The most common cause of freeway merging crashes is:

Match the speed of traffic before you merge; joining at 40 mph into 65 mph traffic is what causes the problem.

Q2. In Hawaii, you must not pass another vehicle when approaching a hill or curve unless you have a clear view ahead of at least:

The handbook lists approaching a hill or curve where there is not 700 to 1000 feet of clear distance ahead as one of the situations where passing is prohibited.

Q3. In Hawaii, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q4. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle travelling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q5. The back of your car starts to slide to the right. You should:

Steer into the skid: point the front wheels where you want the car to go, ease off the pedals, and make small corrections.

Q6. Driving after using marijuana is:

Legal to possess does not mean legal to drive on. Impaired driving laws cover cannabis the same way they cover alcohol.

Q7. In Hawaii, when you count seconds to check your following distance, the handbook says you should have at least:

The handbook's following-distance method says that if you reach the two-count before your vehicle reaches the marker the leading car passed, you have at least a two second interval, and you should never have less.

Q8. When an airbag is fitted, you should sit:

Airbags deploy explosively. Handbooks ask for 10 inches or more, some 12. Distance plus a properly worn seat belt is what makes them protective rather than harmful.

Q9. When towing a trailer you should:

Extra weight lengthens every distance. Many states also set lower speed limits for vehicles that are towing.

Q10. In Hawaii, leaving a child unattended in a motor vehicle is prohibited by law when the child is under nine years old and left for:

Hawaii law prohibits a driver or any adult passenger from leaving a child under nine years of age unattended in a motor vehicle for five minutes or longer, because of risks like heat exposure.
