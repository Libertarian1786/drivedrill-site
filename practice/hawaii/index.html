Q1. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q2. In Hawaii, passing is prohibited when you are approaching within a set distance of an intersection or railroad crossing. That distance is:

Hawaii's no-passing rules prohibit passing when approaching within 100 feet of, or while traversing, an intersection or railroad crossing.

Q3. In Hawaii, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q4. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q5. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.

Q6. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q7. In Hawaii, having how many persons in the front seat of a vehicle may constitute illegal interference with the driver?

The handbook states that driving with more than three persons in the front seat of a vehicle may constitute interference with the driver, and such interference is prohibited.

Q8. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q9. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q10. In Hawaii, leaving a child unattended in a motor vehicle is prohibited by law when the child is under nine years old and left for:

Hawaii law prohibits a driver or any adult passenger from leaving a child under nine years of age unattended in a motor vehicle for five minutes or longer, because of risks like heat exposure.
