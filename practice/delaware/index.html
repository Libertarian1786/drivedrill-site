Q1. In Delaware, before turning or changing lanes, the law requires you to signal:

Delaware law requires drivers to signal 300 feet prior to the intended turn, stop, or lane change.

Q2. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q3. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q4. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q5. Worn tire tread is dangerous mainly because it:

Tread channels water away. Once it is shallow, wet grip falls off sharply. Check with a tread gauge or the built-in wear bars.

Q6. In Delaware, the move-over law requires you to change lanes, or slow down, when passing:

Move to a lane that is not next to the stopped vehicle when you safely can. When you cannot, slow down. People working at the roadside are struck every year by drivers who did neither.

Q7. In Delaware, the Division of Motor Vehicles may take administrative action, such as a warning letter or suspension, against a driver who accumulates:

Delaware's point system allows the Division to take administrative action against a driver who acquires 8 or more points within a two year period.

Q8. An unrestrained pet in the car is a problem because:

Secure pets in a carrier or harness. Several states treat an animal in your lap as distracted driving.

Q9. In Delaware, headlights must be turned on any time you cannot see:

Delaware law requires headlights on any time visibility drops to less than 1,000 feet, in addition to sunset-to-sunrise use.

Q10. In Delaware, a child under the age of 12 generally should:

Delaware's driver manual advises that a child under the age of 12 should not occupy the front passenger seat of a vehicle.

Q11. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q12. In Delaware, a child must ride in a rear-facing car seat until the child is:

Delaware law sets the rear-facing car seat requirement at under age 2 and 30 pounds.

Q13. In Delaware, a new resident who holds an out-of-state driver license must apply for a Delaware license:

Delaware's manual states that a driver moving into the state must apply for a Delaware license within 60 days after becoming a resident.

Q14. An unsecured load in the back of a pickup truck is:

You are responsible for anything that falls off your vehicle. Tie loads down and cover loose material.

Q15. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q16. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q17. A curb painted with diagonal yellow lines next to a wide striped area means:

Striped buffer areas separate traffic from hazards, medians or opposing lanes. Driving or parking in them is prohibited.

Q18. A wide white line across your lane at an intersection is:

Stop at the line, not past it. If your view is blocked once stopped, edge forward slowly only after stopping at the line.

Q19. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q20. The pedestrian signal shows a flashing orange hand with a countdown while you wait to turn. It means:

The countdown is for people already crossing. Wait until the crosswalk is clear before completing your turn.

Q21. In Delaware, if you cannot safely move out of the lane next to a stopped emergency vehicle, you must slow to:

Changing lanes is the first choice. The speed rule is what applies when traffic or the road makes a lane change unsafe.

Q22. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q23. In Delaware, cell phone use while driving for a holder of a Graduated Driver License (GDL) is:

Delaware's manual states that all cell phone or electronic device use, including hands-free, is prohibited for drivers with a Graduated Driver License.

Q24. In Delaware, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q25. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.
