Q1. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q2. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q3. You come up behind slow-moving farm equipment displaying an orange triangle. You should:

The orange triangle marks a vehicle travelling under 25 mph. Closing speed is higher than it looks, so slow early and pass only with a long clear view.

Q4. In Delaware, unless another limit is posted, the speed limit in a school zone when children are present is:

The 20 mph school-zone limit applies when children are present. Watch for children who may dart into the street.

Q5. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

The license consequence is administrative and starts before any court date in most states, and the record follows you for years.

Q6. In Delaware, headlights must be turned on any time you cannot see:

Delaware law requires headlights on any time visibility drops to less than 1,000 feet, in addition to sunset-to-sunrise use.

Q7. Counting seconds after the vehicle ahead passes a fixed point is a way to:

Pick a sign or pole; when the vehicle ahead passes it, start counting. If you reach it before your count is up, drop back. States set the minimum anywhere from two to four seconds in good conditions, and every state says to add time for rain, night and large vehicles.

Q8. An unsecured load in the back of a pickup truck is:

You are responsible for anything that falls off your vehicle. Tie loads down and cover loose material.

Q9. In Delaware, a new resident who holds an out-of-state driver license must apply for a Delaware license:

Delaware's manual states that a driver moving into the state must apply for a Delaware license within 60 days after becoming a resident.

Q10. In Delaware, a child must ride in a rear-facing car seat until the child is:

Delaware law sets the rear-facing car seat requirement at under age 2 and 30 pounds.
