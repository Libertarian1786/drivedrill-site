Q1. The pedestrian signal shows a flashing orange hand with a countdown while you wait to turn. It means:

The countdown is for people already crossing. Wait until the crosswalk is clear before completing your turn.

Q2. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump to build pressure, downshift for engine braking, apply the parking brake gradually, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q3. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q4. In Delaware, headlights must be turned on any time you cannot see:

Delaware law requires headlights on any time visibility drops to less than 1,000 feet, in addition to sunset-to-sunrise use.

Q5. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.

Q6. In Delaware, a child must ride in a rear-facing car seat until the child is:

Delaware law sets the rear-facing car seat requirement at under age 2 and 30 pounds.

Q7. In Delaware, the Division of Motor Vehicles may take administrative action, such as a warning letter or suspension, against a driver who accumulates:

Delaware's point system allows the Division to take administrative action against a driver who acquires 8 or more points within a two year period.

Q8. In Delaware, before turning or changing lanes, the law requires you to signal:

Delaware law requires drivers to signal 300 feet prior to the intended turn, stop, or lane change.

Q9. When leaving a freeway you should:

Keep your speed until you are on the deceleration ramp, then slow. Braking in the travel lane surprises the traffic behind you.

Q10. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.
