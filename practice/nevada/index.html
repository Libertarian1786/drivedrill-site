Q1. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q2. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q3. A deer suddenly steps into the road ahead of you. What is usually the safest choice?

Swerving to miss an animal can send you into oncoming traffic or off the road, which is often worse than hitting it. Brake firmly, stay in control and keep your lane.

Q4. In Nevada, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q5. In Nevada, you may not park on a crosswalk or within how many feet of one?

Nevada does not allow parking on a crosswalk or within 20 feet of one, so drivers can see people crossing.

Q6. You are about to turn at a corner, but you realize you are not in the proper lane for the turn. What should you do?

Do not turn from the wrong lane or cut across other traffic at the last moment. Drive on to the next intersection, or around the block, and make the turn from the proper lane.

Q7. In the United States, which way does traffic travel around a roundabout?

Traffic in a roundabout moves counterclockwise, to the right of the center island. Never drive around it the wrong way, even to reach an exit on your left.

Q8. In Nevada, you must also use your headlights whenever people or vehicles cannot be clearly seen at a distance of:

Besides the night hours, headlights are required whenever persons or vehicles cannot be clearly seen at 1,000 feet, such as in rain, snow or dust.

Q9. What is a reversible lane?

Reversible lanes carry traffic one way at some times and the other way at other times, usually to handle rush-hour traffic. Check the signs and lane-use signals before you enter one.

Q10. An oncoming driver fails to dim their high beams. You should:

Watching the right edge line keeps you in your lane while your eyes recover. Retaliating with high beams blinds you both.

Q11. In Nevada, a driver may use a hand-held phone to:

The Nevada handbook says you may use the device to report an emergency if stopping the vehicle would be inadvisable, impractical or dangerous.

Q12. In Nevada, a white curb usually means:

A white curb usually allows only a very short stop to take on or let off passengers, or to drop mail in a mailbox.

Q13. You are at a flashing red light. What should you remember about the traffic on the cross street?

Traffic on the cross street often has a flashing yellow light and may not have to stop at all. Do not pull out until there is a safe gap.

Q14. In Nevada, studded snow tires may be used:

Nevada allows studded snow tires only from October 1 through April 30.

Q15. In Nevada, you may not make a U-turn where rain, fog or another condition keeps you from seeing clearly in each direction for at least:

The Nevada handbook says never to make a U-turn where rain, fog or another condition keeps you from seeing clearly 200 feet in each direction, since oncoming traffic must be able to see and react to the turning vehicle.

Q16. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is unsafe and often against the law, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q17. A car moves into the space in front of you and is much too close. What should you do first?

If a vehicle merges in front of you too closely, ease off the accelerator to rebuild your following distance. Braking hard could cause the driver behind you to hit you.

Q18. In Nevada, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q19. Compared with daylight, how does darkness change when you can see an object on the road?

At night you must be closer to an object than in daylight before you can see it. Slow down so you still have time to react when you do see it.

Q20. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q21. In Nevada, school zone speed limits of 15 or 25 mph are generally in effect:

Nevada school zone speed limits are either 15 or 25 mph, and unless otherwise posted they apply on school days starting a half hour before school begins and ending a half hour after school ends.

Q22. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q23. Nevada's rules for approaching a stopped emergency or service vehicle also cover a stopped:

The rules cover law enforcement vehicles, tow trucks, NDOT vehicles, garbage trucks, any vehicle showing non-flashing blue lights, and anyone repairing a vehicle (NRS 484B.607).

Q24. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q25. You may park in a space marked with the International Symbol of Access:

Accessible spaces need a valid placard or plate, and the person it belongs to must be involved in the trip. Fines are steep and vehicles are towed.
