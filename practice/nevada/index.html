Q1. In Nevada, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q2. In Nevada, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q3. A flashing yellow arrow at a left-turn lane means:

A flashing yellow arrow is an unprotected turn. Oncoming traffic has the green, so you may only go when there is a safe gap.

Q4. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q5. School zone speed limits usually apply:

Read the sign: many zones are time-based, others use flashing beacons or a when-children-are-present condition.

Q6. Implied consent means that by driving in the state you have agreed to:

Refusing the test carries its own suspension, usually longer than the one for failing it, and the refusal can be used against you.

Q7. Driving through a work zone at night, you should:

Lane patterns change day to day in a work zone, and workers may be close to traffic. Read the signs rather than relying on memory.

Q8. In Nevada, how close to a fire hydrant may you legally park where parallel parking is permitted?

Nevada law sets a specific no-parking zone around fire hydrants: within 15 feet where parallel parking is allowed, or within 20 feet where angle parking is allowed.

Q9. When there is no adjacent lane to move into, how much clearance must a Nevada driver give a bicyclist when passing?

When a motorist cannot move into an adjacent lane to pass a bicyclist, Nevada law requires passing to the left of the bicycle at a safe distance of at least three feet.

Q10. The best way to reduce glare from headlights behind you at night is to:

The night position deflects the bright image while keeping a usable view behind you.
