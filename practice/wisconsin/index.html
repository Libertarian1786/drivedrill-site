Q1. In Wisconsin, in a two-lane roundabout, which lane do you use to make a U-turn?

In a Wisconsin two-lane roundabout, left turns and U-turns use the left lane. Right turns use the right lane.

Q2. You stop behind another vehicle in traffic. How much room should you leave?

When you stop behind a vehicle, leave enough room to see its rear tires on the pavement. That gives you space to go around it if it stalls.

Q3. A large truck is backing up into a loading dock across your path. What should you do?

Truck drivers have a large blind spot behind the trailer and may not see you at all. Never cross behind a truck that is backing up; wait until it is done.

Q4. In Wisconsin, a family friend age 30 wants to supervise a 16-year-old permit holder. What does the friend need first?

The Wisconsin handbook says that if the permit holder is under 18, an accompanying licensed person 21 or older must have written authorization from the minor's parent or guardian.

Q5. In Wisconsin, to start parallel parking, you stop even with the car in front of the space. About how far from that car should you be?

The Wisconsin handbook says to pull up even with the car ahead of the space and stop about 2 feet away from it.

Q6. A pedestrian starts across the street, and your light turns green before they reach the other side. What must you do?

If the light changes after a pedestrian has entered the crosswalk, the pedestrian still has the right-of-way. Wait until they have finished crossing before you go.

Q7. You are stranded in a snowstorm and run the engine for heat. What should you check to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning?

Keep the tailpipe clear of snow whenever the engine is running, because a blocked pipe can send carbon monoxide into the car. Run the engine only for short periods, with a window open slightly.

Q8. At a railroad crossing, a small blue sign shows a phone number and a crossing ID number. When should you use it?

The blue Emergency Notification System (ENS) sign gives the railroad's emergency number and the crossing's ID. Call it if a vehicle is stalled on the tracks or the signals or gates are not working, so trains can be warned; call 911 too if anyone is in danger.

Q9. In Wisconsin, which vehicles may never drive in a flex lane, even when it is open?

The Wisconsin handbook says no large trucks can drive in the flex lane.

Q10. In Wisconsin, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

This is a true zero tolerance state: any measurable alcohol is enough for a suspension, and mouthwash or cough syrup can reach it.

Q11. In Wisconsin, the graduated driver license (GDL) restrictions on a probationary license are required for the first:

Wisconsin's graduated driver license (GDL) restrictions last for the first nine months of a probationary license, unless the driver turns 18 sooner. Once you turn 18, the GDL hours and passenger restrictions no longer apply.

Q12. In Wisconsin, you hit a deer with your car. What does the handbook say about the deer?

The Wisconsin handbook says that if you hit a deer, you can keep it. Call the police so they can issue a tag for the deer.

Q13. You come to a stop sign, but no white stop line is painted on the road. Where should you stop?

Stop at the stop line if there is one. If there is no stop line, stop before the crosswalk; if there is no crosswalk either, stop before you enter the intersection, where you can see the cross traffic.

Q14. A vehicle is coming straight at you in your lane. What should you do?

If a vehicle comes at you in your lane, sound your horn and flash your lights, brake, and steer to the right. Do not swerve left, because the other driver may move back into their own lane.

Q15. Your car stalls in rising floodwater. If you can leave it safely, what should you do?

If your car stalls in floodwater, leave it as soon as you safely can and move to higher ground. Floodwater can rise quickly and sweep a car and its passengers away, and trying to move a stalled vehicle has cost lives.

Q16. In Wisconsin, when must you present proof of insurance?

You must show proof of insurance whenever a police officer asks for it, and you must carry that proof every time you drive. You may show it on paper or on your phone.

Q17. Another driver is behaving aggressively toward you. You should:

Do not engage. Let them go, change your route if they follow, and drive to a police station rather than home.

Q18. In Wisconsin, if you cannot see at least how far ahead, measured in seconds, you must slow down?

The Wisconsin handbook says that if you cannot see 10 to 15 seconds ahead, you must slow down so you have better control of your vehicle.

Q19. How does driving faster change what you can see?

The faster you drive, the narrower your field of vision becomes, so hazards at the sides of the road are harder to notice. Slow down where people or cars may come from the side.

Q20. Where should a child in a rear-facing car seat never ride?

An inflating front airbag can strike a rear-facing car seat and seriously injure or kill the child. A rear-facing seat belongs in the back seat.

Q21. In Wisconsin, you are in a crash and someone is injured or killed. What does the handbook say about the vehicles?

If someone is injured or killed, stay on the scene and do not move the vehicles. Call 911, and stay in the vehicle if it is safe to do so.

Q22. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q23. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q24. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q25. A bicyclist is riding at the right edge of your lane while an oncoming truck approaches. What is usually the best way to handle both?

When two hazards meet you at the same time, adjust your speed so you deal with one danger at a time. For example, slow down and let the truck pass before you pass the bicyclist.
