Q1. In Washington, how close may you park to a fire station driveway on the opposite side of the street?

Washington does not allow parking within 75 feet of a fire station driveway on the opposite side of the street.

Q2. In Washington, a minor with an alcohol, drug or firearm offense risks having the driver license delayed until what age?

The Washington guide says minors with alcohol, drug or firearm offenses run the risk of delaying their license until they are 17.

Q3. In Washington, how long do you have to respond to a traffic ticket (a notice of traffic infraction)?

Washington law (RCW 46.63.070) says to respond to a notice of traffic infraction within 30 days of the date of the notice. If you do not respond to a ticket for a moving violation, your driving privilege can be suspended. The guide still says 15 days.

Q4. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q5. Brown signs on a highway point to:

Brown is the color for recreational and cultural interest areas: parks, campgrounds, ski areas, museums and historic sites, for example. Blue, by contrast, marks motorist services.

Q6. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q7. On a highway, about how much longer does it take to pass a large truck than to pass a car?

A truck is much longer than a car, so passing it takes about three to five seconds more. Make sure you have that extra room before you start to pass.

Q8. In Washington, about how much distance does a loaded truck with properly adjusted brakes need to come to a complete stop from 55 mph?

The guide notes that a loaded truck traveling 55 mph needs roughly 450 feet to stop completely, much farther than a car, so drivers should never cut closely in front of one.

Q9. You are inside a roundabout when an ambulance with its siren on comes up behind you. What should you do?

Do not stop inside a roundabout for an emergency vehicle. Continue to your exit or the next one, leave the roundabout, and then pull over to the right and stop.

Q10. A pedestrian hybrid beacon over a mid-block crosswalk shows two steady red lights. What must you do?

A pedestrian hybrid beacon is dark until someone pushes the crossing button. Steady red means stop and stay stopped while pedestrians cross; only when it changes to flashing red may you go once your path is clear.

Q11. You should never stop your vehicle:

Never start across tracks unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side. Trains overhang the rails by about three feet on each side.

Q12. A small gasoline fire starts at your car. What should you never use on it?

Water spreads a gasoline fire instead of putting it out. Use a fire extinguisher only if the fire is small, and otherwise get everyone away and call 911.

Q13. The water covering a road ahead looks shallow. What is true about it?

Floodwater can hide dips and wash away the road surface beneath it, so you cannot judge safety by how shallow it looks. Turn around rather than try to cross.

Q14. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.

Q15. In Washington, may you park in an electric vehicle charging space without plugging in?

Washington makes it illegal to park in an electric vehicle charging station if the vehicle is not connected to the charger.

Q16. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q17. An open container of alcohol in the passenger area of the vehicle is:

Open container laws apply to the whole passenger compartment. Keep sealed alcohol in the trunk or a locked area.

Q18. A three-point turn should be used only:

The maneuver blocks the whole road for several seconds, so it needs a low-traffic street and a clear view both ways.

Q19. You stop at a red light far behind the stop line. Why can that be a problem at some signals?

Many signals use sensors in the pavement near the stop line to detect waiting cars. If you stop too far back, the signal may not know you are there.

Q20. In Washington, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q21. During a Washington driving skills exam, who may be in the vehicle?

In Washington, you and the examiner are the only people allowed in the vehicle during the skills exam. Service animals and interpreters are the exceptions.

Q22. In Washington, when is it a crime to leave an animal alone in a parked vehicle?

Washington makes it a crime to leave an animal unattended in a vehicle if it could be harmed or killed by heat, cold, lack of ventilation or lack of water.

Q23. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q24. In Washington, if you are enrolled in a driver training course, the youngest age at which you can get an instruction permit is:

With a driver training course you can get the permit at 15. Without one, the minimum age is 15 and a half. Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q25. In Washington, how must children ages 2 to 4 ride?

Washington requires children ages 2 to 4 to ride in a car seat with a rear- or forward-facing harness.
