Q1. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q2. A U-turn should never be made:

You need to be visible for the whole maneuver. Blind curves and crests hide you from traffic that arrives at speed.

Q3. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q4. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q5. Texting while driving is dangerous mainly because:

Five seconds looking at a phone at 55 mph covers the length of a football field blind. Every state restricts phone use for new drivers, and most restrict it for everyone.

Q6. You are in the left lane of a highway and a line of traffic builds up behind you. You should:

The left lane is for passing. Once traffic stacks up behind you, moving right is both safer and, in many states, required.

Q7. You arrive at an intersection where the traffic signal is completely dark because of a power failure. You should:

A dark signal is treated as an all-way stop. Stop, then take turns in the order vehicles arrived.

Q8. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q9. Unless a sign prohibits it, a right turn on red is allowed:

Right on red requires a full stop first, then yielding to everyone already entitled to go. A NO TURN ON RED sign removes the option entirely.

Q10. In Kentucky, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q11. In Kentucky, a court-ordered ignition interlock device prevents a vehicle from starting if the driver's breath-alcohol concentration exceeds:

Kentucky's handbook explains that an ignition interlock device prevents a driver from operating the vehicle if the driver's breath-alcohol concentration exceeds .02.

Q12. In Kentucky, a first DUI conviction within a ten-year period results in a license suspension of:

Kentucky law provides that a first DUI conviction within a ten-year period results in a license suspension of four to six months, in addition to fines and possible jail time.

Q13. In Kentucky, if you change your address or name, you must obtain a duplicate driver's license within:

Kentucky requires drivers who change their address or name to obtain a duplicate license within 10 days.

Q14. In Kentucky, if heavy rain, snow, or fog limits your visibility to no more than 100 feet, the handbook says you cannot safely drive faster than:

Kentucky's handbook states that when you cannot see farther than 100 feet ahead in heavy rain, snow, or fog, you cannot safely drive faster than 25 miles per hour.

Q15. A flashing red traffic light means:

Treat a flashing red exactly like a stop sign: full stop, yield to traffic and pedestrians, then proceed when the way is clear.

Q16. Your vehicle registration must be:

Expired registration is an easy stop for an officer and, in many states, grounds for towing.

Q17. A prescription medicine that makes you drowsy:

Impaired driving laws cover any drug, prescribed or not. Read the label and do not drive when it warns you about machinery.

Q18. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q19. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q20. In Kentucky, unless posted otherwise, the speed limit in an off-street parking facility open to the public is:

Kentucky sets the default speed limit in an off-street parking facility offered for public use at fifteen miles per hour.

Q21. In Kentucky, a driver under 21 is over the limit with:

The under-21 threshold is far below the 0.08% adult limit, so even a small amount of alcohol can reach it.

Q22. In Kentucky, it is unlawful to follow an emergency vehicle responding to a call any closer than:

Kentucky's handbook states that it is unlawful to follow any emergency vehicle closer than 500 feet.

Q23. In Kentucky, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q24. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q25. In Kentucky, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.
