Q1. In North Dakota, a new resident may operate a Class D vehicle on an out-of-state operator's license for:

A new North Dakota resident may drive a Class D vehicle on their out-of-state license for 60 days before they are required to obtain a North Dakota operator's license.

Q2. In North Dakota, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours. Night driving is where new drivers crash most, which is why a share of the total has to be after dark.

Q3. You arrive at an intersection where the traffic signal is completely dark because of a power failure. You should:

A dark signal is treated as an all-way stop. Stop, then take turns in the order vehicles arrived.

Q4. Children must ride in a child restraint or booster:

Requirements differ by state, and the back seat is safest for younger children. A belt that crosses the neck or belly means the child still needs a booster.

Q5. In North Dakota, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offence. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.

Q6. If your vehicle begins to hydroplane you should:

Sudden inputs make it worse. Take your foot off the gas, hold the wheel steady, and let the tires find the surface again.

Q7. Before changing lanes you should:

Mirrors do not cover the blind spot. A quick head check before moving is what catches the car that is already beside you.

Q8. In North Dakota, unless otherwise posted, the speed limit when passing a school during recess or during opening or closing hours is:

North Dakota sets a default 20 mph limit when passing a school during recess or while children are arriving or leaving, unless a different limit is posted.

Q9. In a work zone you should:

Rear-end collisions are the most common work-zone crash. More space is what prevents them.

Q10. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. Most states also require them in rain, fog, snow or whenever wipers are on.
