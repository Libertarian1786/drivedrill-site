Q1. Your hood flies open and blocks your view. You should:

Use the gap under the hood or the side window, signal, slow gradually and stop off the road.

Q2. In North Dakota, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offense. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.

Q3. An anti-lock braking system is designed to:

ABS preserves steering control. On gravel or deep snow it may even lengthen the stop, but you can still steer around the hazard.

Q4. Driving while your license is suspended usually results in:

It is one of the few traffic offenses that regularly carries jail, and it extends the original suspension.

Q5. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q6. In North Dakota, unless otherwise posted, the speed limit when passing a school during recess or during opening or closing hours is:

North Dakota sets a default 20 mph limit when passing a school during recess or while children are arriving or leaving, unless a different limit is posted.

Q7. At night, pedestrians are hardest to see:

Most pedestrian deaths happen after dark. Lower your speed so your stopping distance fits inside your headlight range.

Q8. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q9. You smell smoke and see flames under the hood. You should:

Do not open the hood; air feeds the fire. Get people at least 100 feet away and let the fire department handle it.

Q10. A funeral procession with its lights on is passing through an intersection. You should:

Do not cut into or interrupt a procession, even if you have a green light, unless directed by an officer.

Q11. In North Dakota, a new resident may operate a Class D vehicle on an out-of-state operator's license for:

A new North Dakota resident may drive a Class D vehicle on their out-of-state license for 60 days before they are required to obtain a North Dakota operator's license.

Q12. In North Dakota, if you have an open container of alcohol in your vehicle, the law requires that it:

North Dakota's open container law does not allow an open container of alcohol anywhere in the vehicle except the trunk.

Q13. After passing another vehicle, you should return to your lane when:

Seeing both headlights of the vehicle you passed in your mirror means you have enough room to move back over without cutting the driver off.

Q14. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q15. In North Dakota, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Q16. In North Dakota, the minimum age to apply for a learner permit is:

Applicants under 18 also need parental consent, and the permit must be held for a set period before the road test.

Q17. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q18. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump to build pressure, downshift for engine braking, apply the parking brake gradually, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q19. In North Dakota, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q20. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q21. In North Dakota, when a school bus is stopped and flashing its red lights, traffic must stop:

North Dakota requires drivers approaching from both directions to stop for a school bus that is stopped with its red lights flashing.

Q22. In North Dakota, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q23. In North Dakota, if an adult in the front seat is not wearing a seat belt:

This is a primary enforcement state: no other violation is needed. The belt is also the single most effective thing in the car for surviving a crash.

Q24. An exit number on a green sign tells you:

Exit numbers usually match the nearest mile marker, which makes it easy to judge how far away your exit still is.

Q25. For drivers 21 and older, it is illegal to drive with a blood alcohol concentration at or above:

0.08% is the per-se limit for adult drivers in most of the country. You can still be arrested below it if your driving is impaired.
