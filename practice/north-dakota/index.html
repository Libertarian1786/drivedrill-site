Q1. In North Dakota, a 15-year-old applying for a restricted license (before turning 16) must have at least how many hours of supervised driving?

North Dakota requires at least 50 hours of supervised practice, including night, gravel or dirt roads, rural and city driving, and winter conditions, for drivers licensed before age 16 (NDCC 39-06-17). Applicants who are 16 or 17 have no set hour requirement, but must hold the permit for six months.

Q2. In North Dakota, you may not park within how many feet of a fire hydrant?

North Dakota prohibits parking within 10 feet of a fire hydrant.

Q3. Your vehicle has four-wheel drive. How should you drive on an icy road?

Four-wheel drive does not make ice safe, because the vehicle can still skid and slide, so drive slowly and carefully. It can give a false sense of security.

Q4. A space cushion around your vehicle is useful because:

Space is time. The more of it you keep on all four sides, the more options you have when something goes wrong.

Q5. A small sign under a railroad crossbuck reads 2 TRACKS. What does it tell you?

When more than one track crosses the road, a sign under the crossbuck shows the number of tracks.

Q6. In North Dakota, you must switch to low beams when you are following another vehicle within:

North Dakota requires low beams when you follow another vehicle within 300 feet, so your lights do not blind the driver ahead through the mirrors.

Q7. At a railroad crossing, a small blue sign shows a phone number and a crossing ID number. When should you use it?

The blue Emergency Notification System (ENS) sign gives the railroad's emergency number and the crossing's ID. Call it if a vehicle is stalled on the tracks or the signals or gates are not working, so trains can be warned; call 911 too if anyone is in danger.

Q8. A large truck is backing up into a loading dock across your path. What should you do?

Truck drivers have a large blind spot behind the trailer and may not see you at all. Never cross behind a truck that is backing up; wait until it is done.

Q9. In North Dakota, you must never pass another vehicle within:

North Dakota prohibits passing within 100 feet of an intersection, a railroad crossing, or a tunnel.

Q10. At night, a vehicle with only one headlight is coming toward you. What should you do?

A single light could be a car with a burned-out headlight, so you cannot tell how wide it is. Drive as far to the right as you safely can until it passes.

Q11. Parking is prohibited:

Never park in a crosswalk, on a sidewalk, in an intersection, or where you would block a curb ramp.

Q12. Your brakes fail while driving. You should:

Pump the pedal to build pressure. If that does not work, apply the parking brake gradually and shift to lower gears for engine braking, and look for an open escape path or an uphill slope.

Q13. For driver licensing, North Dakota treats you as a resident after living in the state for:

A person who has lived in North Dakota for 90 consecutive days is considered a resident.

Q14. Your hood flies open and blocks your view. You should:

Use the gap under the hood or the side window, signal, slow gradually and stop off the road.

Q15. You turn left from a two-way street onto a street with two lanes going your way. Which lane should you turn into?

Finish a left turn in the left lane closest to the middle of the street, going your way. After the turn, you may signal and change lanes when it is safe.

Q16. Good drivers look:

Looking far ahead gives you time to spot brake lights, debris and merging traffic while you still have choices.

Q17. A person with a history of seizures can get a North Dakota license after being seizure-free for at least how long, with a medical exam?

North Dakota requires at least six consecutive months with no seizures, confirmed by a medical exam.

Q18. Strong, gusty winds are blowing across the highway. What should you do about cruise control?

In high winds, do not use cruise control. Keep your own foot on the accelerator so you can react right away when a gust hits.

Q19. In North Dakota, speeding in a work zone while work is in progress and workers are present carries a minimum fee of:

North Dakota law (NDCC 39-06.1-06) sets a $150 fee for going 1 to 10 mph over a work zone speed limit when workers are present and the sign says "Minimum Fee $150". The manual still says $80.

Q20. Under North Dakota's rule for new drivers, who may not use a phone or other electronic communication device while driving?

North Dakota bans all use of electronic communication devices while driving for anyone driving on an instruction permit, at any age, and for licensed drivers under 18.

Q21. In North Dakota, a new resident may operate a Class D vehicle on an out-of-state operator's license for:

A new North Dakota resident may drive a Class D vehicle on their out-of-state license for 60 days before they are required to obtain a North Dakota operator's license.

Q22. A person hurt in a crash is bleeding badly. What should you do until help arrives?

Stop serious bleeding by pressing thick cloth pads firmly on the wound, and keep up the pressure until help arrives.

Q23. You are on a narrow mountain road, and a sharp curve ahead blocks your view. What should you do?

Sound your horn as you approach a blind curve on a narrow road so oncoming drivers know you are there. Keep to the right and do not count on them to see or yield to you.

Q24. In North Dakota, the maximum speed limit on rural interstate highways is:

That is the highest limit the state allows on a rural interstate. The posted sign always governs, and a lower speed can still be required by conditions.

Q25. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams reach much farther than low beams, but at highway speed you can still outrun them, which is why night speeds should be lower.
