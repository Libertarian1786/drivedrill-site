Q1. In North Dakota, a new resident may operate a Class D vehicle on an out-of-state operator's license for:

A new North Dakota resident may drive a Class D vehicle on their out-of-state license for 60 days before they are required to obtain a North Dakota operator's license.

Q2. In North Dakota, an applicant under 18 must log how much supervised driving before the road test?

A parent or guardian certifies the hours.

Q3. Driving far below the speed limit when conditions are good:

Traffic that moves at very different speeds causes crashes. If you must go slow, stay right and let others pass.

Q4. You notice one headlight is out at night. You should:

From a distance one light reads as a motorcycle, and oncoming drivers misjudge how much room you take. Fix it before driving at night again.

Q5. In North Dakota, using a hand-held cell phone while driving is:

There is no statewide hands-free law, so holding a phone to talk is not itself an offense. Texting is banned for all drivers, and new drivers face tighter limits.

Q6. The road is most slippery:

Oil and rubber build up on dry pavement and float to the surface when rain starts. Slow down and double your following distance.

Q7. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q8. In North Dakota, unless otherwise posted, the speed limit when passing a school during recess or during opening or closing hours is:

North Dakota sets a default 20 mph limit when passing a school during recess or while children are arriving or leaving, unless a different limit is posted.

Q9. A red warning light appears on the dashboard while you are driving. You should:

Red generally means stop now: oil pressure, temperature, brakes or charging. Amber usually means service soon.

Q10. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.
