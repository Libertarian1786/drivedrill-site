Q1. Switching on your hazard lights while parked illegally:

Hazard lights warn other drivers; they do not create a parking permit.

Q2. A Georgia driver under 21 convicted of speeding 24 mph or more over the limit:

For drivers under 21 a single serious violation suspends the license, including reckless driving, racing and passing a school bus.

Q3. A Georgia driver under 18 has their license suspended for accumulating:

The adult threshold is much higher. For a driver under 18, a couple of tickets is enough.

Q4. Under Georgia's Hands-Free Act, a driver may not:

A vehicle stopped at a red light still counts as being in operation in Georgia, so the phone must stay out of your hand.

Q5. A flagger in a work zone holds up a sign reading SLOW. You should:

Flaggers have the same authority as signs and signals in a work zone. Follow their direction and watch for workers on foot.

Q6. The safest way to deal with a distracting situation inside the car is to:

Nothing inside the car is worth your attention at speed. Find a safe place to stop.

Q7. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q8. A conviction for driving under the influence usually results in:

Besides the suspension, fines and possible jail, a DUI stays on your driving record for years, and insurers usually raise premiums sharply.

Q9. Georgia requires headlights to be on:

Lights must also be on when visibility is poor enough that you cannot clearly see a person or vehicle 500 feet ahead.

Q10. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q11. After you move, you must usually:

States set a deadline, often 10 to 30 days. Renewal notices and suspension notices go to the address on file.

Q12. In Georgia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q13. Georgia's Super Speeder law adds a state fee for driving:

The Super Speeder fee is charged by the state on top of the local fine, and failing to pay it suspends your license.

Q14. Excessive speeding, well above the limit, is treated more seriously because:

Kinetic energy rises with the square of speed, so a small increase in speed is a large increase in damage and stopping distance.

Q15. Unless posted otherwise, the speed limit on a Georgia unpaved county road is:

Loose surfaces lengthen stopping distances and make skids more likely, which is why the unpaved limit is low.

Q16. In Georgia, driving while wearing headphones or a headset that impairs your hearing is:

You need to hear horns and sirens. Georgia law (O.C.G.A. 40-6-250) bans driving with headphones or a headset that impairs your hearing; a headset used for communication, such as a phone earpiece, is allowed.

Q17. You may not pass another vehicle within a certain distance of the crest of a hill because:

A blind crest hides an oncoming vehicle until it is far too late to abort the pass.

Q18. Overdriving your headlights means:

High beams let you see about 350 feet ahead, and low beams much less. At highway speed you can outrun that, which is why night speeds should be lower.

Q19. A Georgia crash must be reported when:

Stop at the scene, give aid, exchange information, and call police when the threshold is met. Leaving is a criminal offense.

Q20. Before opening your door into traffic after parking, you should:

Dooring a cyclist is a common and serious crash. Check the mirror and over your shoulder, and open with the far hand so your body turns toward traffic.

Q21. A flashing yellow traffic light means:

Flashing yellow means proceed with care. You do not have to stop, but you must slow down and watch for cross traffic and pedestrians.

Q22. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, commonly 10 to 15 feet. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q23. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q24. You are driving through a residential street where children are playing. You should:

Children act on impulse and are hidden by parked cars. Cover the brake and cut your speed well below the limit.

Q25. Backing up on a Georgia controlled-access roadway, such as an interstate, is:

If you miss an exit, continue to the next one. Traffic on a controlled-access road never expects a vehicle moving backward.
