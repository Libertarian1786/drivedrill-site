Q1. Driving without the insurance your state requires can result in:

Financial responsibility is a condition of driving. The penalties apply whether or not you have a crash.

Q2. A pedestrian using a white cane or a guide dog is crossing ahead. You must:

Stop well back and stay quiet. A horn or engine noise from an unexpected place can disorient someone who is navigating by sound.

Q3. Georgia requires headlights to be on:

Lights must also be on when visibility is poor enough that you cannot clearly see a person or vehicle 500 feet ahead.

Q4. In Georgia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q5. You are on a road that ends at a T intersection with no signs. You should:

At a T intersection the road that ends yields to the road that continues.

Q6. Backing up on a Georgia controlled-access roadway, such as an interstate, is:

If you miss an exit, continue to the next one. Traffic on a controlled-access road never expects a vehicle moving backward.

Q7. When you park facing uphill next to a curb, you should turn your front wheels:

Uphill with a curb: wheels away from the curb, then let the car settle back so the tire rests against it. Downhill: wheels toward the curb.

Q8. A front tire blows out at highway speed. You should:

Braking or sudden steering after a blowout can spin the car. Grip the wheel, come off the gas, then ease onto the shoulder once you are slow.

Q9. Georgia's Slowpoke Law requires a driver in the left lane to:

The law applies on roads with two or more lanes in your direction. It does not authorise the faster driver to speed.

Q10. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.
