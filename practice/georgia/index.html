Q1. The only thing that reduces blood alcohol concentration is:

The liver processes alcohol at a fixed rate. Nothing speeds it up, so the only plan that works is not driving.

Q2. You are on a divided highway with a median and a school bus stops on the other side. In most states you:

A physical median or barrier usually removes the requirement for traffic on the far side. A painted line is not a median, and rules vary, so learn your state's version.

Q3. Georgia requires headlights to be on:

Lights must also be on when visibility is poor enough that you cannot clearly see a person or vehicle 500 feet ahead.

Q4. In Georgia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q5. You should never park:

Hydrant clearance is set by state law, commonly 10 to 15 feet. Blocked hydrants delay firefighters and are towed.

Q6. Backing up on a Georgia controlled-access roadway, such as an interstate, is:

If you miss an exit, continue to the next one. Traffic on a controlled-access road never expects a vehicle moving backward.

Q7. White lines between lanes tell you that:

White separates lanes travelling the same way. Broken white lines may be crossed to change lanes; solid white lines discourage or prohibit it.

Q8. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q9. Georgia's Slowpoke Law requires a driver in the left lane to:

The law applies on roads with two or more lanes in your direction. It does not authorise the faster driver to speed.

Q10. Using cruise control on a wet road is discouraged because:

If a tire starts to hydroplane, cruise control may hold or add throttle. Drive with your own foot when the road is wet.
