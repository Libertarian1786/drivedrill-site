Q1. You are entering a Georgia highway, and solid white lines (a gore area) separate your acceleration lane from traffic. You must:

Drivers may not cross the solid white gore lines; they must keep traveling in the acceleration lane until the solid white line disappears.

Q2. After you move to a new address in Georgia, you must update your license within:

The Georgia manual says you must change your address with DDS within 60 days of moving.

Q3. You are waiting to turn left, and an oncoming car has its right turn signal on. When should you start your turn in front of it?

A driver may have left a signal on by mistake or may change their mind. Do not assume the car will turn; wait until it actually starts its turn before you turn left.

Q4. Which Georgia roads may bicyclists legally use?

Bicyclists are entitled to use every road in Georgia except the interstate and interstate-like (limited-access) highways.

Q5. In Georgia, you may not park within how many feet of a crosswalk at an intersection?

Georgia allows only a momentary stop within 20 feet of a crosswalk at an intersection.

Q6. Fluorescent yellow-green warning signs usually warn you about:

Fluorescent yellow-green is used for warning signs about schools, school buses, pedestrians and bicycles, so you notice where these people may be.

Q7. In Georgia, the Class D license is:

Georgia's Class D is a provisional license issued to drivers ages 16 and 17.

Q8. You are starting your car from a stop on a snow-covered road. What should you do?

Start slowly and accelerate smoothly on snow, because starting too fast makes the drive wheels spin. Spinning tires lose even more grip.

Q9. You stop behind another vehicle in traffic. How much room should you leave?

When you stop behind a vehicle, leave enough room to see its rear tires on the pavement. That gives you space to go around it if it stalls.

Q10. Under Georgia's Hands-Free Act, a driver may not:

A vehicle stopped at a red light still counts as being in operation in Georgia, so the phone must stay out of your hand.

Q11. A bicycle on a public road is:

Riders must obey signals and signs, and drivers must yield to them the same way they would to another vehicle.

Q12. A strong crosswind is pushing your vehicle. You should:

Wind gusts are strongest in open country and beside large vehicles. Slow down, hold the wheel firmly, and expect a push when a truck passes.

Q13. On Georgia highways, where does milepost zero start?

Mileposts count up from zero, which always starts at the south or west border where a route begins.

Q14. In Georgia, how far before your left turn may you enter a shared center turn lane?

Georgia lets you move into the center turn lane no more than 300 feet before the place where you will turn left.

Q15. You should switch on your headlights:

Headlights are about being seen as much as seeing. The law often also requires them in rain, fog or snow, or whenever the wipers are on.

Q16. You should check your mirrors:

Knowing what is behind you is what turns a sudden stop into a controlled one.

Q17. In Georgia, a driver under 21 is over the limit at a blood alcohol level of:

At 0.02% or higher, an under-21 driver faces a minimum six-month license suspension.

Q18. A signal at the end of a freeway on-ramp lets cars onto the freeway one at a time, switching between red and green. What must you do?

A ramp meter controls how quickly cars join the freeway. Stop at the line while it is red and go when it turns green.

Q19. You are about to turn at a corner, but you realize you are not in the proper lane for the turn. What should you do?

Do not turn from the wrong lane or cut across other traffic at the last moment. Drive on to the next intersection, or around the block, and make the turn from the proper lane.

Q20. For Georgia's Class D passenger limits, who counts as an "immediate family member"?

Georgia defines immediate family as the driver's parents and step-parents, grandparents, siblings and step-siblings, children, and any other person who lives at the driver's residence.

Q21. Your car has lane-keeping and automatic braking features. What should you remember in heavy rain or fog?

Driver-assist features such as lane keeping and automatic braking may not work properly in rain, fog and other poor conditions. You are still responsible for watching the road and controlling the car.

Q22. You are at a flashing red light. What should you remember about the traffic on the cross street?

Traffic on the cross street often has a flashing yellow light and may not have to stop at all. Do not pull out until there is a safe gap.

Q23. About how long does it take your body to get rid of the alcohol in one standard drink?

Your body removes alcohol at about one drink per hour, and nothing speeds that up. Coffee, food or a cold shower will not make you sober faster.

Q24. At a two-lane roundabout with no signs showing otherwise, you plan to take the first exit to the right. Which lane should you use?

To turn right at a multi-lane roundabout, enter in the right lane and exit from the right lane. Signs and lane arrows before the roundabout show which lane goes where.

Q25. Fog becomes so thick that you cannot see well enough to drive safely. What is the safest choice?

When fog is too thick to see the road, pull well off the road and wait for it to lift. Stopping or creeping in a travel lane risks being hit by drivers who cannot see you.
