Q1. The accelerator sticks open. You should:

Neutral disconnects the engine from the wheels so you keep power steering and brakes. Once stopped, shut the engine off.

Q2. You must:

Driving without carrying the license is a separate violation in most states, even if you are licensed.

Q3. Georgia requires headlights to be on:

Lights must also be on when visibility is poor enough that you cannot clearly see a person or vehicle 500 feet ahead.

Q4. In Georgia, the law requires a car seat or booster seat until:

State law is a minimum. Safety experts recommend a booster until the adult belt fits properly: lap belt low across the hips and shoulder belt across the chest, not the neck.

Q5. Liability insurance pays for:

Liability is the coverage states require. Collision and comprehensive, which cover your own vehicle, are optional.

Q6. Backing up on a Georgia controlled-access roadway, such as an interstate, is:

If you miss an exit, continue to the next one. Traffic on a controlled-access road never expects a vehicle moving backward.

Q7. The first ability alcohol affects in a driver is:

Judgment goes first, which is why an impaired driver often believes they are fine. Reaction time, vision and coordination follow.

Q8. Driving on the shoulder to get around slow traffic is:

The shoulder is for emergencies and for stopped vehicles. Using it as a lane is both illegal and a common cause of crashes with people standing outside their cars.

Q9. Georgia's Super Speeder law adds a state fee for driving:

The Super Speeder fee is charged by the state on top of the local fine, and failing to pay it suspends your license.

Q10. After a crash with injury or significant damage you must usually:

Every state sets a damage threshold and a deadline for reporting. Exchange information at the scene and file the report on time.
