Q1. On an Oklahoma road divided into four or more lanes, a vehicle may not:

On a roadway of four or more lanes, a vehicle cannot impede the normal flow of traffic by driving in the left lane. Use it to pass, then move back right.

Q2. You see a large puddle across your lane on a wet road. What is the best way to handle it?

Slow down and go around large puddles when it is safe to do so. Standing water can cause hydroplaning, can hide a pothole, and can make your brakes work poorly.

Q3. Compared with daylight, how does darkness change when you can see an object on the road?

At night you must be closer to an object than in daylight before you can see it. Slow down so you still have time to react when you do see it.

Q4. In Oklahoma, an uninsured driver in a crash will have the license suspended when the crash causes any injury or property damage of at least:

If you have no insurance and are in a crash with any injury or $500 or more in property damage, your license will be suspended.

Q5. Under Oklahoma law, you must stop and not proceed at a railroad crossing when a train approaching within about:

Oklahoma law spells out several conditions that require a stop at a railroad crossing, including an approaching train close enough to be an immediate hazard.

Q6. Before you turn or change lanes, you should signal:

Signal early and continuously. A signal given as you are already turning tells other drivers nothing they can use.

Q7. On Oklahoma's point schedule, failing to stop or stay stopped for a school bus that is loading or unloading adds:

Failing to stop or remain stopped for a loading or unloading school bus adds 4 points to your Oklahoma record.

Q8. Why is it easy to misjudge an oncoming motorcycle?

Because a motorcycle is small, it can look farther away than it is. Assume it is closer than it looks before you turn or pull out in front of it.

Q9. You have just driven through deep water, and your brakes feel weak. How can you dry them?

After driving through water, test your brakes. If they are wet, drive slowly in a low gear and press the brake pedal lightly until they work normally again.

Q10. In Oklahoma, deer-vehicle crashes happen most often from:

Deer-vehicle crashes most often occur from October to December, so be extra alert in the fall.

Q11. When backing straight, the Oklahoma manual says to hold the steering wheel:

To back up, grasp the wheel at the 12 o'clock position with your left hand, put your right arm on the back of the seat, and look through the rear window.

Q12. Combining alcohol with another drug usually:

Interactions are unpredictable and often far stronger than the sum of the parts.

Q13. Where should a child in a rear-facing car seat never ride?

An inflating front airbag can strike a rear-facing car seat and seriously injure or kill the child. A rear-facing seat belongs in the back seat.

Q14. Can you be convicted of driving under the influence if no breath or blood test was done?

You can be arrested and convicted of DUI without a BAC reading. Officers and courts can rely on other evidence, such as how you drove and how you did on field sobriety tests.

Q15. You stop at a red light far behind the stop line. Why can that be a problem at some signals?

Many signals use sensors in the pavement near the stop line to detect waiting cars. If you stop too far back, the signal may not know you are there.

Q16. Your car lacks anti-lock brakes, and you must stop on a slippery road. How should you use the brakes?

Without anti-lock brakes, brake gently on a slippery road and ease up at the first hint the wheels are slipping. Wheels that stop rolling slide and cannot steer.

Q17. Which habit helps keep carbon monoxide from leaking into your car?

Have the exhaust system checked regularly so a leak does not let carbon monoxide into the car. Do not drive with all the windows shut tight, and never warm up the engine in a closed garage.

Q18. Is it safe to leave a child or pet in a parked car on a warm day if a window is cracked open?

A parked car heats up fast, even with a window slightly open, and children and pets can die from the heat. Never leave them alone in a parked car.

Q19. A driver is following you too closely. The safest response is to:

Increase your own space cushion and give them a chance to go by. Brake-checking turns their mistake into your crash.

Q20. In Oklahoma, unless otherwise posted, the speed limit on county roads is:

County roads in Oklahoma have a 55 mph limit unless otherwise posted.

Q21. Before you pass in Oklahoma, the manual says to check ahead for at least how much clear road without a No Passing Zone?

Check ahead for at least 200 feet of clear roadway with no No Passing Zone before you start to pass.

Q22. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.

Q23. In Oklahoma, a teen driver convicted of a moving violation while holding a learner or intermediate license:

Oklahoma's Graduated Driver License program holds a teen back at their current level for a set period after a moving-violation conviction before they can advance.

Q24. You are stranded in a snowstorm and run the engine for heat. What should you check to avoid carbon monoxide poisoning?

Keep the tailpipe clear of snow whenever the engine is running, because a blocked pipe can send carbon monoxide into the car. Run the engine only for short periods, with a window open slightly.

Q25. For Oklahoma's drive test, your tires must have a minimum tread depth of:

Worn tread is checked as part of the pre-test vehicle inspection because shallow tread reduces grip, especially on wet roads.
