Q1. You must never pass another vehicle when:

You need to see far enough ahead to complete the pass and return safely. Hills, curves, intersections and railroad crossings take that view away.

Q2. According to the Oklahoma Driver Manual, most vehicles will become buoyant and float away in:

The manual warns that it takes surprisingly little water to float a car, which is why you should never try to drive across a flooded road.

Q3. In Oklahoma, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practice across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a license in a weekend.

Q4. In Oklahoma, points already on your driving record are reduced all the way to zero if you have:

Oklahoma's point system gradually forgives points for clean driving, and a full reset to zero happens after three consecutive years without a pointable conviction.

Q5. Worn tire tread is dangerous mainly because it:

Tread channels water away. Once it is shallow, wet grip falls off sharply. Check with a tread gauge or the built-in wear bars.

Q6. A flagger in a work zone holds up a sign reading SLOW. You should:

Flaggers have the same authority as signs and signals in a work zone. Follow their direction and watch for workers on foot.

Q7. Before backing out of a parking space you should:

Cameras miss low objects and fast-moving children. Look behind the vehicle before you get in, then back slowly while checking all around.

Q8. On a long downgrade you should:

Continuous braking overheats the brakes and they fade. Let the engine hold the vehicle back and brake in short firm applications.

Q9. Before driving on a frosty morning you should:

Driving with a peephole is a citation in most states, and snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield or the car behind you.

Q10. In Oklahoma, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.
