Q1. A motorcycle is entitled to:

Never share a lane with a motorcycle. Riders move within the lane to avoid road hazards and to stay visible.

Q2. In many states, whenever your windshield wipers are on continuously you must also:

Wipers-on means lights-on in most states. It is about being seen by other drivers as much as your own vision.

Q3. In Oklahoma, a learner permit must be held for at least how long before the road test?

The holding period exists so new drivers practise across seasons and conditions rather than cramming a licence in a weekend.

Q4. In Oklahoma, points already on your driving record are reduced all the way to zero if you have:

Oklahoma's point system gradually forgives points for clean driving, and a full reset to zero happens after three consecutive years without a pointable conviction.

Q5. Black ice is most likely to form:

Bridges and shaded stretches lose heat fastest. If the road looks wet but spray has stopped coming off other cars, suspect ice.

Q6. Driving into low sun glare, the best response is to:

Glare hides brake lights and pedestrians. Slow down, clean the inside of your windshield, and leave more room.

Q7. The light is green but traffic ahead is backed up past the intersection. You should:

Stopping inside an intersection blocks cross traffic and emergency vehicles, and it is a citation in most states.

Q8. According to the Oklahoma Driver Manual, most vehicles will become buoyant and float away in:

The manual warns that it takes surprisingly little water to float a car, which is why you should never try to drive across a flooded road.

Q9. In Oklahoma, the maximum posted speed limit on urban interstate highways is:

Urban interstates are held to a lower maximum than open rural highway, because of interchange spacing and traffic volume.

Q10. A crash with the vehicle ahead is unavoidable. The safest choice is usually to:

Reduce speed as much as possible and choose a glancing impact with something soft over a head-on with something solid.
